# Battle presentation in state-of-the-art strategy and tactics games (2019–2026): visual and animation craft

Reading notes for the report writer:
- Research date: November 2026. Web search and page fetch only.
- **[fetched]** = I read the page. **[snippet]** = the claim comes from a search-result summary of that URL; the page itself was not read (paywall, truncation, 403/404/429, or budget). Treat [snippet] items as lower confidence.
- Release years in parentheses are general knowledge unless a link sits next to them. Games before 2019 are cited only as the origin of a technique still in use.
- Target context for the "browser" notes: React + Tailwind DOM grid (8×8) with a 2D-canvas effects layer, a staged "war scene", and a three.js mounted duel; AI-painted 2D art, procedural geometry and shaders, no animators. All browser-approximation notes are my inferences, not sourced claims, and live under "Inferences".

## 1. Grid readability: tiles, ranges, threat zones, height, facing, selection, path previews

### Takeaway
The best-regarded boards show *consequences*, not just ranges: every enemy's next action is drawn on the tiles before the player commits (Into the Breach), threat is layered onto the movement range with a second colour plus enemy-to-target lines (Fire Emblem, Triangle Strategy), and turn order is shown as a timeline that updates on hover (FFT: The Ivalice Chronicles). The repeated failure mode is colour-only encoding and indicator overload.

### Cited Findings

**Into the Breach (2018; the reference point every later tactics UI is measured against)**
- Design rule from co-designer Justin Ma: "we would sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time." Weapons and firing patterns that could not be explained to playtesters were cut. [fetched] — [Game Developer, 5 Mar 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-)
- The first internal build showed "all these damage indicators all over the map"; it only read clearly to the developers, who already knew each enemy's role. External playtests forced a clean-up. [fetched] — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-)
- Their "most important UI choice" was *animated tooltips*: a small looping animation of tiles being damaged and units being pushed, which Ma called "a thousand times more effective" than three sentences of text. [fetched] — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-)
- "Every action the enemy will make in their next attack phase is clearly telegraphed through the UI during your turn." [snippet] — [GameSpot review](https://gamespot.com/reviews/into-the-breach-review-a-mechanized-masterpiece/1900-6416865/)
- A dedicated Attack Order control shows the sequence in which enemy attacks will resolve; health is drawn as discrete green squares under each unit silhouette; Undo Move is unlimited and Reset Turn is available once per battle. [fetched] — [Gamepressure interface guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/into-the-breach/interface-battle-map/zcaa73)
- Holding Alt overlays the enemy attack order on the board so the player can make enemies hit each other. [snippet] — [Gamepressure combat guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/into-the-breach/combat/z1aa78)
- Subset Games' GDC 2019 design postmortem (Matthew Davis) is the primary talk; the 80.lv page is only an announcement and has no UI detail. [fetched] — [80.lv, 9 Jan 2019](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-2019-an-inside-look-at-into-the-breach); [GDC Vault entry](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026333/)

**Fire Emblem: Three Houses (2019) and Engage (2023)**
- Three Houses: blue tiles = reachable, red tiles = "Danger Area" reachable by enemies. [snippet] — [Samurai Gamers battle guide](https://samurai-gamers.com/fire-emblem-three-houses/battle-system-guide/)
- Three Houses: highlighting an enemy shows its move + attack range; one button (ZR) toggles the combined danger radius of all enemies. [snippet] — [SuperCheats walkthrough](https://www.supercheats.com/fire-emblem-three-houses/walkthrough/danger-radius)
- Engage keeps the Three Houses "aggro line" device: when you pick up a unit, red lines flow from each enemy that would attack it to the unit, updating as you move the cursor. [snippet] — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/how-to-see-enemy-targeting-in-fire-emblem-engage)
- Engage also allows incoming enemy damage to be made viewable on the map via a setting. [snippet] — [The Escapist](https://escapistmagazine.com/?p=133978)

**Triangle Strategy (2022, HD-2D)**
- Tile colour vocabulary: blue = movable and safe; purple = movable but inside at least one enemy's attack range; red = your attack range; yellow = area of effect of the selected command; red lines = which enemies can attack the unit at that tile. [snippet] — [Game8 guide](https://game8.co/games/Triangle-Strategy/archives/368789)
- A colour-blind reviewer found blue vs purple movement tiles "indiscernible", the red threat lines "difficult to see at a glance" (but "better than nothing"), and asked for a symbol, animation or different outline plus configurable critical colours. [snippet] — [RPGFan feature](https://www.rpgfan.com/feature/project-triangle-strategys-invisible-feature-and-the-trials-of-gaming-while-colorblind/)
- Height matters (higher ground increases damage), and the 360-degree rotatable map was a major production cost: "making maps observable from every side took a lot of resources", with long deliberation on how to finish map edges. [snippet for height: [Nintendo tips](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/tips-for-getting-started-with-triangle-strategy/)]; [fetched for rotation: [Nintendo Everything, 28 May 2022](https://nintendoeverything.com/triangle-strategy-devs-on-how-the-game-uses-accurate-hd-2d)]

**Final Fantasy Tactics: The Ivalice Chronicles (30 Sep 2025)**
- Adds an overhead tactical view, a full turn-order list on the left side "pretty much exactly like Final Fantasy 10", and a UI described as making combat "drastically more readable and informative". [snippet] — [Inverse preview](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/final-fantasy-tactics-preview-inverse-rpg); [Bleacher Report review](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25252362-final-fantasy-tactics-ivalice-chronicles-review-gameplay-impressions-videos)
- Hovering an action previews where it will place the unit in the turn order; reviewers call the full turn order including pending spells "one of the best changes". [snippet] — [RPG Site preview](https://www.rpgsite.net/preview/18317-final-fantasy-tactics-the-ivalice-chronicles-new-voice-acting-makes-it-feel-like-a-whole-new-game); [Sportskeeda review](https://widgets.sportskeeda.com/esports/final-fantasy-tactics-ivalice-chronicles-review)
- The move to HD "necessitated a UI overhaul". [snippet] — [Press Start preview, 3 Sep 2025](https://press-start.com.au/previews/2025/09/03/final-fantasy-tactics-the-ivalice-chronicles-hands-on-preview-tactical-nostalgia/)

**Other board-readability data points**
- Tactics Ogre: Reborn (2022): the combat UI was overhauled "to make it clear to see what your options are", but high-resolution UI sits awkwardly on low-resolution, filtered sprites. [snippet] — [Sportskeeda preview](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/tactics-ogre-reborn-ps5-preview-more-simple-visual-overhaul); [DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=167341)
- Battle Brothers (2017): units stand on "sockets" (bases) so they pop from the terrain without being made large or bright, and the socket also shows the unit's height level. [fetched] — [Battle Brothers Dev Blog #5, 20 Feb 2014](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=414)
- Wartales (2023): a reviewer complained that hovering an enemy does not automatically show its attack and movement range. [snippet] — [Turn Based Lovers review](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/wartales-review-in-details/)
- Age of Wonders 4 (2023): readability was a stated art goal after Planetfall's "muddy" look; reviewers note big readable unit-type icons above units. [snippet] — [Paradox dev diary #11](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/dev-diary-11-the-art-of-age-of-wonders-4.1576391/post-28854759); [xxlgamer review](https://xxlgamer.com/reviews/exit/388)
- Heroes of Might & Magic: Olden Era (Early Access 2026): Unfrozen lists "Visual clarity is essential — everything must be easy to read" as an art pillar; a preview nevertheless reports that many players find the adventure map less readable than HoMM3 (hard to tell which objects are interactive). [fetched] — [GameSpace, 27 Apr 2026](https://www.gamespace.com/all-articles/news/heroes-of-might-and-magic-olden-era-tackles-the-artstyle/); [snippet] — [VGTimes preview](https://vgtimes.com/video-game-previews/156390-heroes-of-might-and-magic-olden-era-preview.html)
- Baldur's Gate 3 (2023): initiative order is a portrait strip at the top of the screen read left to right; adjacent allies share a slot and can act interchangeably. [snippet] — [GamesRadar combat guide](https://gamesradar.com/baldurs-gate-3-combat); [bg3.wiki](https://bg3.wiki/wiki/Gameplay_mechanics)

### Inferences
- A consistent four-colour tile vocabulary has emerged across Fire Emblem and Triangle Strategy: *move-safe / move-threatened / attack / area-of-effect*, plus a line overlay from threatening enemies to the hovered tile. The "purple = movable but dangerous" blend is the single most transferable idea for an 8×8 board, provided it is doubled with a non-colour cue (hatch pattern, pulsing outline, small skull glyph), which is exactly what the colour-blind critique asks for.
- Into the Breach's approach (draw every enemy's committed action on the tiles, number the resolution order, show push/damage results as a ghost preview on hover) fits an 8×8 board unusually well because the whole board is always on screen.
- Turn-order timelines that react to hover (FFT:IC) are cheap to build and were singled out as the best change in a 2025 remaster; this is pure UI work.
- **Browser approximation (DOM + canvas).** Tile states as CSS classes on the DOM grid with a second SVG/canvas overlay for (a) threat lines as animated dashed bezier curves (`stroke-dashoffset` animation), (b) a path preview as a rounded polyline with an arrowhead and per-step footprints, (c) attack telegraphs as inset tile borders plus a directional chevron. Hover preview: ghost the unit at the destination at 50% opacity and show the predicted HP delta as a notched segment on the target's bar. Height can be shown by a 2–6 px CSS `translateY` plus a darker "cliff" strip under raised tiles and a soft drop shadow; facing by a small wedge on the token base. None of this needs animators.
- Animated "how this ability works" tooltips can be faked with a 3×3 mini-grid in the tooltip running a CSS keyframe loop — no art required.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Into the Breach GDC talk itself or the Game UI Database entries; exact tile art (the red corner brackets, directional arrows, numbered order badges, spawn markers) is described here only via guides, not a primary design source. The widely repeated fact that Into the Breach uses an 8×8 grid that always fits on one screen is background knowledge, not verified against a fetched source in this pass.
- No primary source found for how Triangle Strategy, Fire Emblem or FFT:IC draw *height* and *facing* on tiles; nothing found on Baldur's Gate 3's surface/AoE preview or path-line design beyond the turn-order strip (searches returned mostly rules explanations and user complaints).
- Paradox's AoW4 art dev diary returned HTTP 500; its specifics on silhouette/saturation rules are unverified.
- A search summary (outlet not pinned down) said Subset also capped how many status effects one weapon may apply to keep the UI clear, and that Gigazine reports a "Three Reads" framing; neither was verified.

## 2. Unit visual identity on the board: silhouettes, team colour, class recognition, idle, health/status, banners, shrinking formations

### Takeaway
Top games treat the unit token as an information display first and a character second: a unique silhouette feature, exaggerated weapon shapes, a base/plinth for team colour and height, discrete health pips or a "lazy" health bar, and a banner whose colour/flash state carries morale. Low-budget games succeed by composing layered busts or billboards rather than animating full bodies.

### Cited Findings
- **Silhouette rules (Riot, applies to TFT's shared art pipeline).** Each character needs "a unique defining primary feature"; the silhouette should hint at the source of power (bow = ranged, staff = caster); size signals role (large and armoured = tank); facing must be readable from silhouette alone; animation is only a secondary differentiator. Three stated goals: gameplay clearly conveyed, hierarchy preserved (most important thing gets most attention), noise minimal. [fetched] — [Riot "Clarity in League", 12 Mar 2021](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)
- **Teamfight Tactics (2019–; K.O. Coliseum set, 2025).** Riot did a full style pass on every champion with new shaders and *outlines*, iterating so it "didn't interfere with gameplay clarity"; the outline tech is described as reusable for future sets. [fetched] — [TFT dev: K.O. Coliseum Learnings, 27 Oct 2025](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-tft-ko-coliseum-learnings)
- **Battle Brothers (2017).** A three-person team (one artist) abandoned full-body Flash figures because every pose would need redrawing for every armour type. Solution: busts on sockets ("figurine like", inspired by Unity of Command), built from swappable layers (head, helmet, armour, weapon). Goal: "having all equipment be clearly visible on any character." Weapon icons are deliberately stubby/exaggerated because real proportions are too thin to read when zoomed out. Every armour and helmet has a *damaged variant* and characters show visible injuries. Walking and turning animations were cut. Faces must stay readable at max zoom-out; the slightly cute caricature style serves small-scale readability. [fetched] — [Battle Brothers Dev Blog #5](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=414)
- PC Gamer called the legless busts an indie workaround that avoids luxuries like idle animation. [snippet] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/battle-brothers-is-turn-based-mount-blade-meets-xcom)
- **Songs of Conquest (EA 2022 / 1.0 2024).** World is real 3D (ground, cliffs) with 2D pixel billboards (trees, buildings, characters); "tens of thousands of billboards" on screen, most animated; one texture atlas per faction to limit draw calls; atlas animations are 8-frame horizontal strips scrolled in the shader (drawback: everything shares one frame rate). Pixel art made in Aseprite, tiles in PyxelEdit, engine Unity. [fetched] — [80.lv interview with Lavapotion, 6 Sep 2019](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Some players find Songs of Conquest's chunky pixel art hard to read; the style cannot be switched off. [snippet] — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/songs-of-conquest/can-the-pixel-art-style-be-turned-off/za1142b)
- **Fire Emblem: Three Houses (2019) battalions.** Units lead squads; on the plain grid they are single figures, but in the zoomed combat view the character fights with their battalion's soldiers around them, which reviewers say makes the game look far more impressive than the map suggests. Battalions can also perform "Gambits" as a separate attack. [snippet] — [Twinfinite, Jul 2019](https://twinfinite.net/2019/07/7-improvements-that-fire-emblem-three-houses-has-over-its-previous-games/); [RPGamer preview](https://rpgamer.com/2019/05/fire-emblem-three-houses-preview-covers-more-characters-crests-abilities-battalions/)
- **Symphony of War (2022).** Each map token is a squad of up to nine units that resolve combat automatically in a separate scene. [snippet] — [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_of_War:_The_Nephilim_Saga)
- **Total War health and morale read-outs.** Warhammer III (2022) added "lazy" health bars that highlight the new value and then drain to it, so burst damage is visible, with tick marks at 75/50/25%. [snippet] — [totalwar.com QoL post](https://www.totalwar.com/?p=16273)
- Morale lives on the unit banner: a coloured bar at the top of the banner turns yellow under morale penalty and red when wavering (Rome II manual, origin of the convention); in Three Kingdoms (2019) the unit icon flashes when wavering and a white flag replaces the weapon symbol when routing; a Warhammer wiki describes the banner flashing then turning white. [snippet] — [Total War: Rome II manual](https://r2enc.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0087_enc_page_battle_play_phase_conflict_morale); [Total War wiki, 3K battle](https://totalwar.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_(Total_War:_Three_Kingdoms)); [Warhammer wiki "Break"](https://totalwarwarhammer.gamepedia.com/Break)
- **Unicorn Overlord (2024).** Director Takafumi Noma: characters were drawn larger "to make the battles feel more powerful and realistic." Reviewers call class designs distinct and memorable. [fetched] — [Inverse interview, 24 Mar 2024](https://nc.inverse.com/gaming/unicorn-overlord-interview-vanillaware); [RPGFan review, 8 Mar 2024](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/unicorn-overlord)
- **Into the Breach.** Health as discrete squares rather than a number or bar. [fetched] — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/into-the-breach/interface-battle-map/zcaa73)
- **Olden Era (EA 2026).** Each faction should own a leading colour palette, but the palette "should not control every visual decision"; "stylisation ... prolongs the life of the art style." [fetched] — [GameSpace](https://www.gamespace.com/all-articles/news/heroes-of-might-and-magic-olden-era-tackles-the-artstyle/)
- **Total War: Three Kingdoms (2019).** Art lead Pawel Wojs: "And color, lots of color"; UI built from flowing black ink on paper rather than skeuomorphic wooden panels, with painted character illustrations layered on top. [fetched] — [GamesBeat, 16 Aug 2018](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)

### Inferences
- The Battle Brothers recipe (layered bust + plinth + exaggerated weapon icon + damaged-armour variants, no walk cycle) is the most directly reusable identity system for a no-animator, AI-painted project: an AI-painted general portrait bust on a faction-coloured base, with a troop-type glyph (spear / horse / bow / siege) as an oversized badge, reads at 8×8 scale and needs only slide/tween movement.
- "Troop counts shrinking" has three proven visual encodings: (1) discrete pips (Into the Breach), (2) a lazy/ghost-drain bar with threshold ticks (Warhammer III), (3) literal soldier figures around the leader that thin out (Three Houses battalions, Symphony of War squads). For a Three Kingdoms unit token, (3) can be faked in DOM/canvas by drawing N tiny soldier sprites in a formation block behind the general's bust and removing them in proportion to losses; combine with (2) on the bar.
- Banner-as-morale (flash = wavering, white flag = routing) is a culturally apt device for a Three Kingdoms game: a small CSS-animated banner on each token can carry faction colour, morale state and status icons without adding HUD chrome.
- **Browser approximation.** Idle life without animators: 2–3% scale "breathing" on the bust (`transform: scaleY` keyframes, phase-offset per unit), banner flutter via a CSS `skewX` loop or a 4-vertex canvas sine warp, subtle rim outline (`drop-shadow` filter in team colour) to mimic TFT's outline pass. Selection: ring under the base plus a 4 px lift. Damage variants: a second AI-painted "battered" bust swapped at <50% strength (Battle Brothers idea).
- **Needs professional animators:** Vanillaware-quality multi-part sprite animation and Fire Emblem-style per-class 3D combat sets. Not needed for good board identity.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for how Songs of Conquest visualises stack size on battlefield sprites (searches only returned stack-cap rules).
- No developer source located on Fire Emblem Engage's map-sprite design or on Unicorn Overlord's overworld unit tokens.
- The classic Advance Wars convention (number of vehicles/soldiers shown in the battle cut-in reflecting unit HP) is background knowledge; not confirmed in any source I retrieved.
- Vanillaware's exact 2D animation method (painted parts deformed/tweened vs per-frame drawing) was not confirmed; an old Kamitani interview on Game Developer raises the question ("a bit Flash-like at times") but the answer was not retrieved — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/king-of-2d-vanillaware-s-george-kamitani) [snippet].

## 3. Attack presentation: transitions, cut-ins, camera, anticipation / impact / follow-through, hit-stop, shake, numbers, crits, kill cams, tolerable length

### Takeaway
Critics praise attack presentation when (a) the cut to the combat view feels continuous with the map location, (b) hits have weight (anticipation, a short freeze, shake scaled to damage, loud bassy sound), and (c) animation expresses character. The same critics and players abandon it quickly when it is slow or unskippable — the evidence points to pauses of ~1–3 seconds between actions being read as wasted time.

### Cited Findings

**Map-to-combat transitions**
- Fire Emblem Engage (2023): "it never gets old to see the top-down view of the battlefield seamlessly transition to a 3D brawl between the two chosen combatants"; the camera "zooms in dramatically"; blows are "weighty"; Emblem signature moves play as "impressively impactful cutscenes". [fetched] — [Gfinity review, 17 Jan 2023](https://www.gfinityesports.com/reviews/fire-emblem-engage/)
- Engage's zoomed-in combat camera is "fully rooted in where on the map you're fighting" (the backdrop is the actual map location). [snippet] — [Pocket-lint](https://www.pocket-lint.com/games/reviews/nintendo/163968-fire-emblem-engage-review)
- Engage: "gorgeous attack animations and delicious sound design" are the norm; over-the-top animations "help define each character and make critical hits more satisfying". Dissent: one reviewer found them not "as snappy or frenetic" as earlier entries. [snippet] — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/reviews/fire-emblem-engage-review-breathes-life-into-old-combat); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/fire-emblem-engage-hands-on-preview); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1195513/fire-emblem-engage-review-engaging-where-it-counts)
- Engage used only 3D models (earlier entries sometimes fell back to 2D illustration where 3D could not reach the desired richness). [snippet] — [Nintendo "Ask the Developer" Vol. 8, part 2](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-8-fire-emblem-engage-part-2/)
- Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave (Switch 2, 17 Sep 2026): previews call its battle animations "the best they've ever looked"; battle load time is 3–4 s versus 15 s (Engage, Switch 1) and 30–35 s (Three Houses, Switch 1) — i.e., the map-to-battle hand-off is now near-instant; ghosting appears in handheld mode during fast animation/camera moves. [fetched] — [RPG Site, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/21403-fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-vs-three-houses-engage-visuals-graphics-performance-load-times); [snippet] — [Game Informer preview, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.gameinformer.com/preview/2026/08/13/there-and-back-and-there-again)

**Auto-resolved combat scenes as showpieces**
- Unicorn Overlord (2024): because battles run automatically, "we paid particular attention to captivating animation" that fast action games cannot afford (director Noma); auto-combat was fixed at the planning stage. [fetched] — [Inverse interview](https://nc.inverse.com/gaming/unicorn-overlord-interview-vanillaware)
- Critics: "some of the most gorgeously animated I have ever seen"; watching them "never gets old"; yet skipping animations for unimportant fights "saves a lot of time" (Graphics score 95). [fetched] — [RPGFan review](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/unicorn-overlord)
- Advance Wars 1+2: Re-Boot Camp (2023): battle cut-ins are "gawky soldiers fighting in front of low-detail backgrounds" (WCCFTech) and "crisp and consistent but not overly flashy" (GeekDad); CO Power animations are the highlight ("gorgeous", "cool the first time you see them"); eliminated units "bounce off the screen with a cartoonish sound effect". [snippet] — [WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/review/advance-wars-1-2-re-boot-camp/); [GeekDad](https://geekdad.com/2023/04/war-never-changes-geekdad-reviews-advance-wars-12-re-boot-camp-for-nintendo-switch/); [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/advance-wars-re-boot-camp-review); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-advance-wars-12-re-boot-camp-isnt-here-to-make-friends/); [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/134936/advance-wars-1-2-reboot-camp-preview)

**Cinematic cameras and cut-ins (Firaxis lineage and peers)**
- Marvel's Midnight Suns (2022): combat defaults to a close-up camera with an optional zoomed-out top-down "tactics mode"; Firaxis describes the combat as "fast and cinematic", translating comics by "pushing the animation to extremes". [snippet] — [Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20210909/281663963113477); [Unreal Engine developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/firaxis-games-talks-about-designing-and-developing-the-superhero-tactical-experience-of-marvel-s-midnight-suns)
- Reviews: "every single card has awesome animations and visual effects", but they "become repetitive after a while"; one review calls the close-ups "slightly-too-long" and unskippable. Camera movement around the battlefield "can be a little cumbersome". [snippet; outlets for the first quotes among TheGamer / Prima / GamesRadar results, not individually verified] — [TheGamer review](https://www.thegamer.com/marvels-midnight-suns-review/); [Laptop Mag](https://www.laptopmag.com/reviews/marvels-midnight-suns)
- XCOM 2 (2016, origin of the over-the-shoulder "action cam"): seamless cuts from over-the-shoulder targeting into a kill sequence, described as more varied than Enemy Unknown's. [snippet] — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/xcom-2-exclusive-class-reveal-the-sharpshooter/1100-6429035/)
- BATTLETECH (2018) is still cited in 2025 for its "cinematic critical-hit camera" and "weighty animations"; Warhammer 40,000: Battlesector for "crisp, weighty animations". [fetched] — [Strategy and Wargaming list](https://strategyandwargaming.com/2025/11/15/the-20-best-looking-strategy-games-to-play-in-2025)
- Wartales (2023): little animation fidelity overall, but "a bit of flair in its camera design, mainly during close-up shots during critical strikes". [snippet] — [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/wartales-review-in-details/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms (2019): hero duels play out inside the live battle with trash talk; firing arrows into a duel carries a morale penalty as "a very dishonorable thing to do". [fetched] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- Total War: Warhammer II (2017): "matched" fighting animations are authored for specific pairs of entities (e.g., Carnosaur vs dragon). [fetched] — [Game Developer, 2 Feb 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)
- Demonschool (2025): "visuals really pop, especially during combat, with all the flashy combos and abilities", plus "a satisfying animation when a demon is defeated"; bosses are larger low-poly 3D models. [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/demonschool-review); [3rd-strike](https://3rd-strike.com/demonschool-review/)
- Slay the Spire 2 (Early Access 5 Mar 2026): "improved visuals and animations" while retaining the rough brushstroke look. [snippet] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/slay-the-spire-2-early-access-features); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/new-slay-the-spire-2-gameplay-drives-home-just-how-much-better-the-game-looks/)

**Game-feel toolkit (how impact is manufactured)**
- Vlambeer "The Art of Screenshake" (Jan Willem Nijman, 2013) sequence, as reconstructed by a recreation project: bigger projectiles, muzzle flash, impact effect, hit reaction, enemy knockback, permanence (debris stays), camera lerp, screenshake, attacker knockback/recoil, *hit pause*, random explosions. [snippet] — [Art of Screenshake recreation devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects)
- Hit-stop guidance: "typically 2 to 5 frames"; "60–120 ms is plenty. Longer than that and the game feels laggy instead of punchy." [snippet] — [game feel guide](https://gamedo-live.pages.dev/news/game-feel-juice-guide-indie-devs); [Godot hit-stop tutorial](https://dev.to/saltmire/godot-4-screen-shake-and-hit-stop-in-one-script-11eh)
- Trauma-based shake (Squirrel Eiserloh, GDC 2016 "Juicing Your Cameras With Math"): gameplay adds trauma in 0..1, trauma decays over time, visible shake = trauma raised to a power (so small hits barely register), driven by smooth (Perlin) noise sampled at different offsets per axis rather than random jitter. [snippet] — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023146/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Juicing); [Bevy reference implementation](https://bevy.org/examples/camera/2d-screen-shake/); [Unity implementation crediting the talk](https://github.com/baratgabor/Unity3D-PerlinCameraShake)
- Juice checklist (Lovato): easing instead of linear tweens (ease-out for appearing UI); squash and stretch; hold/hit-stop "for a few frames"; dust on movement and sparks on impact; trails on fast weapons that vanish on hit; impact sounds with emphasised bass and mids, mixed louder; layered sine waves on rotation/scale/opacity for cheap procedural motion. Caution: excess juice "wastes time and annoys players". [fetched] — [Game Developer, 1 Apr 2015](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/squeezing-more-juice-out-of-your-game-design-)
- Hit-reaction craft (Massoudi): victim animation starts from the damaged pose, moves fast then eases out; zero cross-fade into hit reactions; heavy attacks get stronger shake; shake can include rotation and FOV, with FOV narrowing recommended only for one-on-one fights. [fetched] — [Game Developer, 25 Mar 2016](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-role-of-animations-in-hit-effects)
- Riot: visual/audio intensity should scale with gameplay importance (damage, hard CC, dodgeability, game impact), and important effects benefit from an audio cue before they land. [fetched] — [Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)
- "Poses instead of animation": community and indie-dev commentary credits Darkest Dungeon (2016) with selling attacks through held poses, sound and camera zoom/shake rather than animation; players nevertheless built a mod that removes the zoom on ordinary hits but keeps it for crits, and some found the zoom/shake/tilt nauseating. Darkest Dungeon II (2023) moved to 3D characters over 2D backdrops with short wind-up and recovery. [snippet] — [Nexus mod](https://www.nexusmods.com/darkestdungeon/mods/210); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/0/612823460256613837); [DD2 wiki](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Combat_Mechanics_(Darkest_Dungeon_II))

**How long before players want to skip**
- XCOM 2's most famous mod, "Stop Wasting My Time", targets a 1–3 s pause after shooting/grenades/abilities and a 2.75 s pause on entering cover; with the built-in Zip Mode "what was a 3-second pause is now a 0.6-second pause". The mod explicitly keeps weapon animations — it removes dead air, not spectacle. [snippet] — [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/620600092); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/50396/having-performance-issues-xcom-2-mod-stops-wasting-time/index.html)
- Advance Wars Re-Boot Camp: a reviewer "did turn off the battle animations rather quickly". [snippet] — [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/63616/advance-wars-12-re-boot-camp-switch-review)
- Battle Brothers' developers predicted in 2014 that attack animations "get repetitive" and players would speed them up or prefer instant movement. [fetched] — [Dev Blog #5](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=414)

### Inferences
- The strongest praise goes to transitions that preserve spatial continuity (Engage: the combat backdrop is the map location). For a DOM grid + staged war scene, the equivalent is to seed the war-scene background from the tile's terrain type and time of day, and to bridge with a 250–400 ms camera push (scale + blur on the grid, then cross-fade) rather than a hard cut.
- "Remove the dead air, keep the hit" is the lesson of the XCOM mod: budget every beat. A reasonable target, extrapolated from the evidence (not directly sourced as a number): routine attack exchange ≤ ~1.5–2.5 s end to end, signature/crit moments 3–5 s and always skippable, with inter-action pauses under ~0.6 s.
- A no-animator "impact sandwich" built entirely from the cited toolkit: anticipation (attacker token slides back 6–10 px, 120 ms ease-in) → lunge (ease-out, 80–100 ms) → hit-stop (60–120 ms freeze of both tokens) with white flash on the victim → trauma shake scaled to damage share → victim knockback 4–8 px and ease back → damage number pops with overshoot and drifts → lazy health-bar drain → dust/spark particles on canvas. Crits add: brief desaturation of the board, a diagonal portrait cut-in strip (AI-painted bust, CSS clip-path wipe, 300–500 ms), larger number with a second colour, and a lower-pitched impact sound.
- For the three.js duel: Eiserloh's trauma model maps directly to camera offset/roll from simplex noise; hit-stop is a temporary `timeScale` of ~0.05 for 80–120 ms; FOV punch (narrow by 3–5 degrees then ease back) suits one-on-one fights per Massoudi; a kill cam can be a scripted dolly along a spline with slow-motion on the final blow.
- Darkest Dungeon's held-pose approach is the best fit for AI-painted art: two or three painted poses per general (ready / strike / hit) swapped with zoom, parallax shift between foreground and background layers, and shake can substitute for frame animation — but make the zoom optional, since players modded it out.
- **Needs professional animators:** matched/sync-kill animation pairs (Total War), Fire Emblem-style bespoke crit animations per class, Midnight Suns-style card cinematics, and Vanillaware-style multi-layer 2D animation. Procedural IK gaits and ragdoll-ish reactions can cover the 3D duel only to a "stylised" standard.

### Gaps
- Firaxis's own account of the XCOM/Midnight Suns cinematic camera system could not be read (GamesRadar and PC Gamer pages returned truncated content; Unreal Engine interview returned 403). No documented data on how often the action cam fires or its target shot lengths.
- No developer source found on Fire Emblem Engage's animation pipeline (motion capture, hit-stop values, crit cut-in design); Nintendo's "Ask the Developer" results surfaced only the 3D-models point.
- No hard, sourced number for "seconds before players skip"; the 1–3 s figures are from a mod changelog, not a study.
- Mechabellum, Slay the Spire 2 and TFT feedback specifics (damage numbers, card impact frames) were only available at headline level.
- Whether Midnight Suns ever added a skip for ability animations was not confirmed.

## 4. Mass battles: conveying thousands of soldiers; faking large armies in 2D or lightweight engines

### Takeaway
AAA mass battles are a rendering-plus-simulation problem solved with LOD, instancing, GPU particles, and decoupled logic/display threads, then sold with banners, matched animations, and weather. Lightweight games convey "an army" with a leader plus a handful of visible soldiers, billboards in a 3D scene, and a separate combat cut-in — and in the browser, vertex-shader animation on a single instanced mesh now reaches tens of thousands of figures.

### Cited Findings

**AAA approaches**
- Total War: Warhammer II (2017): LODs for units and environment "help a great deal from a GPU-load perspective" but were not sufficient alone; the engine moved to multicore "in earnest" and *decoupled battle logic/animation from display* — "the logic generates the 'future' whilst the display renders the 'now'." Every projectile is physically simulated, so collision checks are queued. Matched combat animations are authored per entity pair. [fetched] — [Game Developer, 2 Feb 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)
- Total War engine (Attila era; Tamas Rabel, lead graphics programmer): particles are spawned on CPU, simulated and sorted on GPU, with three GPU particle types (quad, point light, projected decal); 2–4 shadow cascades with terrain excluded from the shadow map; RTS scenes are characterised by "many small meshes". [fetched] — [AMD GPUOpen, 27 Jul 2016](https://gpuopen.com/learn/anatomy-total-war-engine-part/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms (2019): an Intel deck titled "Instancing and OIT in Total War: Three Kingdoms" describes a GPU particle buffer where dead particles are sorted to one end and, when the buffer is full, the particles farthest from the camera are overwritten. [snippet; PDF too large to fetch] — [Intel slides](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/develop/external/us/en/documents/instancing-and-oit-in-total-war-three-kingdoms.pdf)
- Three Kingdoms campaign/battle art reduces distant visual noise with fog and depth of field ("that subtle blur at a distance"). [fetched] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- Dynasty Warriors: Origins (Jan 2025): the programmers "wanted to show at least 10,000 soldiers on the screen" (producer Tomohiko Sho); the original brief was "as many enemies as the PS5 could handle". [fetched] — [TheGamer via OpenCritic, 13 Oct 2024](https://opencritic.com/news/7288/dynasty-warriors-programmers-set-themself-the-goal-of-getting-10-000-soldiers-on-screen); [snippet] — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/dynasty-warriors-origins-devs-aimed-to-put-as-many-enemies-as-the-ps5-could-handle-on-screen/)
- Origins hands-on: "governed by individually assigned algorithms, thousands of soldiers independently jostle in a single screen"; an aerial "Eyes of the Sacred Bird" view (L2) lets the player order arrow volleys, spear charges that break lines, and encirclements; a duel is staged with enemy soldiers forming a ring around the two fighters; morale visibly swings after ambushing a commander. [fetched] — [PlayStation Blog, 25 Sep 2024](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=396742)
- Reviews call Origins the best the series has looked and note "1 vs 1000" encounters. [snippet] — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/dynasty-warriors-origins-proves-you-can-teach-an-old-dog-new-tricks-review/); [VideoGamer](https://videogamer.com/reviews/dynasty-warriors-origins-review)
- Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord (1.0 2022): official battle-size slider runs 200–1000 combatants; horses count as separate entities (1,000 cavalry = 2,000 entities); larger armies arrive as reinforcement waves; mods push to ~2,048. [snippet] — [Raider King guide](https://raiderking.com/mount-and-blade-ii-bannerlord-how-to-increase-battle-size/); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/mount-blade-ii-bannerlord-to-feature-battles-of-up-to-1000-soldie/ze4a9)
- Manor Lords (Early Access Apr 2024): combat received "a complete animation rework"; reviewers describe "blocky formations and shield walls crashing into each other" and Total War-like emphasis on formations; some find the combat itself disappointing and numbers-driven. [snippet] — [GameSpace](https://www.gamespace.com/all-articles/news/manor-lords-battle-features-trailer/); [eXputer](https://exputer.com/reviews/manor-lords-2/)
- Mechabellum (EA 2023 / 1.0 2024): PC Gamer's assessment is that "the units are readable but impactful, the battles chaotic but understandable". [snippet] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/strategy/mechabellum-is-a-conversation-you-should-be-having); [DBLTAP review](https://www.dbltap.com/reviews/mechabellum-review)

**Lightweight / 2D fakes**
- Songs of Conquest: 2D billboards in a 3D scene; rotation approximated in a custom shader with each object's origin baked into the mesh so billboards do not need individual draw calls; "tens of thousands" of mostly animated billboards; real-time top-down-projected lightmap rendered by a separate camera, including cloud shadows. [fetched] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Fire Emblem: Three Houses: one leader plus battalion soldiers in the zoomed view stands in for a company. [snippet] — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2019/07/7-improvements-that-fire-emblem-three-houses-has-over-its-previous-games/)
- Symphony of War: nine-figure squads per token fight in a cut-in. [snippet] — [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_of_War:_The_Nephilim_Saga)
- Unicorn Overlord: enlarged hand-drawn characters against "beautifully painted backgrounds". [fetched] — [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/unicorn-overlord)
- Romance of the Three Kingdoms 8 Remake (Oct 2024; closest genre peer): praised hand-drawn artwork but characters are "static portraits"; battles and duels are the only 3D-intensive parts; reception divisive. [snippet] — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-8-remake-brings-back-a-classic/); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/strategy/a-remake-of-strategy-classic-romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-8-has-proven-divisive-among-its-fanbase)

**Browser / three.js evidence**
- A Feb 2026 three.js showcase renders a walking crowd of up to 100,000 figures in *one draw call*: one merged low-poly geometry in a single `InstancedMesh`; limb motion computed in the vertex shader using a per-vertex part ID (head/body/legs); vertex-shader LOD that simplifies or merges limbs with distance and for back-facing figures; per-instance colour/flags packed into a few instance attributes; transform updates staggered across frames with distant figures updated less often; author reports sub-2 ms GPU time. [fetched] — [three.js forum, 19 Feb 2026](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/one-draw-call-massive-crowd-performance-engineering-in-three-js/89928)
- `InstancedMesh` does not support skinned or morph-target animation per instance; the standard workaround is a vertex animation texture (bake animation frames into a texture, sample in the vertex shader, per-instance frame offset as an instanced attribute). [snippet] — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/instancedmesh-animations-like-with-skinnedmesh/12388); [Wildlife Studios on texture animation](https://medium.com/tech-at-wildlife-studios/texture-animation-techniques-1daecb316657)
- Octahedral impostors exist as community three.js code: a demo shows 200k trees using a three-tier LOD (mesh → simplified mesh at 15–100 units → impostor at 100+); core `InstancedMesh` has no per-instance LOD, so use one instanced group per LOD level; impostor quads need a corrected bounding sphere or the whole batch gets frustum-culled. [snippet] — [three.js forum: forest of octahedral impostors](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/a-forest-of-octahedral-impostors/85735); [LOD + instancing thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/lod-instancing/20524); [cinevva blog, 4 May 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-05-04-open-world-browser-part-19-imposters)

### Inferences
- What reads as "a huge army" is less the count than a set of cues: uniform formation blocks with slight per-soldier phase offsets, many banners, weapon glints, a dust band at ground level, arrow volleys as arcing streaks, and depth haze that swallows the rear ranks (the Three Kingdoms fog/DoF note). All of these are shader/particle work rather than animation work.
- **Staged war scene, 2D route.** Three to five parallax layers: painted sky and far hills (AI-painted) → far army as a tiled silhouette strip with a scrolling spear-tip shimmer and 1–2 px vertical jitter → mid formation blocks as sprite crowds drawn on canvas from a small atlas (4–8 frame strips scrolled in step, as in Songs of Conquest) → foreground hero figures and banners → dust/smoke overlay. Casualties shown by thinning the mid layer and dropping banners; a rout shown by reversing a block's march direction, breaking its grid alignment, and swapping its banner to white.
- **Staged war scene, three.js route.** One merged low-poly soldier in a single `InstancedMesh` with vertex-shader walk/attack cycles and per-instance colour, exactly as in the Feb 2026 showcase; 2,000–10,000 figures is plausible on mid-range hardware. Cavalry can be a second instanced mesh. Arrows as instanced thin quads on ballistic arcs; dust as soft particle quads; banners as instanced planes with sine-wave vertex displacement. Far ranks can drop to camera-facing sprite impostors.
- Dynasty Warriors: Origins' ring of soldiers around a duel is an inexpensive framing device for the existing mounted-duel scene: an instanced ring of idle/cheering figures and banners gives the duel a "whole army is watching" context without simulating a battle.
- **Needs professional animators / engineers:** matched combat between individual soldiers, per-soldier collision and physics, and motion-captured formation drills. Shader-driven loops are enough for mid- and far-distance crowds.

### Gaps
- No technical talk (CEDEC, Digital Foundry or otherwise) found explaining how Dynasty Warriors: Origins renders or simulates its crowds; the 10,000 figure is a stated development goal, not a verified on-screen count.
- The Intel Three Kingdoms slides could not be opened; instancing specifics (draw-call counts, LOD tiers, whether impostors are used) are unverified. The Game Developer article does not mention impostors or instancing at all.
- Nothing found on Manor Lords' or Bannerlord's rendering/animation tech (motion capture, dust, formation logic) beyond review-level description.
- No source found describing parallax or sprite-crowd techniques in a named 2D strategy game; the 2D recipe above is inferred from adjacent evidence.

## 5. Environmental atmosphere: weather, time of day, fire, smoke, lighting, destruction, post-processing

### Takeaway
Atmosphere is increasingly *systemic and mid-battle* (Total War: Pharaoh's shifting weather and spreading fire), while the stylised camp (HD-2D, Songs of Conquest) gets its look from a small, repeatable post-processing stack: point lights attached to VFX, depth of field/tilt-shift, bloom, colour grading, fog, and cloud shadows — all of which are available in the browser.

### Cited Findings
- Total War: Pharaoh (Oct 2023): weather states include Sweltering Heat, Sandstorm, Fog, Rain, Storm and Thunderstorm; "conditions can change throughout the battle itself"; rain can escalate to storm; heat after rain dries mud; units can ignite buildings and tall grass and heat turns a small blaze into an "uncontrollable inferno"; fog reduces visibility; thunderstorms add "lightning clapping the second your troops collide". [fetched; note the article is a paid partnership with Sega] — [PC Gamer, 4 Oct 2023](https://www.pcgamer.com/total-war-pharaohs-dynamic-weather-system-lets-you-feel-the-heat-of-battle)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms (2019) night battles: a blue palette for a "mystical, almost magical sense", not "too dark and gritty"; lanterns and fires provide contrast; goal: "It feel like night, but it's super clear and visible in how you play it out." Campaign map runs a continuous day–night cycle. [fetched] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- HD-2D (Octopath Traveler 2018 → Triangle Strategy 2022 → later titles): pixel characters in 3D scenes with tilt-shift, depth of field, bloom, volumetric light and fog, and particles. [snippet] — [Wikipedia: HD-2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D)
- Acquire found plain battle VFX underwhelming, so they attached a *point light to each visual effect*, which also casts character shadows onto the environment. [snippet] — [Unreal Engine spotlight, 25 Oct 2019](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/spotlights/octopath-traveler-s-hd-2d-art-style-and-story-make-for-a-jrpg-dream-come-true)
- Triangle Strategy: "accurate" HD-2D is built up from a deliberately stylised base image with realistic effects added on top, not by shrinking a photorealistic render (which "looks like lowered image quality"); Artdink was chosen because only its test achieved this. Asano says HD-2D costs more than people assume. [fetched] — [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/triangle-strategy-devs-on-how-the-game-uses-accurate-hd-2d); [snippet] — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/triangle-strategys-art-style-costs-more-than-youd-think-producer)
- Songs of Conquest: real-time lightmap made of light sprites rendered by a separate top-down camera and projected in shaders; supports light, darkness and colour, including drifting cloud shadows; day/night mostly via colour grading plus ambient settings; post stack = bloom, vignette, colour grading, depth of field on zoom-ins. [fetched] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Total War engine: particle types include point-light particles and projected decals (scorch marks, blood) in addition to quads. [fetched] — [GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/learn/anatomy-total-war-engine-part/)
- Manor Lords (EA 2024): naturalistic lighting that shifts with time of day, seasonal weather, terrain deformation. BATTLETECH: destructible buildings and strong lighting. [fetched] — [Strategy and Wargaming](https://strategyandwargaming.com/2025/11/15/the-20-best-looking-strategy-games-to-play-in-2025)
- Unicorn Overlord (2024): stage hazards include fire, siege weapons and sandstorms. [fetched] — [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/unicorn-overlord)
- Juice-level ambience: looping quiet soundscapes (fire crackle, water) under the music; layered sine waves for wind on plants; bonfire as animated scale and opacity. [fetched] — [Game Developer (Lovato)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/squeezing-more-juice-out-of-your-game-design-)

### Inferences
- The "light attached to the effect" trick is the highest-leverage HD-2D lesson: every fire arrow, explosion or stratagem should briefly light its surroundings. On a DOM/canvas board this is an additive radial gradient (`globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'`) under the effect plus a short brightness bump on neighbouring tiles; in three.js it is a pooled `PointLight` (or an emissive sprite with bloom) per effect.
- Three Kingdoms' rule for night ("feels like night but perfectly readable") translates to a colour-grade rather than darkness: multiply-blend a blue tint over painted tiles, keep unit tokens at near-full value, add warm torch glows.
- **Browser approximation.** Time of day and weather as a *grading layer*: CSS `mix-blend-mode`/canvas overlay with a LUT-like tint per state (dawn amber, noon neutral, dusk magenta, night blue); rain as 2–3 parallax layers of slanted streaks; fog as two slow-scrolling noise textures; sandstorm as a horizontal noise scroll plus desaturation; cloud shadows as a large soft noise texture multiplied over the map (Songs of Conquest). Fire spread on the grid: tiles gain an animated flame sprite/shader, ember particles, a flickering warm light, then switch to a scorched variant (a projected-decal analogue). In three.js: fog (`FogExp2`), bloom and vignette via post-processing, tilt-shift blur on the war scene to get the HD-2D diorama feel.
- Fire attacks are central to Three Kingdoms fiction; Pharaoh shows that making fire *spread and persist* (scorched ground, smoke columns visible from the map) is what sells it, more than the initial burst.
- **Needs specialists:** volumetric lighting with true shadows on 2D sprites and physically based destruction. Painted "before/after" variants per destructible prop are a sufficient substitute.

### Gaps
- Unreal's Octopath spotlight and Octopath II interview returned 403; exact settings (DoF strength, bloom thresholds, how sprites receive light) are unverified beyond the snippet.
- No rendering-level detail on how Pharaoh draws sandstorms/fire spread; the source is a sponsored feature.
- Nothing retrieved on Manor Lords' weather/lighting tech or on destructible elements in 2025–2026 tactics releases (e.g., Mars Tactics is only listed as having destructible buildings — [MagicGameWorld](https://www.magicgameworld.com/11-best-turn-based-strategy-tactics-games-of-2026/) [snippet]).

## 6. Result and aftermath presentation: victory/defeat, casualty reports, loot, promotion moments

### Takeaway
There is little formal writing on aftermath screens for this genre; what evidence exists says a good result screen states a clear verdict, explains *why*, gives a reconciled casualty breakdown with optional drill-down, stays proportionate to the battle's complexity, and adds a short ceremonial beat that makes the win feel earned.

### Cited Findings
- Teamfight Tactics (2025): a "Top Four ceremony" animation was well received — many players said it "made Top Four feel more like a win"; Riot will keep it in a set-agnostic style. Each champion also got an "anime pop" moment when a Power Up was applied. [fetched] — [TFT dev: K.O. Coliseum Learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-tft-ko-coliseum-learnings)
- Demonschool (2025): reviewers single out "a satisfying animation when a demon is defeated". [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/demonschool-review)
- Advance Wars Re-Boot Camp (2023): criticised for a pause before the game registers a win or loads voice lines — dead air at the moment of victory is noticed. [snippet] — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-advance-wars-12-re-boot-camp-isnt-here-to-make-friends/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms (2019): heroes are "precious"; retreat/extraction after a loss is part of the aftermath fiction. [fetched] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- Design-forum consensus on result screens (lower-authority sources): show the verdict and the reasons, not just abstract numbers; break casualties into killed / wounded / condition; put deep stats behind an "Advanced" button; scale the screen to the battle (an elaborate screen after a simple fight "will be a bit weird"); make totals reconcile (a Paradox bug report shows players notice when summary and detail disagree). [snippet] — [Battlefront forum](https://community.battlefront.com/topic/81877-objectives-and-the-end-game-screen); [Amplitude forum](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/endless-space-2/forums/66-game-design/threads/20628-post-cinematic-battle-analysis-screen-for-better-decision-making?page=1); [GameDev.net thread](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/679013-ways-to-visualise-battle-results); [Paradox forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/battle-statistics-arithmetic-is-wrong.1855193)
- Positech (2026) redesigned a battle-results screen with stats split into offensive and defensive tabs and a toggle between the previous-battle overlay and the next deployment. [snippet] — [Cliffski's blog, Apr 2026](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2026/04/)

### Inferences
- A result sequence that fits the evidence: (1) immediate verdict stinger with no dead air (ink-brush "勝/敗" stamp, banner raise or fall, 0.8–1.2 s); (2) one-line cause ("enemy routed after commander fell"); (3) casualty ledger animated as lazy-drain bars per unit with count-up numbers, split into lost / wounded / captured; (4) spoils and promotions as discrete card reveals; (5) optional "details" tab. Everything skippable with one input.
- The Three Kingdoms ink-on-paper UI language (flowing ink, painted portraits) is a ready-made aesthetic for the result stamp and ledger, and is achievable with SVG masks/`clip-path` wipes and an ink-bleed displacement filter over AI-painted portraits.
- Promotion moments: borrow TFT's "pop" — a brief full-colour burst and outline flash on the promoted general's portrait with a rising chime; no animation staff required.

### Gaps
- No critic or developer source found that analyses victory/defeat, casualty-report or promotion/level-up screens in Fire Emblem, Unicorn Overlord, Total War, Manor Lords or Dynasty Warriors: Origins. This key question is the weakest-sourced in these notes; the forum sources are opinion, several about older or unrelated games.

## 7. Speed and skip options; accessibility of effects

### Takeaway
A fast-forward and a per-fight skip are now baseline expectations, reviewers want more than one speed step, and the best practice from platform guidelines is individual 0–100% sliders (or at least toggles) for screen shake, full-screen flashes and camera motion.

### Cited Findings
- Fire Emblem Engage (2023): menu options to skip combat animations and speed up battles. [snippet] — [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/10061545-fire-emblem-engage-review-switch-gameplay-impressions-videos)
- Unicorn Overlord (2024): per-battle skip praised as a time saver; pausing stops the stage timer so planning is never rushed. [fetched] — [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/unicorn-overlord)
- FFT: The Ivalice Chronicles (2025): a Fast Forward setting adjusts how fast actions and animations play; recommended for grinding but off for careful positioning; a reviewer warns that fast-forwarding enemy turns can leave you "mid-beatdown before they realize it's happening". A Classic mode preserves the original look. [snippet] — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Final-Fantasy-Tactics/archives/541793); [Corsair explainer](https://www.corsair.com/explorer/gamer/gaming-pcs/final-fantasy-tactics-the-ivalice-chronicles-whats-new-vs-original/); [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25252362-final-fantasy-tactics-ivalice-chronicles-review-gameplay-impressions-videos)
- Tactics Ogre: Reborn (2022): only a single speed-up step — "it would have been nice to see a few more speeds"; one reviewer found 2x "still too slow" with two dozen units and resorted to a Cheat Engine speedhack; conversely enemy action choices flash by in under a second at default speed. [snippet] — [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/62324); [DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=167341); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/tactics-ogre-reborn-ps5-preview-more-simple-visual-overhaul)
- Advance Wars Re-Boot Camp (2023): fast-forward for repetitive battle animations, plus the ability to turn them off. [snippet] — [WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/review/advance-wars-1-2-re-boot-camp/); [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/63616/advance-wars-12-re-boot-camp-switch-review)
- XCOM 2 (2016): built-in Zip Mode; the community mod removing inter-action pauses is described by a user as among the most popular mods ever. [snippet] — [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/620600092); [Nexus Mods comments](https://www.nexusmods.com/xcom2/mods/159?tab=posts)
- Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117: "Avoid the use of camera shake, camera bobbing effects, motion blur ... or provide an option to turn off these behaviors"; provide a mechanism to disable or pause moving, blinking, scrolling or flashing content, including animated backgrounds behind text. Example: Halo Infinite exposes separate 0–100% sliders for radial blur, screen shake, full-screen effects and speed lines. [fetched] — [Microsoft Learn, XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)
- XAG 118: all games should be tested for photosensitive seizure triggers whether or not flashing is intentional; mitigations are lower contrast between brightest and darkest parts of a flash and lower frequency. [snippet] — [Microsoft Learn, XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)
- Steamworks' accessibility checklist includes "Camera Comfort": players can adjust or disable screen shake, camera bob or motion blur, or the game has none. [snippet] — [Steamworks docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/accessibility_features?l=english)
- Gears Tactics (2020) ships a camera-shake intensity setting; Cult of the Lamb (2022) offers screenshake sensitivity, reduced camera motion and a flashing-lights toggle. [snippet] — [Gears Tactics accessibility page](https://www.gearsofwar.com/en-us/games/gears-tactics/accessibility/); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/cult-of-the-lamb/accessibility-settings/z9102ae)
- Darkest Dungeon (2016) has a Camera Shake option, yet players still made a mod to remove combat zoom except on crits. [snippet] — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/0/612823460256613837); [Nexus mod](https://www.nexusmods.com/darkestdungeon/mods/210)
- Colour: do not rely on colour alone for critical tile states; allow UI colour configuration. [snippet] — [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/feature/project-triangle-strategys-invisible-feature-and-the-trials-of-gaming-while-colorblind/)

### Inferences
- Recommended option set, synthesised from the above: battle speed 1x / 2x / 4x (more than one step, per the Tactics Ogre complaints); "combat scenes: full / short / off" with separate settings for player and enemy actions (the FFT:IC caveat shows that fast enemy turns hide information, so pair fast-forward with a readable end-of-turn summary or combat log); hold-to-skip on any cinematic; screen shake 0–100%; flashes on/reduced/off; camera zoom-on-hit on/off; reduced-motion master toggle; colour-blind tile patterns.
- **Browser specifics.** Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` as the default for the reduced-motion toggle; drive all effect durations from a single `timeScale` multiplier so speed settings apply to DOM tweens, canvas particles and the three.js clock together; clamp full-screen flashes to low contrast and never more than about three per second (the WCAG general flash threshold was mentioned in search results alongside XAG 118 but not verified on a fetched page).
- Because first-viewing spectacle and hundredth-viewing tedium conflict, an "auto-shorten after first view" rule (full signature animation the first time a general uses it in a campaign, short version thereafter) reconciles the Advance Wars "cool the first time you see them" observation with the skip evidence.

### Gaps
- No documented example found of a 2024–2026 tactics game with a reduced-motion or flash-reduction option specific to combat cut-ins; the guideline sources are platform-level, the game examples older or outside the genre.
- Could not confirm exact speed/skip options in Unicorn Overlord (a search summary mentioned a fast-forward plus outright skip and that late-game battles are long at normal speed, but the outlet was not pinned down), Symphony of War, Songs of Conquest, Olden Era, Triangle Strategy, or Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave.
- Game Accessibility Guidelines (gameaccessibilityguidelines.com) pages were referenced by XAG 117 but not fetched.

## 8. Cautionary lessons: over-long animations, unreadable effects, clutter

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are dead air between actions, single-step or missing speed controls, effects whose loudness is not proportional to their importance, colour-only encodings, filters or pixel styles that reduce legibility, and information spread over too many screens.

### Cited Findings
- **Dead air and length.** XCOM 2's 1–3 s post-action pauses and 2.75 s cover-entry pause spawned one of its most-used mods. [snippet] — [Steam Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/620600092). Midnight Suns' card animations "become repetitive after a while". [snippet] — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/marvels-midnight-suns-review/). Advance Wars reviewers switched animations off early. [snippet] — [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/63616/advance-wars-12-re-boot-camp-switch-review). Symphony of War's squad battles are reported (search summary; outlet not pinned) to run slowly until numbers thin out when many characters are on screen — see [RPGFan review](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/symphony-of-war-the-nephilim-saga) / [TechRaptor review](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/symphony-of-war-nephilim-saga-review) [snippet, unverified attribution].
- **Speed without information.** Tactics Ogre: Reborn shows enemy choices for "barely ... a second"; FFT:IC fast-forward can hide a beat-down in progress. [snippet] — [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/tactics-ogre-reborn-ps5-preview-more-simple-visual-overhaul); [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25252362-final-fantasy-tactics-ivalice-chronicles-review-gameplay-impressions-videos)
- **Filters and resolution mismatch.** Tactics Ogre: Reborn's sprite smoothing "can't be turned off", was called a "drab visual filter", and clashes with crisp high-resolution UI; producer Hiroaki Kato confirmed HD-2D was never considered. [snippet] — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/tactics-ogre-reborn-review); [Tech-Gaming](https://tech-gaming.com/tactics-ogre-reborn); [DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=167341); [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/news/2022/09/tactics-ogre-reborn-producer-we-didnt-think-about-making-it-hd-2d)
- **Style over legibility.** Songs of Conquest's large-pixel look is hard to read for some players. [snippet] — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/songs-of-conquest/can-the-pixel-art-style-be-turned-off/za1142b). Olden Era's art was called "fairly unremarkable ... typical of cheap mobile fantasy strategy games" with weak map readability by one preview, despite clarity being a stated pillar. [snippet] — [VGTimes](https://vgtimes.com/video-game-previews/156390-heroes-of-might-and-magic-olden-era-preview.html)
- **Indicator overload.** Into the Breach's early build with damage markers "all over the map" only made sense to its authors. [fetched] — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-)
- **Effect noise.** Riot: keep noise minimal, reserve heavy VFX for high-impact abilities, never let a cosmetic be less clear than the base. [fetched] — [Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/); [snippet] — [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/?p=235988)
- **Chaos at scale.** A negative Mechabellum review (outlet not pinned in search summary) says "half the time you can't really see what's going on"; PC Gamer's counter-view is "chaotic but understandable". [snippet] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/strategy/mechabellum-is-a-conversation-you-should-be-having)
- **Colour-only encoding.** Triangle Strategy's blue/purple tiles and thin red lines fail colour-blind players. [snippet] — [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/feature/project-triangle-strategys-invisible-feature-and-the-trials-of-gaming-while-colorblind/)
- **Camera nausea.** Darkest Dungeon's zoom/shake/tilt was called nauseating by some players; FOV punches distract when several enemies attack at once. [snippet] — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/0/612823460256613837); [fetched] — [Game Developer (Massoudi)](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-role-of-animations-in-hit-effects)

[snippet] — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/marvels-midnight-suns-review/); [fetched] — [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/unicorn-overlord); [snippet] — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-8-remake-brings-back-a-classic/)

- **Cost realism.** HD-2D is "more expensive than people think" and rotating maps consumed "a lot of resources". [snippet] — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/triangle-strategys-art-style-costs-more-than-youd-think-producer); [fetched] — [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/triangle-strategy-devs-on-how-the-game-uses-accurate-hd-2d)

### Inferences
- A practical "loudness budget" for a three-mode battle presentation: rank every event (routine hit < crit < stratagem < general defeated < battle decided) and assign shake, flash, sound and duration strictly in that order, so the war scene and duel finale are the only places that use the top of the range.
- AI-painted art carries two specific risks highlighted by these cases: (1) Tactics Ogre-style mismatch between crisp UI and soft/filtered art — keep painted assets at consistent resolution and avoid runtime smoothing; (2) uneven polish — a lavish duel beside static, lifeless tokens will be noticed, so spread small idle motion everywhere before deepening any one scene.
- Fixed, non-rotating boards avoid the largest HD-2D cost centre (360-degree maps), which favours keeping the 8×8 grid at one authored angle with painted depth cues.
- The single most repeated lesson across sources: let the player see what will happen before it happens, and let them stop watching it once they have.

### Gaps
- Critiques of clutter in Baldur's Gate 3 combat (surfaces, overlapping AoE markers), Age of Wonders 4 and Warhammer III spell effects were sought but no citable critic or developer analysis was found; BG3 results were mostly about rules complexity and character-sheet UI.
- No quantitative player research (telemetry on skip rates, preferred animation lengths) was found for any game in scope.
- Several notable 2025–2026 releases named in listings (MENACE, Mars Tactics, Star Wars: Zero Company, Endless Legend 2, Songs of Silence) could not be assessed for battle-visual craft from the pages retrieved; Songs of Silence is known here only by a review headline calling it "a gorgeous auto-battling feast for the eyes" — [SI/GLHF](https://videogames.si.com/reviews/songs-of-silence-early-access) [snippet; page now returns HTTP 410].
