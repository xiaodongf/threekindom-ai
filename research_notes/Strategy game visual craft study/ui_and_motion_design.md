## Layout and hierarchy: how the best strategy UIs organise top bar, resources, side panels, city/character screens, notifications and turn controls; how much screen stays free; praised versus cluttered density

### Takeaway
Praised interfaces keep the map dominant, pin a small amount of always-relevant information to the screen edges, and move detail into on-demand panels, pinned pop-ups and tooltips; density itself is not the complaint. What gets called "cluttered" or "bad" is undifferentiated priority (everything shouts, or important things are silent), information hidden two or more clicks deep, empty space next to missing data, and notifications or modals that stack over what the player is doing.

### Cited Findings

**Map-first chrome and on-demand detail**
- Total War: Three Kingdoms (2019) kept detail in pop-ups so the main screen stayed simple, and TechSpot called the UI the game's "real visual star" — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/review/1854-total-war-three-kingdoms/) (S). GameSpot called the interface clean and noted information can be pinned and dismissed at will — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/total-war-three-kingdoms-review-true-romance/1900-6417142/) (S).
- Frostpunk 2 (2024): a Japanese play report described the layout as clean and easy to see, with icons placed unobtrusively at the screen edges — [Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2024/10/01/145618.html) (S).
- Frostpunk 2's gamepad "Command Radial" (top-level navigation: Idea Tree, Frostland, Council) "temporarily hides most HUD elements with a blurred background, focusing the player's attention", shows progress indicators for research/council/expeditions on the radial itself, and pauses time while open; an "Extended City View" also freezes time and lets players cycle through HUD icons for more depth — [Xbox Wire, 18 Sept 2025](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/09/18/adapting-frostpunk-2s-depth-to-a-gamepad/).
- Crusader Kings III console team: players want "as much information at their fingertips as possible"; the design avoids fullscreen menus to preserve immersion and keeps the PC behaviour of "flicking between menus, the map and popups". Structure is a top-level Command Bar, radial menus (character, map modes), a Quick Access Bar, Hints and Notifications — [Paradox console dev diary 3](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/crusader-kings-iii/news/ck3-console-dev-diary-3-uiux-and-controls).
- CK3's art team moved character portraits "front and center" and aimed for a clean look with room to breathe, drawing on tabletop and computer RPG interfaces — [CK3 Dev Diary 28, art focus](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ckiii-dev-diary-28-art-focus.1393627/) (S). PCGamesN credits CK3 with having "massively overhauled the series' formerly byzantine interface" — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/best-strategy-games) (S).
- Manor Lords (EA 2024): top and bottom screen icons give "just the right amount of information" — [Magnetic Mag](https://magneticmag.com/2022/10/manor-lords-demo-review/) (S); the UI shows where supplies stand "and how long they will last" — [Sports Illustrated](https://videogames.si.com/reviews/manor-lords-early-access) (S); "a dizzying array of options, but it never feels overwhelming" — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/best-city-builder-2024-manor-lords) (S); the interface is contextual — [Gameluster](https://gameluster.com/manor-lords-early-access-review-hamfisted-hochadel/) (S). Counter-view: "a bit tricky to read" — [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/manor-lords-early-access-1383573/) (S).
- Persona 5's battle UI sorts information into three tiers — core actions around the active character, supportive (party HP/SP, enemy name and target), and extraneous (party chatter in a corner) — with the four most-used actions grouped together — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=7280).

### Inferences
- A consistent recipe emerges: (1) thin persistent chrome on the edges (top resource strip, small corner clusters, one obvious end-turn / time control); (2) one contextual side sheet for the selected thing (city, hero, army) rather than a fullscreen takeover; (3) detail via pinned pop-ups and nested tooltips; (4) notifications in a single feed with importance tiers and per-type mute; (5) irrelevant items hidden rather than greyed (Victoria 3's convoy counter).
- For the target game (top bar + city panel + heroes/diplomacy/tech/advisor panels + many modals + chronicle): demote as many modals as possible to side sheets or non-blocking toasts; queue modals so they never stack over an event choice (the Endless Legend 2 complaint); show whole numbers in the top bar and decimals only in breakdowns (the Civ VII fix); put "time-to-X" values (turns until growth, supplies remaining) on the surface, since reviewers single those out as valuable (Manor Lords, Civ VII).
- Preview-before-commit is the most praised density feature in 2025–2026 4X (Endless Legend 2 previews, Civ VII 1.2.5 green/red arrows). For military orders, recruitment and construction modals, show before/after deltas inline.
- Implementation: a full-viewport CSS grid overlay with `pointer-events: none` and `pointer-events: auto` only on panels keeps the three.js canvas interactive; side sheets ~360–420 px wide; a blurred, dimmed backdrop (`backdrop-filter: blur(8px)` plus a 40–60% scrim) for focus states like Frostpunk 2's radial; `position: sticky` headers inside panels so the title and close control never scroll away. These sizes are my suggestions, not sourced values.

## Visual language: flat versus skeuomorphic/diegetic materials, panel framing and ornament, illustration inside UI, colour discipline, dark versus light surfaces

### Takeaway
The titles praised for looking premium pair a restrained, clean structure with one strong setting-specific material motif (ink and paper in Three Kingdoms, leather and earthy texture in Against the Storm, mechanical circles with a gold accent in Frostpunk 2) and a strict colour system where hue carries meaning. Both extremes are punished: heavy "patchwork" ornament hurts readability, but stripping all texture produces the "drab grey slabs" criticism levelled at Civilization VII and the backlash Eremite Games faced in 2022.

### Cited Findings

**Total War: Three Kingdoms — ink, paper, and a five-colour system**
- Art director Pawel Wojs: "Rather than the skeuomorphic, wooden detailed panels like we've done previously in, say, the Shogun games, we wanted to take a much more minimalist approach." The base is "lots of flowing black ink you can see in the UI, with this paper just dipped in the ink", with character illustration and a painterly effect layered on top — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/).
- "We moved away from the skeumorphic panels of the UI to something much more freeform", using "flowing ink that falls into the visuals of the 3D space"; the art is "inspired by a modern painter painting these historical landscapes… so it doesn't look like parchment" — [TheSixthAxis interview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/08/16/interview-exploring-ancient-china-in-total-war-three-kingdoms/).
- The UI is "arranged around lovely flowing ink effects"; Creative Assembly spent weeks mixing ink at different consistencies and filming how it flowed to build custom shaders. The Reforms tech tree is "depicted as a literal tree" with cherry blossoms bursting on researched nodes — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/total-war-three-kingdoms/total-war-three-kingdoms-features).
- Wu Xing colour coding: Earth, Metal, Water, Wood, Fire map to yellow, purple, blue, green and red — [GamesBeat review](https://gamesbeat.com/total-war-three-kingdoms-review/) (S). Generals, units, buildings and tech are colour-coded this way — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/review/1854-total-war-three-kingdoms/) (S). The coding extends beyond the UI ("Instinct is red in the UI", Vanguards wear red, military buildings have red roofs) and serves as "a visual language to introduce new players to the complexities of Total War" — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/total-war-three-kingdoms/total-war-three-kingdoms-features).
- UI audio was designed to match: traditional Chinese instrument recordings combined with abstract sounds to represent the ink effect, avoiding heavy weapon sounds — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-audio-of-i-total-war-three-kingdoms-i-) (S).

**Against the Storm — the documented swing from ornate to minimal and back to a blend (2022)**
- The old UI was "very unique and appealing" with "all the ornaments and elaborate frames", but had become "a patchwork of various ideas and styles" with readability complaints — [Eremite hotfix post](https://eremitegames.com/interface-hotfix-we-hear-you/); [Eremite devlog](https://eremitegames.com/?p=236833).
- The April 2022 Interface Update made it "more minimalistic and toned down" and "met with mixed responses"; the studio's finding was that "the 'handcrafted', warm feeling is what you miss the most from the previous UI" — [Eremite hotfix post](https://eremitegames.com/interface-hotfix-we-hear-you/); [Eremite devlog](https://eremitegames.com/?p=236833).
- The correction: earthy green textured backgrounds, leatherwork ornaments, wider and more decorated window frames, some vibrant colours toned down, building icons zoomed out to fit their frames, and building-window elements better differentiated. The studio wanted "to meet you halfway" "without sacrificing the unique look of the UI" — [Eremite devlog, 26 Apr 2022](https://eremitegames.com/?p=236833).
- A later update explicitly aimed for "the decorative and warm style of the old interface, and the clean, readable structure of the new one" — [IndieDB, Rationing Update](https://www.indiedb.com/news/rationing-update-is-here) (S).
- Small but telling fix: the Cornerstone panel's animated background was moved behind the icon rather than behind the text — [Eremite hotfix post](https://eremitegames.com/interface-hotfix-we-hear-you/).

**Frostpunk 2**
- "Circles are practically sacred in Frostpunk's design language"; the art team used "thick-lined, mechanical wheels" and "worn-out surfaces", with gold as an accent colour — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/09/18/adapting-frostpunk-2s-depth-to-a-gamepad/).
- "Harsh but beautiful industrial visual design": "burnt orange and copper mechanized industrial buildings and parchment paper script"; most of the screen is a quiet blue tone and the reviewer found their eye drifting to the warm parts; event art includes "graffiti details on the drawings that accompany pop-up choices" — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/frostpunk-2-review).
- The interface is "uniquely stylish and fitting Frostpunk 2's icy aesthetics" but "took a bit of getting used to" — [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/frostpunk-2/review) (S).

**Civilization VII — restrained palette read as unfinished**
- Menus that once had "custom art and thematic flourishes" are now "drab grey slabs"; an early note was that it "looks like an iPad version of Civ V" — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/civilization-vii-review/).
- A detailed player critique liked the grey-gold scheme but said windows and the top bar look like "some minimally configured linux distro"; HUD icons have "a conflicting cartoonish style" against the grey interface and are "very flat" next to 3D-textured ones; the publisher's colourful logo breaks the palette — [Steam feedback thread, 6 Feb 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1295660/discussions/2/591762563949163578/).
- Poll context: style-only options ranked low ("More Ornamentation" 16.5%, "Boxiness/Flatness" 13.4%, "Replace the whole look and feel" 12.4%) versus functional ones (minimap 51.5%, city banner 40.2%, readability 35.1%) — [CivFanatics poll](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/which-ui-elements-do-you-think-need-work-the-most.694205/page-2).
- A small colour-discipline fix: "The color of a Memento's icon now aligns with the color of its effect for easier readability" (1.2.2) — [2K notes 1.2.2](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-jun-23-patch-1-2-2/).

**Colour as identity and as label (menu-driven benchmarks)**
- Persona 5 kept its signature red and avoided sub-colours except in HP/MP elements; relevant information is always on the same side as the white area — [Siliconera, CEDEC+KYUSHU 2017 coverage](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=668266) (S). A blue highlight is reserved for the currently selected item against the red/black/white theme — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=7280). Colour makes each Persona game recognisable from a single screenshot — [Siliconera, 31 Jan 2024](https://www.siliconera.com/what-makes-the-ui-and-menus-in-persona-games-so-good/).
- Atlus treated UI as a marketing and identity tool from Persona 3 onward: "The UI has to convey information to the player, but also convey the identity" — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=668266) (S).
- Metaphor: ReFantazio (2024): the team first tried a colourful, stylised direction but it "didn't feel unique enough to stand on its own"; the final menus are lavish but pragmatic — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/jrpg/the-lead-ui-designer-on-metaphor-refantazio-had-never-designed-for-a-game-before-he-just-rolled-up-and-made-some-of-the-best-ui-ive-ever-seen-in-a-jrpg) (S). Director Hashino: "we have unique designs that we make for each and every menu" while trying "to keep things simple, practical, and usable" — [Kotaku, quoting The Verge](https://kotaku.com/metaphor-refantazio-persona-5-menu-ui-hashino-1851667088).
- Balatro: every number uses a fixed palette so colour functions as the label — chips blue `#009dff`, multiplier red `#fe5f55`, money gold `#f0c040`, hands green `#50c878`, planet/upgrade purple `#9b59b6`. A CRT treatment sits on top: 4 px-period scanlines at `rgba(0,0,0,0.15)`, a radial vignette to `rgba(0,0,0,0.4)`, and a faint phosphor text-shadow. These hex values and CSS are the guide author's reconstruction, not shipped code — [Blake Crosley design guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro).

### Inferences
- "Premium" in this genre reads as: clean grid and generous spacing + one tactile material + one metallic accent + semantic colours used only for meaning. Three Kingdoms is the most directly transferable precedent for a Three Kingdoms-period game: paper/ink surfaces, no wood-and-bronze frames, a five-element colour code, and ornament that is motion (ink bleed, blossoms) rather than static filigree.
- Faction colour should be a single accent channel (banner strips, borders, map tint), kept separate from the semantic palette (gain/loss, resource types, element classes). No source states a maximum accent count; the observed pattern is one brand hue plus a selection highlight (Persona 5) or five functional hues (Three Kingdoms, Balatro).
- Dark versus light: sources describe dark or neutral chrome with a warm accent as the dominant strategy-game pattern (Civ VII grey-gold, Frostpunk 2 blue with warm accents, CK3 sombre), while Three Kingdoms and Persona 5 use a light/paper or white field as the information-bearing surface. For three themes: "cinematic dark blue-gold" maps to the dark-chrome pattern; "ink-and-gold" maps to the Three Kingdoms paper pattern (light information surfaces inside dark ink frames); the neon "chrono" theme is closest to Balatro's CRT layer and should keep glow for state (selected, active) only.
- Implementation notes (suggested, not sourced):
  - Themes as CSS custom properties on a root `data-theme` attribute (`--surface`, `--surface-raised`, `--ink`, `--accent-metal`, `--faction`, `--gain`, `--loss`), mapped into Tailwind via `theme.extend.colors` pointing at `var(--…)`.
  - Paper/leather/lacquer grain: an inline SVG `feTurbulence` noise as a `background-image` data URI at 3–6% opacity over a flat colour; no bitmap needed.
  - Panel framing: 9-slice `border-image` from one SVG, or four absolutely positioned SVG corner ornaments plus a 1 px gradient hairline (`linear-gradient(90deg, transparent, var(--accent-metal), transparent)`).
  - Ink edges and ink-bleed reveals: `mask-image` with an ink-blot texture and animated `mask-size` / `mask-position`, or an SVG `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` filter on the panel edge; for hero moments, a small fragment shader on a full-screen quad in the existing three.js renderer with a noise-thresholded alpha.
  - CRT layer for the neon theme: `repeating-linear-gradient` scanlines and a radial vignette as `pointer-events: none` pseudo-elements, per the Balatro reconstruction above; make it a toggle (see accessibility).
  - Keep animated or busy backgrounds behind icons and illustrations, never behind body text (the Against the Storm fix).

### Gaps
- No source states explicit rules for how many accent colours leading strategy UIs use or how faction colours are applied within panels.
- No developer commentary was found on Civilization VI's art direction (cartographic / age-of-exploration styling); only player opinion.
- "Excessive glow and gradients" as a named failure was not directly documented beyond the "bright, glossy" remark about Warhammer III and the forum remark about EU5.
- Game UI Database entries could not be read (HTTP 403), so screenshot-level comparison of panel materials was not possible.

## Typography and iconography: display versus body typefaces, number formatting, icon consistency, minimum sizes, localisation including CJK

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are small or thin text, mixed typefaces without a rule, icons that share a silhouette and hue, low-contrast coloured text on dark surfaces, and CJK fonts treated as an afterthought; the recurring fixes are a single disciplined type system, icons distinguished by shape as well as colour, rounded numbers on the surface, and a proper CJK face. Firaxis replaced Civilization VII's Simplified Chinese font within a month of launch.

### Cited Findings

- On a 4K display "many of the icons look fuzzy and low res"; some reward icons have "faint, white 1 pixel borders"; "clusters of monochromatic icons are not distinguishable enough" — [Steam feedback thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1295660/discussions/2/591762563949163578/).

**CJK and localisation**
- Leading: "Most design guides recommend leading to be between 120% and 145% of the font size"; "Chinese doesn't have much use for kerning"; "Automatic font resizing don't solve problems, it creates problems" — build a formatting hierarchy and fit text with a linguist instead — [Culturalization guide, font issues](https://sites.google.com/view/culturalizationguide/culture-taboos/user-interface/font-issues).
- Noto Sans CJK "covers over 65,000 glyphs for simplified Chinese, traditional Chinese, Japanese, and Korean" (also distributed as Source Han Sans); check licences; have a native reader check how the font style comes across — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-localize-indie-games-for-non-latin-alphabets).
- Because Chinese characters are more complex, guides advise larger font sizes and using icons rather than shrinking type to fit — [Translated.com guide](https://translated.com/resources/mobile-game-china-localization-guide) (S); [Culturalization guide, UX issues](https://sites.google.com/view/culturalizationguide/culture-taboos/user-interface/ux-issues) (S).
- Player reports: Noto Sans CJK Light is "too slim to read" in game UI — [Bohemia feedback tracker](https://feedback.bistudio.com/T128076) (S); mixed CJK fallback fonts produce varying sizes, styles and stroke weights — [Age of Empires forum](https://forums.ageofempires.com/t/east-asian-typeface-mess/66543) (S).

### Inferences
- A workable type system for a bilingual English/Chinese strategy UI: one display face for titles and banners (serif or brush-influenced), one sans body face for everything dense, and a hard rule for which appears where — the Civ VII critique was about the absence of a rule, not about serif fonts as such. Numbers should always be in the body face with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums` so count-ups and columns do not jitter.
- CJK implementation (suggested): set `lang="zh-Hans"` (or `zh-Hant`) on the root so Han-unified glyphs resolve to the right regional forms; font stack such as `"Noto Sans SC", "Source Han Sans SC", "PingFang SC", "Microsoft YaHei", sans-serif` for body and `"Noto Serif SC", "Source Han Serif SC", "Songti SC", serif` for display; use Regular/Medium weights for body (avoid Light); line-height ~1.5–1.7 for Chinese body text against ~1.4 for Latin; no `letter-spacing`, `text-transform: uppercase` or italics on CJK runs (Anno 117's "italics to Roman" option points the same way); `line-break: strict` for punctuation. Subset or lazy-load CJK webfonts because full files are several megabytes.
- Minimum sizes (suggested; no authoritative figure was found): body 14 px at 1080p with nothing below 12 px, CJK body 15–16 px; the UI-scale setting must scale tooltips too (Civ VII's tooltips initially did not).
- Number formatting (suggested): whole numbers on the surface, one decimal only inside breakdowns; signed deltas with colour plus arrow glyph (Civ VII 1.2.5 added green-up/red-down arrows); locale-aware abbreviation (1.2k in English, 1.2万 in Chinese) via `Intl.NumberFormat` with `notation: "compact"`.
- Icons: lucide's uniform stroke weight gives consistency for chrome and actions, but resource and yield icons need distinct silhouettes and fills (custom SVGs), since the Civ VII complaint was precisely same-shape, near-hue icons. Test the resource row in greyscale. Never use saturated red text on dark grey; pair a lighter tint with an icon.

### Gaps
- No authoritative minimum pixel size for CJK UI text was found.
- No developer source documented a display/body typeface pairing for any of the covered games.
- No source discussed number-abbreviation conventions in strategy UIs.

## Tooltips and progressive disclosure: nested/lockable tooltips, inline icons in text, "why is this number what it is" breakdowns

### Takeaway
Nested, lockable tooltips with highlighted concept links and numeric breakdowns have become the genre standard since Crusader Kings III (2020): Victoria 3, Old World, Age of Wonders 4 and Endless Legend 2 all use them, and Civilization VII — criticised at launch for missing tooltips — began retrofitting them in February 2026. They are praised as progressive disclosure but are not a substitute for onboarding, and they need lock modes, forgiving hit areas and a depth limit.

### Cited Findings

**The CK3 model**
- CK3's dev diary ("Tutorials and Tooltips and Encyclopedias") introduced tooltips that themselves have tooltips — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/the-crusader-kings-3-team-is-working-hard-to-make-the-game-easier-to-learn) (S); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/articles/16333/) (S).
- Quoted from the dev diary: "the aim of this is that relevant information can just be a mouse move away" whenever blue highlighted text appears; "There are two different modes for Tooltips in Tooltips, timer lock and action lock", timer lock being the default; the lock timer is set in the settings menu; action lock means "clicking in the middle mouse button to manually lock them in place", which experienced players can switch to so nested tooltips only appear when wanted — [Obsidian forum quoting the diary](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/nested-popovers/573).
- A designer's analysis found the pattern "surprisingly intuitive and easy to use" because it explains a supporting concept in place, but flagged that it is frustrating to "move your cursor 1 pixel outside of the bounds of the element", that keyboard/assistive access is unaddressed, that touch needs a tap model, and suggested "2 or 3 nested tooltips" as a sweet spot — [Philip Ardeljan](https://philip.design/blog/tooltips-in-tooltips).
- CK3 1.5 added a function letting mods show breakdowns of their own script values, matching the base game's number breakdowns — [CK3 Dev Diary 87](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/zh-CN/games/crusader-kings-iii/news/dev-diary-87-royal-modding) (S).

**Adoption and refinements elsewhere**

- Old World lists nested tooltips and undo as headline quality-of-life features — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/597180/Old_World/) (S); tooltips lock with the middle mouse button — [Quarter to Three forum](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/old-world-pka-ten-crowns-from-soren-johnson/134746?page=78) (S). ### Inferences
- A specification that matches the praised implementations: (1) concept terms rendered as visibly distinct inline links (CK3 uses blue); (2) hover opens a tooltip after a short delay; (3) the tooltip locks either after a visible timer or on an explicit action (middle-click or a key), user-selectable; (4) locked tooltips can contain further links, to a depth of about three; (5) numeric values open a breakdown (base, each modifier with sign and source, total); (6) a searchable glossary backs the tooltips (the Endless Legend 2 gap, the Stellaris Databank fix); (7) tooltips clamp to the viewport and sit below modals in z-order (two separate Civ VII fixes).
- Implementation (suggested): a `<Term id="…">` React component that renders an underlined span and registers with a tooltip stack; positioning via Floating UI (`flip`, `shift`, `safePolygon` for the "moving toward the nested tooltip" corridor that Victoria 3 had to fix); the lock timer shown as an SVG ring or a border progress using `stroke-dashoffset` animated linearly over the lock duration; `Escape` pops one level; focusable via keyboard with `aria-describedby`; tap-to-open on touch. Hover delay roughly 300–500 ms and lock timer roughly 0.8–1.5 s are reasonable starting values, but these are my estimates — no source gave CK3's or Civ VII's actual figures.
- Inline icons in text: 1em inline SVGs with `vertical-align: -0.125em`, coloured by semantic token, always followed by the number so the icon is never the only carrier of meaning (the EU5 remark about undecipherable icon-only tooltips).
- Keep first-level tooltips compact; the Civ VII "five to six times larger for the same information" complaint and Firaxis's "more concise" refresh both point to density inside tooltips being valued.

### Gaps
- CK3's default hover delay and lock-timer values were not found; the original Dev Diary 16 could not be opened (Paradox forum pages failed to load).
- Victoria 3 Dev Diary 29 (UX pillars) could not be opened (HTTP 500/404); only a search summary was seen.
- No source described inline-icon typesetting conventions in any covered game.

## Motion design: transitions, count-ups, fly-outs, banners, event staging, hover/press states, map-to-screen transitions; durations and easing; responsive versus slow

### Takeaway
Published game-specific timing data is scarce; the usable numbers come from general UI motion guidance (most transitions 100–400 ms, ease-out on entry, shorter exits, shorter still for frequent actions) and from analyses of Balatro, whose feedback is layered, sequenced and scaled to the size of the event. Evidence from strategy games is mostly about speed and interruption — Firaxis sped up Civilization VII's notifications, Paradox made map-mode fades configurable — and about motion as theme (Three Kingdoms' ink, Persona's posed menus), with motion-sickness and overstimulation as the documented downside.

### Cited Findings

**General timing and easing references**
- Most UI animations should fall within 100–400 ms; simple feedback (toggles) about 100 ms; substantial changes such as a modal 200–300 ms; large movements up to 400 ms; above 500 ms feels sluggish; pop-ups about 300 ms to appear and 200–250 ms to disappear. Use ease-out for entering elements, ease-in for exiting, avoid linear. "It is far more common for animations to be too long than too short." The more often an animation is seen, the shorter and subtler it should be. Respect "reduce motion" — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/).
- Ease-out and ease-in around 200–500 ms; bounce/elastic 800–1200 ms so they can settle, and only where they suit the product's personality; a quintic ease-out for very quick motion — [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/choosing-the-right-easing).
- Microsoft Fluent control durations: 250 ms normal, 167 ms fast, 83 ms faster. Entering: "Fast Out, Slow In" `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0, 1)`, which feels responsive "even if it's preceded by a moment of unresponsiveness". Exiting: "Slow Out, Fast In" `cubic-bezier(1, 0, 1, 1)` — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/motion/timing-and-easing).
- One design system's scale: 100 ms micro-interactions, 200 ms hover, 300 ms dropdowns, 400 ms modals and large panels; ease-out default, linear for colour/opacity; transitions disabled or shortened under reduced motion — [Toyota design system](https://dx-playbook.toyota-europe.com/cds/foundations/animations/) (S).

**Juice and game-feel craft references**
- "Juice it or lose it" (Jonasson and Purho, 2012) demonstrates colour, tweening/easing of position, rotation and scale, random delays, trails, screen shake and character-like touches on a Breakout clone — [course handout](https://mywebspace.quinnipiac.edu/jbwarren/archive/2018-410/class-04.html) (S).
- Linear interpolation gives "tasteless results"; use ease-out for UI entrances; "treat UI elements like characters, easing them in and out"; hold key moments for a few frames; give every interaction audio feedback. Warnings: match juice to tone, and "the more common an action, the simpler the juice" — [GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/squeezing-more-juice-out-of-your-game-design).
- An academic survey defines juicing as amplification — adding feedback that emphasises player actions to communicate event importance — and lists elements including screen shake, colour flashing, one-shot particles, hit stop, audio feedback, trails, "fluid interfaces" (continuity between UI states, keeping elements from the previous view) and idle animations — [Pichlmair and Johansen, arXiv 2011.09201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09201).
- One game-feel guide recommends screen shake of 0.1–0.3 s maximum, strongest at the start and scaled to the impact, and warns of motion sickness — [SlashSkill guide](https://www.slashskill.com/game-feel-and-juice-the-complete-guide-to-making-your-game-satisfying/) (S; attribution to this specific guide not verified).

**Balatro (2024) — the reference for menu-driven feedback**
- Five feedback layers fire per scoring event: card animation, chip counter, screen effects, particles and audio. Cards slide to the scoring area and flip with a spring bounce and ±3° random rotation; jokers trigger left to right, each pulsing as the running total updates; each scored card plays a rising pitch; screen-shake intensity scales with score magnitude; the background pulses toward the hand's colour — [Blake Crosley guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro).
- Reconstructed values (the guide's own CSS approximations, not shipped code): digit roll `transition: transform 0.4s cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` with 50 ms stagger per digit; card hover `0.15s ease-out` to `translateY(-12px) scale(1.05)` with a deeper shadow; selected `translateY(-24px) scale(1.08)` with a warm glow; foil shimmer on a 3 s ease-in-out loop; screen shake small 0.2 s ±2 px, medium 0.3 s ±4 px, large 0.5 s ±8 px with ±1° rotation, at thresholds of 1,000 and 10,000 points — [Blake Crosley guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro).
- Reviewers describe "constant, trippy motion", a UI that "slightly wiggles", shop cards that "gently sway as if suspended", chips landing with a click, each Joker effect raising the pitch and speed of the sequence, and digits that appear to ignite as the tally grows — [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/balatro-hones-the-art-of-making-numbers-go-up). Another writer "can't imagine" the game without its screen shake — [Error and Experimentation](https://errorandexp.substack.com/p/unpacking-balatros-addicting-game).
- A Unity recreation of the card movement and shaders (using the DOTween tweening library) is public — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/balatro-s-card-movements-shaders-recreated-in-unity); [GitHub mixandjam/balatro-feel](https://github.com/mixandjam/balatro-feel).
- Downside: players report motion sickness and eye strain, turn off the CRT effect and set screen shake to zero, and note the background still moves — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/4293690852338460469) (S); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/4201364375109001844) (S).

### Inferences
- What makes strategy-UI motion feel responsive rather than slow, synthesised from the above: the state change happens on input and the animation follows it; entries use a steep ease-out so most movement occurs in the first third; exits are faster than entries; nothing the player triggers dozens of times per session exceeds about 200 ms; anything longer (banners, event staging, score sequences) is skippable or speed-adjustable; and celebratory effects are reserved for rare events and scaled to magnitude (Balatro's tiered shake).
- Suggested motion tokens for a React/Tailwind UI (derived from the NN/g, web.dev and Fluent ranges; not game-measured):

| Interaction | Duration | Easing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Press / active state | 80–100 ms | linear or ease-out | `scale(0.97)` plus darker fill; release springs back 120 ms |
| Hover highlight | 120–170 ms | ease-out | colour, border, 1–2 px lift; no layout shift |
| Tooltip appear / disappear | 100–150 ms / 80 ms | ease-out / ease-in | opacity plus 4 px translate |
| Side panel or sheet open / close | 220–280 ms / 160–200 ms | `cubic-bezier(0,0,0,1)` / `cubic-bezier(1,0,1,1)` | transform and opacity only |
| Modal open / close | 250–300 ms / 200 ms | ease-out / ease-in | scale 0.96→1 plus fade; scrim fades 200 ms |
| Tab or sub-view switch | 150–200 ms | ease-out | cross-fade with 8 px slide; keep shared header static ("fluid interface") |
| Resource count-up | 400–600 ms | ease-out | cap total time regardless of delta |
| Gain/loss fly-out | 600–900 ms | ease-out move, ease-in fade | float 20–32 px then fade |
| Turn / season banner | 250 ms in, 700–1000 ms hold, 200 ms out | ease-out / ease-in | click or key skips; shorten on repeat |
| Celebration pop (victory, recruit) | 400–500 ms | `cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1)` overshoot | rare events only |
| Screen shake | 200 / 300 / 500 ms at 2 / 4 / 8 px | ease-out | tiered by magnitude; user slider; off in reduced motion |

- Technique notes:
  - Count-up: a `requestAnimationFrame` loop interpolating with an ease-out over a fixed duration, rendered with tabular figures; for a showier variant, per-digit columns translated in Y with the 50 ms stagger from the Balatro reconstruction.
  - Fly-outs: an absolutely positioned span spawned at the source, animated with the Web Animations API (`translateY(-28px)`, opacity 1→0) and removed on `finish`; or a FLIP animation that flies an icon from the map/panel to the top-bar counter, then triggers the counter's count-up and a 150 ms scale pulse — this links cause to effect the way Balatro's score trail does.
  - Staggered list entry: 30–50 ms delay per row via a CSS variable (`animation-delay: calc(var(--i) * 40ms)`), capped at about 8 rows.
  - Ink reveal for the ink-and-gold theme: animate `mask-size` or a threshold uniform on a noise-textured alpha mask over 400–600 ms; brush-stroke underline via SVG `stroke-dashoffset`.
  - Blossom/particle bursts on completion (the Three Kingdoms tech-tree idea): a small 2D canvas overlay with 20–40 sprites, 600–900 ms lifetime; no need for the three.js scene.
  - Map-to-screen transitions: ease the three.js camera (dolly or tilt 400–600 ms, ease-in-out) while the panel wipes in, and blur/dim the canvas with CSS `filter` or `backdrop-filter` on the overlay rather than a post-processing pass; keep it interruptible.
  - Card tilt/sway (hero cards, recruitment): pointer-driven `rotateX/rotateY` of ±6–8° with `transform-style: preserve-3d`, a slow sine idle sway, and a moving gradient for foil; all transform-only.
  - Performance: animate only `transform` and `opacity`, add `will-change` just before animating, and never animate layout over the WebGL canvas; Civ VII's hover-induced frame drops and "slow and glitchy" verdict show that sluggish UI is read as bad design.
  - Global controls: one `--motion-scale` CSS variable multiplying all durations, set to 0 (or near 0) under `prefers-reduced-motion` and exposed as an "animation speed" setting.

### Gaps

## Event and story presentation: illustrated event cards, choices, advisor characters, chronicle/log presentation

### Takeaway
The best-received event presentation gives characters and illustration real space, keeps text short, and shows consequences in the same visual language as the rest of the UI; the documented failure modes are event windows that are too small for their cast, a jarring drop from flavour prose to raw numbers, and other pop-ups landing on top of a choice.

### Cited Findings
- Crusader Kings III: the new event window had the biggest impact on how characters are visually presented — [CK3 Dev Diary 22](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/ck3-dev-diary-22-a-medieval-tapestry.1377711/page-7) (S). ### Inferences
- Event card recipe for the target game: a wide illustration band (or the two parties' portraits facing each other), a short title in the display face, two to four sentences of body text, then choices as full-width buttons whose effects are listed with the same inline icons and coloured deltas used elsewhere, each term hoverable for a nested tooltip. This addresses the Old World "flavour versus soulless numbers" gap by styling consequences as part of the card rather than a separate system readout.
- Staging (suggested values): scrim fade 200 ms; card scales 0.96→1 with fade over 250–300 ms; title, body and choices stagger in at 60–80 ms intervals; choices become clickable immediately (never gate input on animation); a one-shot audio cue per event category, following Three Kingdoms' and Frostpunk 2's use of sound to brand UI moments.
- Never stack: route other notifications to the feed while an event or battle modal is open, and release them afterwards (the Endless Legend 2 lesson). Pause or slow the simulation while a decision is pending (Frostpunk 2's option).
- Chronicle/log (extrapolated; no direct source): present as a dated feed grouped by year or season with category icons and faction-colour markers, filterable by type, with the mute and importance controls that Victoria 3 and EU5 had to add after launch; major entries can reuse the event illustration as a thumbnail; an end-of-war or end-of-era summary screen with totals (the EU5 addition) gives the log a payoff.
- Advisor presentation: a named advisor portrait attached to recommendations and warnings (Civ VII marks recommended items and adds advisor "watch-out" notifications) gives a diegetic voice to system hints.

- Details of CK3's event-window layout (backgrounds, animated characters, option tooltips) come from search summaries; the dev diaries could not be opened.
- Age of Wonders 4, Songs of Conquest and Humankind event presentation were not covered by any source read.

## Onboarding visuals: highlights, coach marks, advisor prompts

### Takeaway
Evidence favours short, contextual teaching over long sequential tutorials: Paradox cut CK3's 67-box tutorial to about a third and moved teaching into events and progressively revealed panels, and Firaxis's Civilization VII repairs lean on on-map highlights, recommended-item markers and previews rather than more text. Tooltips help but do not replace onboarding.

### Cited Findings
- CK3 tutorial refresh: the old tutorial had 67 sequential text boxes; the team kept roughly one-third as a baseline and agreed "the new tutorial should be faster to complete than the old one". Telemetry showed that finishing the tutorial "wasn't a major determining factor" in whether players kept playing. A softer phase then uses events as a narrative hook, and the character panel reveals information gradually. Tutorial windows that did not resize to their text "made even short text feel cluttered" and were fixed. Playtesters were colleagues from other teams to counter the "curse of knowledge"; the old tutorial remains for telemetry comparison — [Game Developer deep dive](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/deep-dive-refreshing-the-crusader-kings-iii-tutorial-mode-through-optimized-ux).
- Old World: the tutorial is a series of pop-up messages ("the sorry excuse for a tutorial"); without one, the many tooltips are hard to parse — [Cultured Vultures](https://culturedvultures.com/old-world-pc-review/). The tutorial tip explaining tooltip locking disappears and cannot be found again — [Quarter to Three](https://www.quartertothree.com/fp/2021/07/01/bringing-new-eyes-to-old-world/) (S).
- Age of Wonders 4: tutorial panels plus tooltips make it easy to learn — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/age-of-wonders-4-review) (S); it also has a codex and a hint system — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/age-of-wonders-4-review) (S).
- Anno 117: "info corners" are small contextual hints explaining core concepts; infotips can be pinned — [Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/2FfSSEUp1jtg9isC9NxowP/anno-117-pax-romana-accessibility-spotlight).
- Civilization VII's post-launch guidance additions: advisor-recommendation tooltips only on recommended items (1.1.0); additional "Watch-out / Advisor notifications" for Treasure Fleets (1.1.1); tiles highlighted when a Great Person is selected, and research queuing by clicking a locked tech (1.2.0); tutorials for Legend unlocks and migrants, adjacency arrows shown consistently (1.2.1); loading-screen Tips & Hints (1.2.2); featured placement tiles, green yield numbers on the best tiles, and up/down arrows for gains and losses (1.2.5) — [1.1.0](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-mar-04-patch-1-1-0/); [1.1.1](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-mar-25-patch-1-1-1/); [1.2.0](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-apr-22-patch-1-2-0/); [1.2.1](https://civilization.2k.com/en-GB/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-may-27-patch-1-2-1/); [1.2.2](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-jun-23-patch-1-2-2/); [1.2.5](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-sep-30-patch-1-2-5/).
- Civilization VII at launch failed to explain borrowed systems such as towns: "it fails to properly explain it" — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/civilization-vii-review/).
- EU5: the tutorial is better than earlier Paradox titles but "still leaves some things unexplained" — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/europa-universalis-5-review); a tutorial and mission tree exist for guidance — [Tobold](https://tobolds.blogspot.com/2025/11/europa-universalis-v-first-impressions.html).
- Total War: Three Kingdoms' element colours act as "a visual language to introduce new players" — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/total-war-three-kingdoms/total-war-three-kingdoms-features).
- Balatro's left-to-right scoring sequence is argued to replace a tutorial by showing each rule firing in order (a design claim by the guide's author) — [Blake Crosley guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro).
- Endless Legend 2's tutorial brings players gradually into the mechanics — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/reviews/endless-legends-2-review-the-best-4x-game-money-can-buy) (S). Slay the Spire 2 offers an optional tutorial — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2004704).

### Inferences
- Onboarding pattern to adopt: a short guided first few turns (a handful of steps, one idea each), then contextual hints that fire the first time a system becomes relevant, delivered through the advisor character; every hint re-readable from a help/glossary screen (the Old World vanishing-tip complaint).
- Visual guidance on the map and in lists (highlighted valid targets, "recommended" badges, best-option markers, before/after arrows) is the form Firaxis chose to repair Civ VII, and it doubles as expert-speed UI.
- Implementation (suggested): a coach mark as a spotlight cut-out — a fixed full-screen element with a transparent hole made by a very large `box-shadow` spread or an SVG mask — plus a small callout positioned with Floating UI; a soft pulsing ring (`@keyframes` scaling 1→1.15 with opacity fade over 1.2–1.6 s, two or three cycles then stop) to draw the eye without permanent motion; text containers that size to content (the CK3 fix); respect reduced motion by replacing the pulse with a static outline.
- For the three.js map, highlight valid targets with an emissive outline or a ground decal rather than DOM overlays so highlights track the camera.

### Gaps
- No source described the specific visual treatment of highlights or coach marks (shapes, colours, animation) in any covered game.
- Frostpunk 2, Against the Storm, Manor Lords and Stellaris onboarding visuals were not covered by sources read.
- No quantitative retention or comprehension data beyond the CK3 statement was found.

## Accessibility and options: UI scaling, colour-blind modes, reduced motion

### Takeaway
Current releases are expected to ship UI and text scaling that actually reaches every element, colour-blind options, notification controls and toggles for intense effects; reviews now list their absence or weakness as a con. Reduced-motion and overstimulation controls are the weak spot even in the most praised stylised UIs.

- Notification verbosity is a user setting in Victoria 3 (per-message display and auto-pause) and EU5 (mute and restore alerts) — [Paradox Dev Diary 74](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-74-ux-improvements); [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/news/europa-universalis-5-rossbach-update-1-1-hidden-alerts-army-navy-templates-quality-of-life-changes).
- Balatro: screen shake can be disabled — [Family Gaming Database](https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Balatro) (S); players with motion sickness turn off CRT and shake and still find the moving background a problem — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/4293690852338460469) (S). One player reports settings for a static background, game speed and higher card contrast (single unverified report) — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/4201364375109001844) (S).
- Slay the Spire 2 includes an option to disable the more problematic visual effects — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2004704).
- Persona/Metaphor's UI designer on players who find the menus overstimulating: "That is something we understand we'll need to work on and provide in the future" — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/rpg/persona-and-metaphor-refantazios-ui-designer-is-open-to-accessibility-options-for-players-who-find-the-stylish-menus-overstimulating-that-is-something-we-understand-well-need-to-work-on-and-provide-in-the-future) (headline only).
- General guidance: respect "reduce motion"; avoid flashing and parallax, which can trigger seizures, dizziness or migraines — [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/).

### Inferences
- A baseline options set for the target game, matching what 2025–2026 reviews treat as expected: UI scale (about 80–150%), independent text size, reduced motion, screen-shake intensity, background/CRT effects toggle (important for the neon theme), animation speed, tooltip mode and delay, notification verbosity per category, colour-blind-safe palette, high-contrast mode, and a sans-only font option.
- Implementation (suggested): build all spacing and type on `rem` and set `html { font-size: calc(16px * var(--ui-scale)) }` so one variable scales panels, icons and tooltips together (avoiding the Civ VII partial-scaling failure); `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` sets the global motion multiplier to near zero and disables shake, parallax, idle sway and looping shimmer, with an in-game override; colour-blind support primarily through redundant encoding (icon shape, arrows, patterns on faction areas) plus alternative faction palettes swapped via CSS variables; a high-contrast theme as a fourth `data-theme`; check body text against WCAG 4.5:1 on every theme, especially gold-on-dark and neon-on-dark.
- Because the product has three themes, each theme multiplies the contrast-testing burden; the Civ VII red-on-dark-grey complaints suggest verifying semantic colours (loss, unaffordable, hostile) separately per theme.

### Gaps
- Accessibility option lists for Age of Wonders 4, Stellaris, Crusader Kings III, Total War, Against the Storm and Manor Lords were not found.
- No verified, official list of Balatro's motion and contrast settings was found; only player reports.
- No strategy game with a documented dedicated "reduced motion" toggle was identified in the sources read.

## Cautionary lessons: the Civilization VII launch UI, mobile-style clutter, glow and gradients, low-contrast text, modal overload

### Takeaway
Civilization VII (released 11 February 2025) is the clearest recent case of a visually restrained UI being judged "unfinished": the criticism was about missing information, weak hierarchy, inconsistent type and icons, poor scaling, lag and unpolished alignment far more than about a lack of ornament, and Firaxis spent at least twelve months patching it — polish first, then information on the surface, then previews, then nested tooltips. Other titles add complementary warnings: do not strip all warmth (Against the Storm), do not let one hue flood the UI (Warhammer III), do not let style beat legibility (Persona 5), do not let motion run unchecked (Balatro), and do not let notifications and modals pile up (Humankind, Endless Legend 2, EU5).

### Inferences
- The order of Firaxis's repairs is itself a priority list for any visual upgrade: (1) alignment, clipping, scaling and CJK font correctness; (2) contrast and weight on the things players scan most (health bars, banners, flags, selection); (3) surface numbers and timers that were buried; (4) previews of consequences at decision points; (5) nested tooltips and a refreshed tooltip style; (6) power tools (search, lists, rulers). A cosmetic re-skin that skips steps 1–3 would repeat the launch mistake.
- "Mobile-style" as used by critics refers to oversized padding with little information, flat untextured slabs, monochrome icon clusters and console-first navigation — not to any specific framework. For a Tailwind UI the risk is the default look: uniform rounded grey cards with generous padding. Counter with tighter information rows, a material layer, and distinct icon silhouettes.
- The Against the Storm case warns the other way: when upgrading visuals across three themes, keep whatever players already find characterful and test readability changes with users before shipping; the studio's fix was texture plus structure, not a return to the old clutter.
- Glow, gradient and gloss should be treated as state signals (selected, active, legendary) rather than surface decoration; the only direct evidence is the Warhammer III "bright, glossy" remark and the Balatro approach of reserving glow for hover/selected and special cards, so this is a judgement rather than a documented consensus.
- A practical acceptance checklist drawn from the complaints: no text below the minimum size at 1080p; every number hoverable for a breakdown; no red-on-dark body text; every icon distinguishable in greyscale; no modal can open over another modal with choices; every animation over 300 ms skippable; UI scale affects every element; Chinese text checked by a native reader on all three themes.
