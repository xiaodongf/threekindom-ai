# Campaign / Strategic Map Visuals in State-of-the-Art Strategy Games (2019–2026)

Reading notes for the report writer:
- Release years are given in brackets after each game. Where no source is linked for a year it is general knowledge, not a researched fact.
- "(snippet)" means the fact came from a search-engine extract of the page, not a full read. Paradox forum pages would not load in the fetch tool, so most Paradox dev-diary facts are snippet-level unless a mirror (devtrackers.gg, paradoxinteractive.com, the wikis) was readable.
- The "Browser approximation" bullets under Inferences are my own engineering judgement for a React + three.js (WebGL) game with AI-painted 2D art and procedural geometry. They are not sourced claims.
- Games covered: Civilization VII [2025], Europa Universalis V [2025], Crusader Kings III [2020; All Under Heaven expansion Oct 2025], Victoria 3 [2022], Total War: Three Kingdoms [2019], Total War: Pharaoh [2023; Dynasties map expansion 2024], Total War: Warhammer III [2022], Humankind [2021], Ara: History Untold [2024], Old World [2021], Age of Wonders 4 [2023], Manor Lords [early access 2024], Romance of the Three Kingdoms XIV [2020], Romance of the Three Kingdoms 8 Remake [2024], Nobunaga's Ambition: Awakening [2022 Japan / 2023 West], Songs of Conquest [early access 2022, 1.0 2024], Endless Legend 2 [early access Sept 2025].

## 1. Terrain and world rendering: how are terrain, mountains, rivers, water, forests, coastlines, lighting, clouds, seasons and weather rendered?

### Takeaway
The best-looking maps are not the most realistic ones: they are a stylised heightmap with exaggerated relief, a small set of tiling materials blended by masks, a strong painterly palette, and two or three cheap atmospheric layers (drifting cloud shadows, distance fog / depth of field, a day-night grade). Nearly all of that is shader work rather than art-team work, which makes it the most transferable category for a browser game.

### Cited Findings

**Art direction: diorama, tabletop, painting**
- Civilization VII [2025]: art director Jason Johnson's stated style is "readable realism", combining Civ VI's bright palette with Civ V's realism. His inspirations were museum dioramas and "some of the best game boards ever made and painted"; executive producer Dennis Shirk said Johnson "loves that he's basically creating the best looking tabletop game on the planet", and the environment artists play Warhammer. — [TechRadar interview](https://techradar.com/gaming/consoles-pc/civilization-7-interview-preview-gamescom-art-direction)
- Civilization VII: senior lead environment artist Matthew Quickel said the team studied tabletop dioramas because they convey a lot of information with limited detail; building surfaces get texture and grit for close-ups while details such as brick rows are reduced so structures stay readable from afar. — [Hooked Gamers interview](https://hookedgamers.com/features/2024/10/23/the_art_of_civilization_vii_-_an_interview.html)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms [2019]: senior designer Leif Walter said the art style is "very much inspired by a modern painter painting these historical landscapes" so that it does not look like parchment. — [TheSixthAxis interview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=320428)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms: art director Pawel Wojs described a "very modern approach, almost like looking at the history through a modern lens", an ethereal, painterly look and a deliberately colourful palette. He called the map the most visually diverse environment in a historical Total War, singling out subtropical karst mountains with tight paths between rivers. — [GamesBeat interview](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms reviews: the map and battlefields are "alive with gorgeous, painterly detail". — [Wireframe review](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/total-war-three-kingdoms-review-a-masterful-reinvigoration)
- Age of Wonders 4 [2023]: the world-map look was inspired by 1800s Romanticism paintings, giving a gritty but vibrant surface in which Ancient Wonders and mountains have their scale emphasised by moving pools of light (snippet). — [AoW4 Dev Diary 11 forum thread](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/dev-diary-11-the-art-of-age-of-wonders-4.1576391/page-2)
- Age of Wonders 4 was made by about nine artists (1 animator, 2 environment, 2 character, 3 2D, 1 tech artist, 1 particle-effects artist) over 3.5 years. — [AoW4 Dev Diary 11](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-11)
- Victoria 3 [2022]: the art director's rule for the map is that it should look "interesting yet soothing enough not to compete in complexity with the interface", with visual hierarchy managed by balancing the complexity and scale of assets. — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- Humankind [2021]: technical art director Mathias Gregoire's goal was terrain that is plausible and stylised rather than strictly realistic (snippet). — [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/humankind-terrain-second-developer-diary)
- Endless Legend 2 [early access Sept 2025]: PC Gamer (85) called its art "one of the most pleasing, coherent aesthetics in the entire strategy game space". — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/strategy/endless-legend-2-review/)

**Heightmaps, materials, mountains (documented technique)**
- Crusader Kings III, All Under Heaven update [Oct 2025]: the team adopted Gaea, which generates heightmaps and material masks by simulating erosion, replacing months of manual painting. The original heightmap was kept as a base because map shape affects gameplay. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- CK3: terrain scale is deliberately stylised, "since real-world scale would look like noise when zoomed in". The old mountains used a tiling normal map that created visual noise in dense ranges. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- CK3 pipeline: the world is cut into tiles, each tile is processed through Gaea, and outputs are stitched into full-map textures. The world is split into 7 biomes, each with its own tiling textures, material masks and reference-derived colour palette. Iteration is done on one prototype tile because a full world build takes up to 3 hours. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- Europa Universalis V [2025] map data, per the modding wiki (marked as verified for version 1.1): a location-ID map of 16384 x 8192 px with one unique colour per location; a base world heightmap made of 16 pieces of 8192 x 4096 px in 16-bit greyscale; sea level at 8.34% of the height range; terrain materials spread across 16 mask channels (coastline types, rivers and lakes, border and water transitions, vegetation and climate transitions, variation levels). — [EU5 wiki, Map modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modding)
- EU5 mountains and volcanoes are five base decals (each with its own mask, bitmask and heightmap) that are stamped onto the heightmap hundreds of times. — [EU5 wiki, Map modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modding)
- EU5 gives every location three terrain attributes (climate, topography, vegetation), producing 672 terrain combinations against 16 terrain types in EU4 (snippet). — [Tinto Maps special edition](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/tinto-maps-special-edition-29th-of-september-2025-the-world-v-2.1860982/)
- Songs of Conquest [1.0 2024]: ground and cliffs are 3D meshes generated semi-procedurally in chunks with marching squares. Splat-map data is stored in mesh vertex colours, and a shader with a texture array blends grass, dirt, cliff and seabed. Textures are only 128 x 128 px, about 16 per faction, with transitions done by alpha channels plus noise. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Humankind: each tile has an elevation and the terrain is fully three-dimensional; flatlands, hills and mountains are meant to read as clearly distinct shapes, with "careful blending between terrain elements". — [Amplitude Feature Focus 02](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitudestudios/humankind/blogs/722-humankind-feature-focus-02-reimagining-terrain?page=0)

**Rivers, water, coastlines**
- EU5 rivers are authored as a colour-coded image: special colours for source, tributary and split, and 13 shades for river width. Water uses a separate 8192 x 4096 colour map. — [EU5 wiki, Map modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modding)
- Victoria 3: shoreline waves are done with "smart shader work" specifically so they do not cost much performance. — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- CK3 2025 update: water gained more varied detail and colour by region, improved shoreline waves and a more realistic wave scale. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- Age of Wonders 4: rivers always generate from the centre of a mountain sector and flow down to the ocean (snippet). — [AoW4 Dev Diary 16](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-16)
- Endless Legend 2: the "Tidefall" mechanic lowers the water level at set points so new land and paths appear; PCGamesN's verdict on the visuals was "not really breaking new ground, it's just very pretty". — [PCGamesN impressions](https://www.pcgamesn.com/endless-legend-2/out-now-early-access-impressions)

**Forests and vegetation**
- EU5 vegetation is placed by generators driven by greyscale masks (white allows spawning), with per-layer maximum density, density curve and scale curve. — [EU5 wiki, Map modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modding)
- Songs of Conquest: trees, buildings and characters are 2D billboards on the 3D ground; tens of thousands can be on screen, most animated, using per-faction texture atlases to limit draw calls and 8-frame animations at one shared framerate. A custom shader approximates billboard rotation from an origin position baked into the mesh. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)

**Clouds, cloud shadows, fog, depth of field, lighting, day-night**
- CK3 2025 update: cloud shadows now drift across the landscape (players can switch them off); terrain and map objects have separately adjustable lighting, the map is generally brighter and interactable objects stand out more. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- Songs of Conquest: lighting is a real-time lightmap applied by top-down projection, built from "lighting sprites" rendered by a separate camera. It carries light, darkness and colour, which is how cloud shadows are done. Post-processing is bloom, vignette, colour grading and depth of field on zoom-in; colour grading plus light and ambient settings produce day and night. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms: detail is layered so more appears as the player zooms in; at distance the team "clear[s] that up a bit by tweaking the way things appear at a distance", and new fog and depth-of-field effects add subtle blur at distance to tie the view together. A day-night cycle runs continuously on the campaign map; night is blue-tinted and kept clear enough to play, with lanterns and fires as contrast. — [GamesBeat interview](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms: a preview said the map "feels warmer thanks to new lighting tweaks - most obvious in the day/night cycle" (snippet). — [PC Gamer preview](https://pcgamer.com/uk/total-war-three-kingdoms-is-ambitious-beautiful-and-confusing)
- Victoria 3: clouds add depth to the map and double as the fog of war. — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)

**Seasons, weather and world-state grading**
- Victoria 3: atmospheric weather effects on the map include rain, sandstorms and snowstorms. — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- EU5 has dedicated Winter and Current Weather map modes that colour locations by how much winter or which weather system they are experiencing; the default map mode is Terrain (snippet). — [EU5 wiki, Map modes](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modes)
- Manor Lords [early access 2024]: the environment changes with the seasons — heavy spring rain, autumn colour, winter snowfall. — [Windows Central review](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/manor-lords-review)
- Total War: Pharaoh [2023]: the whole map's look is tied to world state. "If civilization is close to collapse, the lights will literally go out and leave the map colorless" (snippet); another preview says the entire map is cast in fog and dark colours as key cities fall (snippet). — [SI review](https://videogames.si.com/reviews/total-war-pharaoh); [SI gameplay reveal](https://videogames.si.com/news/total-war-pharaoh-gameplay-reveal)

### Inferences
- The common recipe across CK3, EU5 and Songs of Conquest is: one heightmap, a handful of tiling materials, mask textures that say where each material goes, and stamped or instanced "hero" features (mountains, forests). None of it depends on high-resolution unique art; Songs of Conquest ships with 128 px terrain textures.
- CK3's two explicit lessons are directly relevant to a map of China: exaggerate and simplify relief (real scale reads as noise), and avoid a single tiling normal map over mountain ranges.
- Atmosphere is where the "wow" comes from for the least cost: AoW4's moving pools of light, CK3's and Songs of Conquest's cloud shadows, and Three Kingdoms' distance fog plus depth of field are all a scrolling texture or a post-process pass.
- Pharaoh's world-state colour grade is a strong narrative device for a Three Kingdoms game (for example a colder, greyer grade as the Han collapses, warming as a faction nears unification).
- Browser approximation, terrain: a displaced plane (or chunked planes) with a custom shader that blends 4–8 tiling painted textures by an RGBA mask; a public elevation dataset of China, vertically exaggerated and smoothed, as the heightmap; an AI-painted full-map colour image as a low-frequency "macro" tint over the tiling detail. Feasible solo.
- Browser approximation, erosion masks: run an erosion tool (Gaea or an open-source hydraulic-erosion script) once offline and export heightmap, flow and slope masks as PNGs. One-time authoring, no art team needed.
- Browser approximation, mountains and forests: instanced low-poly mountain meshes or heightmap stamps; trees as `InstancedMesh` clumps or crossed billboards scattered by a density mask, with wind sway in the vertex shader. Tens of thousands of instances are realistic in WebGL.
- Browser approximation, cloud shadows and pools of light: sample a tiling noise texture in world-space XZ, scroll it slowly, multiply it into terrain and object lighting. A few lines of shader; should be a settings toggle as in CK3.
- Browser approximation, water and coast: precompute a distance-to-coast texture from the land mask and animate foam bands with a sine of distance minus time; tint water with a painted colour map; scroll one or two normal maps. Rivers as flat spline ribbons with scrolling UVs and width tapering from source to mouth.
- Browser approximation, fog, tilt-shift and day-night: distance fog in the shader plus a tilt-shift or depth-of-field post pass; day-night as an interpolated light colour, a colour-grade lookup and emissive lantern sprites at cities. All feasible, but post passes cost frame rate on integrated GPUs, so make them optional.
- Browser approximation, seasons: a single "season" uniform that blends snow in by altitude and latitude, shifts foliage tint and switches a screen-space rain or snow particle layer. Feasible.
- Needs a AAA team (or a large asset library): Manor Lords-style photogrammetry detail at street level; unique hand-sculpted landmarks across a whole world map (Pharaoh's Mount Olympus, Meteora and so on).

## 2. Zoom levels: how do games transition between a close 3D view and a far paper / parchment / political map?

### Takeaway
Paradox's three-tier zoom (terrain detail → coloured realm layer → illustrated paper map) is the reference implementation and the most praised, and CK3's 2025 update shows where it is heading: a culturally themed, hand-illustrated paper map (a Shan Shui-style one for East Asia) with smooth blending. The recurring complaint is being forced to zoom to change information layer, so zoom-linked styles should be paired with explicit map-mode buttons.

### Cited Findings
- CK3 [2020]: "We represent the map on three different zoom levels. When zoomed far out, the map will turn into an actual paper map." The middle tier is the Realms layer (the usual political view); zoomed in is the detail level where terrain, rivers and holding models are visible; zooming further strips realm colours to show county names over terrain (snippet). — [CK3 Dev Diary 2](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ck3-dev-diary-2-the-medieval-map.1274052/)
- CK3: the paper map is presented as "a rather fancy overview of the world (or excellent screenshots)" and shows only independent realms with no vassal breakdown (snippet). — [CK3 Dev Diary 25](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ckiii-dev-diary-25-map-features-and-map-modes.1388210/)
- CK3 2025 update: a second, new illustrated paper map inspired by Asian folklore covers the whole world and is the default for most East Asian cultures. Its terrain "draws on Chinese painting and Shan Shui, with bold strokes and leafy, steep mountains", and it is decorated with creatures such as the Fenghuang and the Four Symbols (Azure Dragon, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger, Black Turtle). The original paper map was styled after the Carta Marina. Players choose the style in graphics settings. Written by 2D art lead Lucas Ribeiro. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- CK3 2025 update: effects now blend more smoothly between map modes, removing long-standing pop-in and glitches; the political map uses a higher-resolution texture with less visible tiling; a new Terrain Map Mode shows the whole world in the 3D terrain style; players can zoom in closer than before. — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- Victoria 3: the "papermap" is the zoomed-out version of the 3D map, with many small illustrations, and it sits inside a scene with a table and surrounding objects to frame the player as "overlooking the world as a whole". — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- EU5 ships paper-map border models as separate mesh assets (`gfx/models/papermap/`), and early design posts presented a flat map as fitting the map's aesthetic (snippet for the second point). — [EU5 wiki, Map modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modding); [Tinto Talks 2](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/tinto-talks-2-march-6th-2024.1626415/)
- Nobunaga's Ambition: Awakening: the campaign map presents "more detailed information on provinces, counties, and unit movements the closer you zoom in" (snippet). — [TheSixthAxis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/06/18/nobunagas-ambition-awakening-complete-edition-review/)
- Ara: History Untold [2024] launched with two separate modes, the detailed "Living World" and an icon-based "Strategic View"; update 1.4 added a "Living Strategy" mode that "combines these, clearly showing units scaled on the map to enhance visibility and strategic clarity". — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/?p=209905)
- Criticism: CK3 forum users argued that terrain, political and paper maps should be separate buttons, calling zoom transitions "needlessly constraining from a practical perspective" (snippet). — [CK3 Dev Diary 25 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ckiii-dev-diary-25-map-features-and-map-modes.1388210/)
- Criticism: Humankind playtesters said they had to zoom in and out constantly to read the map, and one said zooming made it worse and caused dizziness. — [Amplitude OpenDev thread](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/208-lucy-opendev/threads/38070-visually-overwhelming?page=1)
- Criticism: an EU5 player called the 3D terrain flat and the flat political map among Paradox's worst-looking, while another liked the 3D map but found some icons confusing at certain zoom levels (snippet, anecdotal). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3450310/discussions/0/684112192552984571/)

### Inferences
- The paper map is doing three jobs at once: an at-a-glance political overview, a mood piece (it tells you the era and culture), and a screenshot generator. Paradox explicitly lists the screenshot value.
- CK3's Shan Shui paper map is the single most transferable idea in this study for an ancient-China game: the far-zoom layer can be a 2D illustration, which is exactly what an AI-painting pipeline is good at, and it sidesteps the lack of a 3D art team.
- Ara's history suggests two separate views (pretty versus readable) is the weaker design; it ended up adding a merged view with scaled-up units.
- Browser approximation: drive a single 0–1 "paperness" uniform from camera height. In the terrain shader, lerp from lit 3D albedo to a flat parchment-toned political texture; fade out trees, city meshes and unit models; fade in large territory labels, ink-wash mountain illustrations and decorative cartouches as decals. A desk or lacquer-table mesh around the map edge gives the Victoria 3 framing cheaply.
- Browser approximation: generate the paper layer as one large AI-painted ink-wash map of China (rivers, ranges, the two dozen cities as brush icons), aligned to the heightmap. Add a paper-fibre normal map and slight vignette so it reads as an object.
- Provide explicit map-mode buttons as well as zoom linkage, and cross-fade every layer over a height band instead of switching, since pop-in was a long-standing CK3 complaint that took five years to fix.
- Needs a AAA team: nothing in this category. The cost is one or two illustrations and shader blending.

### Gaps
- No primary description was found of how Civilization VII or Humankind treat their far zoom level. One review extract described Humankind's top zoom as "painted continents" and its middle zoom as an "unsightly greyscale", but the outlet could not be pinned down, so it is not cited.
- The technical method behind Paradox's transition (how the 3D terrain is flattened and blended) is not explained in any source reached.
- No source was found on whether Total War titles or Koei titles offer any stylised far-zoom layer.

## 3. Cities and settlements on the map: how do they visibly grow and reflect ownership, culture, prosperity, fortification and damage?

### Takeaway
Visible city growth is the most loved and the most criticised map feature of this generation: Civilization VII's sprawling, per-culture districts and Victoria 3's threshold-based city growth make the world feel alive, but sprawl is also the root of Civ VII's readability complaints. The safe pattern is growth in discrete, clearly different tiers with a tight footprint and strong silhouettes.

### Cited Findings
- Civilization VII: districts are no longer pre-zoned. A district forms when a building is placed on an urban tile, each urban tile holds two buildings, and new districts must touch the city centre and expand outward, which Firaxis says gives a "more cohesive and unified look for your Cities". Wonders take a full tile. Rural improvements appear automatically as population grows. — [Civ VII dev diary, Managing Your Empire](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/managing-your-empire/)
- Civilization VII: two civ-specific unique buildings on one tile form a Unique Quarter with "distinct visual flair that reflects your civilization's culture and progress". — [Civ VII dev diary, Managing Your Empire](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/managing-your-empire/)
- Civilization VII: in Civ VI, districts looked largely identical across civilizations; in Civ VII each civilization gets its own version of every building, with regional colour changes, gold or marble for grander civs, and motifs and statues. A robust level-of-detail system supports this. — [Hooked Gamers interview](https://hookedgamers.com/features/2024/10/23/the_art_of_civilization_vii_-_an_interview.html)
- Civilization VII: "Settlements now sprawl beyond the City center with the addition of Urban Districts", which also changed siege mechanics (snippet). — [Civ VII dev diary, Combat](https://civilization.2k.com/de-DE/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/combat/)
- Victoria 3: cities ("hubs") grow on the map as their production, government and residential buildings pass thresholds, and are built from culture-specific building sets ranging "from small farms to huge monuments" (first point snippet). — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 50 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-50-living-map.1531318/); [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- Victoria 3 criticism: forum commenters said city sizes on the map do not scale with real population (Paris versus nearby cities was the example), so "you cannot tell looking at the map" (snippet). — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 50 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-50-living-map.1531318/)
- Manor Lords: praised for towns that look as if they evolved organically, with detailed buildings, market stalls, naturalistic lighting and seasonal weather. — [Strategy and Wargaming list](https://strategyandwargaming.com/2025/11/15/the-20-best-looking-strategy-games-to-play-in-2025/4)
- Manor Lords was built by a solo developer in Unreal Engine 4 using photogrammetry, motion capture and pre-calculated destruction simulations, and he credits free Megascans assets and the engine's automatic level-of-detail generation (snippet). — [Epic Games Store article](https://store.epicgames.com/news/manor-lords-solo-developer-s-highly-anticipated-medieval-strategy-game-hits-this-month); [Unreal Engine interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/solo-dev-makes-sophisticated-sim-manor-lords-using-unreal-engine)
- Total War: Pharaoh: the expanded Dynasties map is described by Creative Assembly as intricately detailed with iconic landmarks in each region (Mount Olympus, the Meteora, the statue of Cybele, the Lemnos necropolis), adding 168 settlements (snippet). — [Creative Assembly dev update](https://community.creative-assembly.com/total-war/total-war-pharaoh/blogs/20-total-war-pharaoh-dev-update-%E2%80%93-expanded-map)
- Age of Wonders 4: outposts visibly gain walls and watchtowers as they develop. — [AoW4 Dev Diary 7](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-7)
- Romance of the Three Kingdoms 8 Remake [2024]: graphics "merge 2D ink paintings with 3D maps and combat scenes" (snippet). The producer said the city screen layout is intentionally kept from the 2001 original and reuses 2D assets for the animated populace, and that major effort went into 2D ink-wash battle backgrounds. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-8-remake/out-now-steam); [GamingBolt interview](https://gamingbolt.com/romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-8-remake-interview-links-debates-tales-and-more)
- Civilization VII criticism: players say late-game sprawl is "exhausting to continue to look at", that it is hard to tell where one tile ends and another begins, and that rural improvements (clay pits, mines) look as built-up as urban tiles so "the settlement/city seems to spread over the entire map". — [CivFanatics, cluttered thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/does-anyone-else-think-the-map-just-looks-really-unpleasantly-cluttered.694601/); [CivFanatics, map graphics thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-vii-map-graphics.699306/)
- Civilization VII counterpoint: other players say sprawl makes the world "feel alive and lived in" and that Civ V's cities were "extremely unimpressive dots". — [CivFanatics, cluttered thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/does-anyone-else-think-the-map-just-looks-really-unpleasantly-cluttered.694601/)

### Inferences
- Two models exist. Civ VII and Manor Lords show every building (maximum life, maximum clutter). Victoria 3 swaps the city for a bigger preset at thresholds (less literal, far cheaper, easier to read). For two dozen cities and no art team the threshold model is the right one.
- Culture and ownership are carried mostly by roofs, colour accents and one or two landmark silhouettes, not by every building being unique. Civ VII's "own version of every building per civilization" is the expensive end of that spectrum.
- Players want city size on the map to mean something. Victoria 3 was criticised when visual size and real population diverged.
- Browser approximation: 4–5 city tiers assembled from a small procedural kit (rammed-earth wall segments, gate towers, hip-and-gable roofs, a palace or pagoda centrepiece), plus an instanced scatter of houses and fields in a radius that grows with population. Fortification level changes wall height and tower count; prosperity adds markets, lanterns and chimney smoke.
- Browser approximation, ownership: faction-coloured banners on gates, a coloured roof-trim tint via a shader uniform, and a faction-coloured ground ring. Changing owner becomes a uniform change, not a new model.
- Browser approximation, damage and siege: swap to a scorched ground decal, add fire and smoke particles, drop some instanced houses, desaturate the city tint; restore over turns.
- Browser approximation, landmarks: for the handful of famous cities (Luoyang, Chang'an, Chengdu, Jianye and so on) an AI-painted landmark rendered as a camera-facing billboard or simple extruded card gives unique identity without modelling.
- Keep the footprint tight. A city should not cover the mountain pass or river that makes its location matter; Civ VII players complained that sprawl "diminishes the impact of geography".
- Needs a AAA team: per-culture versions of every building (Civ VII); street-level photogrammetry towns with simulated villagers (Manor Lords, although one developer achieved it with licensed scan libraries over several years).

### Gaps
- No source reached describes how Total War settlement models change by level, culture or damage on the campaign map, although this is a well-known feature of the series.
- No source reached describes city presentation on the Romance of the Three Kingdoms XIV or Nobunaga's Ambition: Awakening maps.
- No technical explanation was found of how Civilization VII assembles district visuals (procedural placement, slot system, level-of-detail budget).
- How damage, pillaging and occupation are shown on the map was not documented for any of the games in the sources reached.

## 4. Borders, territory ownership, fog of war, selection highlights, movement paths and map labels

### Takeaway
The most admired treatments make abstract information look like part of the physical object: unexplored land as mist with terrain silhouettes (Age of Wonders 4), clouds as fog of war (Victoria 3), board-game tiles for the unknown (Civilization VII), and softly textured map lettering (CK3). The most common complaint in the genre is not ugliness but borders and tile ownership that cannot be read at a glance.

### Cited Findings
- Age of Wonders 4: "Unexplored Terrain is covered in animated mists that swirl around mountain tops. We rendered silhouettes of terrain features into the mist so you have some rough awareness of the lay of the land." Explored hexes outside vision become a separate fog of war. — [AoW4 Dev Diary 7](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-7)
- Victoria 3: clouds add depth and provide the fog of war; technical art also handles the look of country borders and occupied "Contested Territories". — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- Civilization VII: fog of war is presented as tile sets. Standard editions have black tiles and the Founders Edition adds a gold tile set, switchable in gameplay settings (snippet, player thread). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1295660/discussions/0/591762915933047305/)
- Humankind: altitude shapes fog of war and line of sight; moving through a valley hides what sits above it (snippet). — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/civ-competitor-humankind-makes-your-choice-of-terrain-vital-to-survival)
- CK3: territory colour is a zoom-dependent layer. Realm colours are shown at the middle zoom and stripped away when zoomed in so county names sit directly on terrain (snippet). — [CK3 Dev Diary 2](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ck3-dev-diary-2-the-medieval-map.1274052/)
- CK3 2025 update: map text was given a subtle texture and softer look so it would "feel less digital and easier on the eyes". — [CK3 Dev Diary 177 (mirror)](https://devtrackers.gg/crusader-kings/p/3c2f2816-dev-diary-177-a-fresh-coat-of-paint)
- EU5: ownership rests on a location-ID image (one unique colour per location, 16384 x 8192) with a text file mapping colours to locations; roads and similar linear features use a spline network file. — [EU5 wiki, Map modding](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/Map_modding)
- Age of Wonders 4: hostile infestations mark their domain with "an ominous red border". — [AoW4 Dev Diary 7](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-7)
- Romance of the Three Kingdoms XIV [2020]: territory is painted hex by hex in the faction's colour, described as "a simplified color system" (snippet); a reviewer found it dull when "just watching armies painting in the blanks as they conquer territory". — [Kotaku game page](https://kotaku.com/games/romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-xiv); [PCGamesN review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-14/review)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms: the interface is built around flowing black ink on paper, with ink flowing into the 3D visuals instead of framed panels. — [GamesBeat interview](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/); [TheSixthAxis interview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=320428)
- Victoria 3: a 2023 game-jam prototype added building highlighting on the map and goods moving along roads, railways and sea lanes (snippet). — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 70](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-70-feature-game-jam-part-2.1562169/)
- Civilization VII update 1.5.1 (reported as 10 November 2026) added a Map Search tool that highlights tiles, units and buildings on the visible map, and gave urban districts on vegetated tiles clearer visuals (snippet; full notes not opened). — [Civ VII game update notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes)
- Criticism, Humankind: borders were the most common readability complaint in the pre-release playtest. Territory shapes were irregular, neutral land inside empires was hard to spot, and one player placed an outpost in the wrong region. Players asked for darker borders and a distinct white border for neutral land. — [Amplitude OpenDev thread](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/208-lucy-opendev/threads/38070-visually-overwhelming?page=1)
- Criticism, EU5: the road overlay colours whole provinces instead of highlighting roads, making routes hard to follow (snippet, anecdotal). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3450310/discussions/0/684112192552984571/)
- Criticism, Old World [2021]: muted colours, few scaling options and limited overlays make the map harder to read and cause misclicks and eye strain (snippet). — [Cultured Vultures review](https://culturedvultures.com/old-world-pc-review/)

### Inferences
- The Paradox data model (an ID texture plus a lookup table) is exactly what a shader wants, and it scales down trivially to a map with a few dozen territories.
- For a Three Kingdoms setting, the Age of Wonders 4 mist-with-silhouettes idea and the Three Kingdoms ink-flow idea combine naturally: unexplored or unseen land as bare rice paper or ink-wash mist with faint brush silhouettes of the mountains underneath.
- Border legibility should be tested at every zoom level before any beauty work. Humankind's playtest shows players forgive plain terrain sooner than unclear borders.
- Browser approximation, ownership: a low-resolution province-ID texture sampled in the terrain shader; owner colours in a small data texture updated on conquest; border lines from edge detection on the ID texture or from a precomputed distance-to-border texture, drawn as a crisp dark line plus a soft inner glow in faction colour that fades toward the territory interior.
- Browser approximation, conquest feedback: animate the owner colour bleeding outward from the captured city like ink soaking into paper, using a radial mask over a second or two. This turns Romance of the Three Kingdoms XIV's "painting the map" into a visual reward.
- Browser approximation, fog of war: a full-map visibility texture blurred on the CPU or in a ping-pong render target; unseen areas blend to desaturated paper or scrolling mist while keeping a faint height-based silhouette.
- Browser approximation, labels: signed-distance-field text (for example troika-three-text) laid flat on the terrain, curved along the long axis of a territory, with size and opacity tied to zoom; a slight paper-grain multiply softens the digital look, as CK3 did.
- Browser approximation, selection and paths: selection as an animated ring decal projected on terrain; movement paths as a ribbon mesh hugging the heightmap with a scrolling dashed texture and an arrowhead; reachable range as a soft tinted overlay from the same ID or distance texture.
- Needs a AAA team: nothing in this category.

### Gaps
- No developer source was found that explains border rendering (gradient borders, distance fields) in any Paradox or Firaxis title.
- No source confirms how Total War: Three Kingdoms renders its unexplored shroud, or how Humankind and Civilization VII stylise the edge of the known world. The Civ VII fog-of-war tile description rests on one player thread.
- No source was found describing selection highlights or movement-path rendering in any of the listed games.
- Colour-blind handling of faction colours was raised by one Civ VII forum poster but not documented by any developer.

## 5. Armies and characters on the map: scale, banners, idle animation, march and combat animation

### Takeaway
Units on the map are treated as oversized painted miniatures: detailed up close, but designed first for silhouette and readability at normal camera distance. Civilization VII's "continuous combat" on the map itself is the most ambitious recent example, while Ara's post-launch decision to scale units up shows that realistic unit scale loses to visibility.

### Cited Findings
- Civilization VII: lead unit artist John Fitzgerald said units are detailed up close (armour, buckles) but built to read clearly at the zoomed-out camera, borrowing from miniatures, with each unit a small part of a larger, visually consistent army. — [Hooked Gamers interview](https://hookedgamers.com/features/2024/10/23/the_art_of_civilization_vii_-_an_interview.html)
- Civilization VII: "continuous combat" keeps paired units fighting on screen until the turn ends, replacing the older pattern where attackers moved forward, animated and returned one at a time; naval battles show ships ramming and crews brawling on deck. — [Hooked Gamers interview](https://hookedgamers.com/features/2024/10/23/the_art_of_civilization_vii_-_an_interview.html); [Civ VII dev diary, Combat](https://civilization.2k.com/de-DE/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/combat/)
- Civilization VI [2016, context only]: its art director said things are more readable if they have different silhouettes (snippet). — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/making-i-civilization-vi-i-both-playable-and-beautiful)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms: characters are visible on the campaign map; a general "can be seen walking around with his army", which also provides vision. — [TheSixthAxis interview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=320428)
- Ara: History Untold: update 1.4's Living Strategy view shows "units scaled on the map to enhance visibility and strategic clarity", alongside sharper unit and city detail. — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/?p=209905)
- Ara: its Nitrous engine is described by Oxide's chief graphics architect as built to handle thousands of units at once (snippet). — [Ara dev diary, Nitrous Engine](https://arahistoryuntold.com/nitrous-engine-with-dan-baker)
- EU5: the Tinto Talks of 21 May 2025 was the first in-depth look at a visual system and covered land unit graphics, shown as work in progress. Forum replies debated how many models should stand for an army and argued other map assets should be smaller relative to units (snippet). — [Tinto Talks 64](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/tinto-talks-64-21st-of-may-2025.1756210/)
- Victoria 3: military 3D units appear on the map only when battles take place, with effects such as cannon fire. — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- Songs of Conquest: characters are animated 2D sprites drawn in Aseprite and rendered as billboards in the 3D scene, with all animations at eight frames and a shared framerate. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Nobunaga's Ambition: Awakening: armies are shown as icons moving on the campaign map, and reviews call the graphics simple and serviceable (snippet). — [Sportskeeda review](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/nobunaga-s-ambition-awakening-review)
- Criticism, Victoria 3: some forum users found the living-map units distracting (snippet). — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 50 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-50-living-map.1531318/)
- Criticism, Humankind: playtesters struggled to locate their own units and cities against the detailed terrain. — [Amplitude OpenDev thread](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/208-lucy-opendev/threads/38070-visually-overwhelming?page=1)

### Inferences
- The consistent rule is that unit scale is a readability decision, not a realism decision. Every game that started realistic (Ara) or dense (Humankind) moved toward larger, clearer units.
- A named general on a horse with a banner is the natural "miniature" for a Three Kingdoms map, and Total War: Three Kingdoms validates showing the character, not an abstract token.
- Songs of Conquest proves a small team can get a rich look from 2D animated billboards on 3D terrain; this matches an AI-painted 2D pipeline better than rigged 3D models do.
- Browser approximation, army token: one hero rider (existing procedural horse-and-rider rig or an AI-painted billboard), 6–12 instanced foot soldiers in a loose formation, and a tall banner in faction colour carrying the commander's name and a troop-strength bar. Keep a minimum on-screen size by scaling the group with camera distance.
- Browser approximation, idle and march: banner cloth with a sine-wave vertex shader; a subtle breathing or weight-shift loop; marching along the path spline with a dust-puff particle trail and the formation bobbing out of phase.
- Browser approximation, combat on the map: when two armies meet, have the instanced soldiers lunge and recoil on a short loop with spark and dust sprites and a clash sound, then hand off to the existing duel or battle scene. A looped "skirmish" state like Civ VII's continuous combat is feasible at this scale because only a few armies fight at once.
- Browser approximation, billboards: if 3D soldiers are too costly, use 8-frame sprite-sheet billboards from one atlas per faction, as Songs of Conquest does.
- Needs a AAA team: motion-captured paired combat animations for many unit types (Civilization VII), and per-culture unit model sets.

### Gaps
- No source reached gives specifics on banner design, idle animation sets or march animation for Total War, Paradox or Koei titles.
- The content of EU5's unit-graphics dev diary (model counts, level-of-detail approach) could not be read directly.
- Whether Romance of the Three Kingdoms XIV or 8 Remake animate officers on the strategic map was not confirmed.

## 6. "Living world" ambient animation: birds, caravans, ships, smoke, waving trees, water motion, day-night

### Takeaway
Ambient motion that reflects game state (Victoria 3's trains and ships upgrading with technology, Ara's simulated citizens) is what reviewers single out as making a map feel alive. It is praised when it is quiet background and criticised when it competes with units for attention or costs frame rate.

### Cited Findings
- Victoria 3: the map shows locomotives progressing from early steam to electric trains as a visual signal that a region is developing, and cargo ships along sea lanes that change from clippers to steamships depending on research and production methods (snippet). — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 50 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-50-living-map.1531318/)
- Victoria 3: commenters praised small animated touches such as wind moving through trees, while others raised performance concerns about the living map's graphics load (snippet). — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 50 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-50-living-map.1531318/)
- Victoria 3: map effects mark buildings being constructed and completed, plus turmoil, revolutions and celebrations. — [Victoria 3 Dev Diary 49](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-49-graphic-overview)
- Ara: History Untold: the Living World is a "vibrant simulation" where "Farmers, bakers, and soldiers are individually simulated entities with their own needs and behaviors". — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/?p=209905)
- Ara: reviewers called the map striking, with animals moving across it, but said the game bogs down on higher visual settings as cities grow and more of the map is explored (snippet). — [Gamereactor review](https://www.gamereactor.eu/ara-history-untold-1436193/)
- Ara: the art director's dev diary covers immersion "integrated into every detail" and the road-network system. — [Ara dev diary, Living Worlds part 2](https://www.arahistoryuntold.com/living-worlds-with-dorian-newcomb-part-2)
- Manor Lords: seasons pass and villagers are seen working, with oxen ploughing and sheep being herded (snippet). — [Epic Games Store article](https://store.epicgames.com/news/manor-lords-solo-developer-s-highly-anticipated-medieval-strategy-game-hits-this-month)
- Age of Wonders 4: mists are animated and swirl around mountain tops; pools of light move across the map (second point snippet). — [AoW4 Dev Diary 7](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-7); [AoW4 Dev Diary 11 forum thread](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/dev-diary-11-the-art-of-age-of-wonders-4.1576391/page-2)
- Songs of Conquest: most of the tens of thousands of billboards on screen are animated, and particle effects are layered with bloom and colour grading. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixing-2d-billboards-and-3d-environments-in-a-game/)
- Total War: Three Kingdoms: lanterns and fires provide light and contrast at night, and the day-night cycle runs continuously on the campaign map. — [GamesBeat interview](https://gamesbeat.com/how-creative-assembly-conceived-the-art-for-total-war-three-kingdoms/)
- Romance of the Three Kingdoms 8 Remake: the city screen keeps an animated 2D populace reused from the original game. — [GamingBolt interview](https://gamingbolt.com/romance-of-the-three-kingdoms-8-remake-interview-links-debates-tales-and-more)

### Inferences
- The strongest ambient elements are diegetic indicators: they are decoration that also tells you something (a developed region has trains; a trade route has ships). Purely decorative motion is the kind players call distracting.
- Motion budget matters: Victoria 3 and Ara both drew performance complaints, so ambient systems should be instanced, capped and tied to a quality setting.
- Browser approximation, traffic: a few dozen instanced carts, pack animals and river junks moving along precomputed road and river splines, with density proportional to the prosperity of the cities they connect and to whether a trade route or supply line is active. This is cheap and state-driven.
- Browser approximation, birds and wildlife: 3–5 small flocks of instanced quads with a flap animation in the vertex shader, circling on looping paths above mountains and lakes; cranes or geese suit the setting.
- Browser approximation, smoke and fire: small billboard particle emitters at cities (cooking smoke scaled by population, black smoke when besieged) and at army camps.
- Browser approximation, vegetation and water: tree sway and grass shimmer in vertex shaders; water with scrolling normals and foam bands; banners and flags sharing one cloth shader.
- Browser approximation, night: city lantern glow sprites that brighten at dusk, campfires for stationary armies, and a cooler ambient grade.
- Needs a AAA team (or a specialised engine): thousands of individually simulated, animated citizens (Ara's Nitrous engine, Manor Lords' villagers).

### Gaps
- The full text of Victoria 3's Living Map diary could not be read, so its technical approach (spline networks, city generation) is unconfirmed.
- No source reached documents ambient animation in Civilization VII, EU5, CK3, Humankind, Old World or the Koei titles.
- Ara's art-director diaries are videos; their detailed content was not available as text.

## 7. Which techniques have developers documented, and what concrete technical detail is available?

### Takeaway

- Open three.js examples of the genre exist: a Civ-style hex map with animated water, coastal waves, procedural tree placement and simple grass shaders, and a hex terrain library with rivers, trees and coast (snippets). — [three.js forum showcase](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/civ-like-3d-hexagonal-terrain-map-early-stage/92890); [threejs-hex-map](https://www.github.com/crempp/threejs-hex-map)

### Inferences
- The games whose techniques are best documented happen to be the ones closest to the target project's constraints: Songs of Conquest (small team, 2D art on 3D terrain, heavy shader use) and CK3 (a stylised real-world map with a paper layer). They should be the primary references.
- Paradox's stack is effectively "textures plus shaders": ID map, heightmap, masks, colour maps and splines. Every element has a direct WebGL equivalent, which is not true of Firaxis's or Creative Assembly's asset-heavy approach.
- Priority order for a no-art-team browser game, by visual return per unit of effort: (1) cloud shadows, distance fog and a colour grade; (2) ink-wash paper map at far zoom with smooth cross-fade; (3) shader-drawn faction borders with ink-bleed conquest animation; (4) mist-with-silhouette fog of war; (5) tiered procedural cities with faction trim and night lanterns; (6) spline traffic, birds and smoke; (7) erosion-derived terrain masks and instanced forests; (8) tilt-shift and seasons as optional extras.
- Summary of what realistically needs a AAA art team: unique per-culture building and unit sets, motion-captured on-map combat, photogrammetry-level settlements, thousands of simulated citizens, and hand-sculpted world landmarks. Everything else in this study is primarily shader, data-texture or illustration work.

## 8. What has been criticised? The cautionary lessons

### Takeaway
Across Civilization VII, Humankind, Old World, Victoria 3 and Ara the same failure repeats: detail and atmosphere were added faster than hierarchy, so players could not find their units, read borders or tell buildings apart. The fixes that players ask for, and that developers eventually ship, are always the same: an overlay or strategic view, stronger borders, bigger units, colour accents and toggles.

### Cited Findings
- Civilization VII: forum players call the map "an overly-busy mess" that is "very hard to read"; the biggest issue cited is "not being able to identify buildings at a glance", and quarters are hard to read without hovering. — [CivFanatics, map graphics thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-vii-map-graphics.699306/)
- Civilization VII: the same players praise the underlying art ("terrain itself looks very nice", "looks a ton better than Civ 6"), so the objection is to density, not quality. — [CivFanatics, map graphics thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-vii-map-graphics.699306/)
- Civilization VII: requested fixes include a strategic or lens view like Civ VI's, coloured roofs or accent colours by building type, coloured hex borders or a hotkey overlay, and tighter cities; one poster noted colour-coding "looked goofy in Civ 6" and another that colour-blind players would struggle. — [CivFanatics, map graphics thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-vii-map-graphics.699306/)
- Civilization VII: unpacked, sprawling cities are felt to diminish the impact of geography, and the map is said to "look and feel small" (second quote snippet). — [CivFanatics, map graphics thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-vii-map-graphics.699306/); [CivFanatics, art direction poll thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/poll-for-civ-vii-which-art-direction-style-do-you-prefer.682218/page-3)

### Inferences
- Rule 1, hierarchy before detail: decide what must be findable in under a second (own armies, enemy armies, cities, borders) and make those the highest-contrast, most saturated elements. Terrain should sit in a narrower, calmer value range. Victoria 3's "soothing enough not to compete with the interface" is the right brief; CK3's 2025 change to light objects separately from terrain is the right mechanism.
- Rule 2, avoid high-frequency, high-contrast texture on terrain. Humankind's "Magic Eye" complaint and CK3's noisy tiled mountains are the same mistake. For AI-painted textures this is a specific risk, because generated paintings tend to be busy; blur or posterise them and keep detail for close zoom only.
- Rule 3, palette discipline: complaints of "too yellow", "washed out" and "muted" show that both over- and under-saturation get punished. Three Kingdoms' and Age of Wonders 4's vivid but controlled palettes are the most praised.
- Rule 4, keep settlement footprints small relative to geography, and make rural and urban areas look clearly different.
- Rule 5, ship toggles: cloud shadows, depth of field, ambient traffic and day-night should each be switchable, both for taste and for integrated-GPU performance in a browser.
- Rule 6, always provide an overlay path: a clean political or strategic mode that flattens the art and enlarges icons. With about two dozen cities this is a small amount of work and removes most readability risk.
- Rule 7, test readability with the interface on screen and at the default zoom, not in beauty screenshots. Most of the criticised games photographed well.
