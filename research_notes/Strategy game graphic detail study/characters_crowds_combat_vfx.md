## 1. Character detail at 5–30 m: armour, cloth, hair, secondary motion, dirt/blood/wetness — what reads and what is wasted

### Takeaway
Recent flagship titles spend their character budget on things that *move or change state* (wind-driven cloth, tassels, hair, and accumulating mud/blood/wetness driven by one shared world simulation), and developers of pulled-back-camera games say outright that shape, silhouette and form are what survive distance. No source found gives a numeric "reads at X metres" rule; that part is inference from screen-size arithmetic.

### Cited Findings

Moving cloth, tassels and secondary motion
- [Dev] Ghost of Yōtei: wind "affects the world and characters, including clothing, weapon tassels, and hanging cloth in settlements"; a GPU-compute cloth system supports multiple simulated cloth layers, cloth-to-cloth collision and many simultaneous simulations chosen by heuristics (Adrian Bentley, Director of Programming, Sucker Punch, 23 Oct 2025) — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [Dev] Ghost of Yōtei art direction deliberately put the heroine in lighter, cloth-based gear (vs. the previous hero's heavy armour), with a sash as a key motif (GDC 2026 creative-direction talk, Nate Fox / Jason Connell, reported 10 Mar 2026) — [4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/840/G084044/20260310041/)
- [Reviewer] Ghost of Yōtei clothing uses "high-quality, physically based materials that sway to the tune of the wind"; per-object motion blur and "high-quality alpha particles" are credited for much of the presentation (25 Sep 2025) — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=628528)
- [Dev] Assassin's Creed Shadows: the Atmos weather system makes wind-blown rain drip off eaves onto characters' clothes and hair (Maxime Beaudoin, Technical Lead, 14 Apr 2026) — [Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-gb/article/6u0kflsdY20YlnZks7rUeG/inside-anvil-the-technology-powering-assassins-creed-shadows)
- [Dev] Assassin's Creed Shadows kept "fluid-driven wind and particle effects & physically animated vegetation" as non-negotiable on every PC preset (Pierre F, Technology Director, 12 Feb 2025) — [Ubisoft Tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/ar-ae/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ/assassins-creed-shadows-tech-qa)
- [Dev] The Witcher 4 UE 5.6 tech demo: Control Rig physics was built "to drive Kelpie's accessories and hair" (Danny Chapman, Senior Animation Programmer, Epic; reported 23 Jun 2025) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/watch-unreal-engine-s-control-rig-physics-on-ciri-s-horse-from-the-witcher)
- [Reviewer] Crimson Desert (Mar 2026): hair flows by movement speed and wind direction; cloaks sit over undergarments without clipping; armour sits correctly under a holstered sword (18 Mar 2026) — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/crimson-desert-30-brilliant-details-that-elevate-the-entire-experience)
- [Reviewer] Phantom Blade Zero (Oct 2026): physically based materials make the hero's tattered cloak and weathered bamboo hat stand out; rain droplets on clothing and "palpable" rust on a weapon add material detail (2 Nov 2026) — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=656112)
- [Dev-shipped settings] Phantom Blade Zero's PC menu exposes separate "Character Texture Quality", "Character Model Quality" and "High-Quality Hair" options, i.e. hair is treated as its own scalable cost (27 Oct 2026) — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/phantom-blade-zero-pc-performance-benchmarks/)

Dirt, blood, wetness build-up
- [Dev] Ghost of Yōtei: "a directional grid deformed around the character records interactions with the world" and drives texture effects that make characters look wet, bloody, muddy or snowy; combat and rolling kick up dust and leaves and leave the heroine muddy and bloody — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [Reviewer] Crimson Desert: clothing and equipment stay visibly wet for a long time after a river dive or heavy rain — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/crimson-desert-30-brilliant-details-that-elevate-the-entire-experience)
- [Third-party guide] Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: fighting and travelling leave the player covered in dirt and blood, which lowers Charisma, so the visual state doubles as a gameplay state (excerpt only) — [Gamer Guides](https://gamerguides.com/kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/guide/getting-started/gameplay/washing-clothes-and-armor)
- [Dev] Warhorse co-founder Martin Klima on KCD2's layered clothing: the system "is quite complex as it is, and it really doesn't need another layer of complexity" (excerpt only) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-nearly-had-a-san-andreas-like-fat-henry-system-that-would-have-let-you-create-bohemias-chunkiest-boy-and-forced-you-to-wear-clothes-that-fit-to-boot)

Muscle and body deformation
- [Dev] Witcher 4 tech demo: Unreal's ML Deformer lets the horse's muscles "move and flex", approximating high-fidelity deformation at runtime without a performance hit (Sebastian Kalemba, Game Director, 3 Jun 2025) — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=405351)

What survives distance (developer statements)
- [Dev] Dynasty Warriors: Origins: because of the soldier count the camera pulls back and players lose their own character ("自分どこにいるの？" / "Where am I?"); the team was applying several techniques to keep the player character findable. The palette was also deliberately muted for a more historical Three Kingdoms look (producer Tomohiko Sho, 26 Jul 2024) — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202407/10868)
- [Dev, Background 2016] Civilization VI art director Brian Busatti: "You want to make sure that things are readable from that distance"; the answer is emphasis on shape and form, and "it's obviously a little more readable if you have different silhouettes to things" — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/making-i-civilization-vi-i-both-playable-and-beautiful)
- [Dev] Tempest Rising (2025): units are given extra real-world gear such as a bulky fuel tank specifically to strengthen silhouettes while staying grounded (game director Frederick Schreiber; excerpt only, article body truncated) — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/how-tempest-rising-uses-unreal-engine-5-to-channel-the-heart-of-90s-strategy-games-without-losing-its-soul)
- [Vendor/Dev tool guidance] For strategy cameras, rarely seen angles can be optimised aggressively; the last LOD should be a remeshed proxy to cut "tiny triangles", overdraw and draw calls; team/player colour should be carried as a baked mask or material-ID so it survives the proxy (Jesper Tingvall, Simplygon, 8 May 2026) — [Xbox Game Dev Blog](https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2026/05/optimize-3d-models-for-strategy-games-with-simplygon/)
- [Reviewer] Counter-example of detail hurting: Phantom Blade Zero's boss-fight effects are "sometimes overwhelming and actually hinders gameplay, because you can't even see what's going on" (14 Nov 2026) — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/phantom-blade-zero-review/); a second review lists "Poor readability" among its cons (26 Oct 2026) — [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/reviews/phantom-blade-zero-review-dark-wuxia-martial-arts-masters-with-an-apprentices-execution)

### Inferences
- Screen-size arithmetic (my calculation, assuming a 45° vertical field of view on a 1080-pixel-high frame): at 5 m a 1.8 m figure is about 470 px tall (1 px ≈ 4 mm); at 15 m about 156 px (1 px ≈ 12 mm); at 30 m about 78 px (1 px ≈ 23 mm). So at 30 m a 5 cm armour plate is ~2 px and a 2 cm tassel cord is under 1 px, while a 30 cm plume or a 1 m cape edge is 13–45 px.
- Consequently, at 15–30 m the things that read are: overall silhouette (helmet crest, plume, cape, weapon length, banner), large value/colour blocks (bronze-gold trim against dark cloth), *motion* of large secondary elements (cape, plume, horse tail, banner), and whole-figure state changes (a wet/dark or blood-reddened tint, a mud gradient rising from the feet). Individual lamellar plates, stitching, strand hair and tack buckles are sub-pixel and wasted beyond roughly 10 m; they belong only in the 5 m duel close-up.
- The games above get "detail" mainly from *one shared driver*: a global wind vector feeding cloth, tassels, hair and particles (Yōtei, AC Shadows, Crimson Desert), and an interaction/accumulation mask feeding wet/mud/blood (Yōtei). For code-built figures this is cheap: a single wind uniform plus per-figure scalar "wetness", "mud height" and "blood" parameters applied in the shader gives the same coherent read without simulation.
- Distance readability and combat spectacle pull against each other (DW Origins' lost player character; Phantom Blade Zero's clutter complaints). A dark, low-key palette makes this worse, so a reserved highlight (the bronze-gold) for the figures the player must find is supported by the developers' own remarks.

### Gaps
- No source found that states numerically which character details remain visible at given camera distances; the distance thresholds above are my arithmetic, not a developer rule.
- No primary technical material found on armour or fur shading (anisotropy, clear-coat, strand vs. card hair) for 2024–2026 titles; the Assassin's Creed Shadows GDC 2025 rendering talk coverage did not include hair/cloth detail ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-2025-talk-rendering-assassin-s-creed-shadows)).
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II's dirt/blood/layering pipeline is described only by guides and one indirect developer quote; no art-pipeline source found.

## 2. Horses and mounted combat: modelling, gaits, transitions, mane/tail, tack, dust, rider balance, procedural approaches

### Takeaway
The benchmark approach (Red Dead Redemption 2, now echoed by the Witcher 4 tech demo) is data-heavy: gait cycles blended by speed and lean, transitions synchronised to footfalls, a separately animated rider locked to the gait by sync tags, and physics layered on for tail, mane, tack and (2025) machine-learned muscle. Every element of that has a parametric core — footfall order, phase offsets, two offset "bouncing masses" for hips and chest — that code-driven figures can reproduce, and experts criticise wrong anatomy and stiff or floppy reins more than low fidelity.

### Cited Findings

Benchmark system (Red Dead Redemption 2)
- [Dev, Background 2018/2021] RDR2 horses use walk, trot, canter and gallop cycles; each animation was analysed for minimum, ideal and maximum speed tied to exertion; blending was driven by linear, angular and lateral velocity and later extended with pitch and lead foot (Tobias Kleanthous, Lead AI/Gameplay Programmer, GDC 2021, summarised 18 Nov 2021) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse)
- [Dev, Background] Gait changes use continuous acceleration/deceleration cycles synced to the feet, plus a custom stopping cycle so the horse stays responsive — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse)
- [Dev, Background] Standard foot IK fixed the feet but looked wrong for the horse's body and head on slopes, so Rockstar added dedicated incline/decline animations clustered by pitch, with left- and right-lead variants — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse)
- [Dev, Background] The rider is animated by a separate team and aligned to the horse through "sync points" and rhythm tags so the rider's bob matches the gait; the tail reacts through layered animation; the system reads terrain angle, deep surfaces (snow, water, mud) and wind; about 6,300 unique motions in total — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse)

2025–2026 state of the art
- [Dev] Witcher 4 UE 5.6 tech demo (3 Jun 2025): the horse Kelpie is "far more complex than our old friend Roach"; ML Deformer drives visible muscle flex without a performance hit — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=405351)
- [Dev] Same demo: multi-character motion matching keeps rider and horse "perfectly synchronized when mounting from any angle and speed", with root-motion movement on the horse (attributed to Wyeth Johnson, Epic, in press coverage of the demo; excerpt only, and which of these two outlets carries the exact quote was not verified) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/five-things-we-learned-from-the-witcher-4s-technical-demo), [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/105585/the-witcher-4-tech-demo-full-breakdown-everything-thats-worth-knowing/); accessories and hair are driven by new Control Rig physics — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/watch-unreal-engine-s-control-rig-physics-on-ciri-s-horse-from-the-witcher)
- [Dev] Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: a real horse in full motion-capture gear was walked and trotted through the studio with its rider (for the CGI trailer; excerpt only) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/rpg/if-you-liked-red-dead-redemption-2s-ultra-realistic-horses-kingdom-come-deliverance-2-devs-also-used-a-real-horse-in-the-motion-capture-studio)
- [Reviewer] Crimson Desert (2026): mount pace and handling change with terrain, incline, momentum and direction; sharp turns show visible weight transfer; uphill galloping slows and tires the mount; horses bolt when scared; riderless war horses gallop through battles; a shoved enemy can hit another soldier's horse, which bucks and throws its rider — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/crimson-desert-30-brilliant-details-that-elevate-the-entire-experience)
- [Reviewer] Ghost of Yōtei: the horse "moves naturally" through different terrain; mud and snow take dynamically generated trails — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=628528)
- [Dev] Dynasty Warriors: Origins: horses can be summoned and mounted at any time and have their own growth system — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202407/10868)

Tack, reins, mane and tail
- [Third-party expert review, Background 2019] Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord: reins are fixed at two points (bit and hand), a special case for cloth physics; TaleWorlds combined cloth simulation with IK that keeps the rider's hand near the horse's neck plus small animation fixes. Game reins usually look "entirely stiff or way too floppy". Mane and tail physics, and the weight of trot and canter, were praised; a rearing pose where the jaw "basically disappears into its neck" was criticised as anatomically impossible (Alice Ruppert, 17 Mar 2019) — [The Mane Quest](https://www.themanequest.com/blog/2019/3/17/mount-amp-blade-ii-bannerlord-takes-their-reins-seriously)
- [Dev, Background 2019] TaleWorlds reused the cloth system already used for horse manes and clothing for reins (excerpt only; page returned 403) — [TaleWorlds dev blog](https://taleworlds.com/en/Games/Bannerlord/Blog/101)

Mounted and matched combat
- [Dev, Background 2018] Total War authors "matched fighting animations between specific entities" for special pairings, and culls or authors animation sets per situation to avoid odd movement (Scott Pitkethly, Creative Assembly) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)

Gait fundamentals usable as code parameters
- [Animator, Background 2012] Walk footfall order is rear-left, fore-left, rear-right, fore-right; a quadruped walk is "like two offset human walk cycles"; hips and chest behave as two offset bouncing balls with the head as a third, offset for overlap; canter is the roughest gait with the most vertical motion; gallop adds a leap/stretch phase and more spine flex as speed rises; rotary gallop order is LR, RR, RF, LF, with transverse gallop mainly seen in horses; forward travel should be non-linear with a push-off just after the passing position; asymmetric cycles read as a limp (Cathy Feraday Miller) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/animating-four-legged-beasts)

Procedural approaches
- [Dev tool] Unreal Engine 5.6 added an experimental "Locomotor" node for Control Rig that generates procedural locomotion from foot sets and global step settings; Epic's tutorial is by Kiaran Ritchie (18 May 2025) — [Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/tutorial-procedural-animation-with-a-locomotor/2515954), [Epic API docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/Locomotor/FRigUnit_Locomotor)
- [Third-party] Users report Locomotor creatures walking with a tilt on uneven ground and snapping when the node is toggled (replies to Jun 2026) — [Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/tutorial-procedural-animation-with-a-locomotor/2515954); a practitioner calls it a fast starting point where a custom solution "lets you fine-tune gait logic and step ordering" (excerpt only) — [LinkedIn](https://ua.linkedin.com/in/ruslan-akhmedzhanov)
- [Third-party student work, 2025] A procedural IK quadruped: only one leg per side may lift at a time, foot targets are placed procedurally and moved along Catmull-Rom splines, a heightmap adapts feet to terrain — [Texas A&M course project](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/sueda/courses/CSCE450/2025S/projects/David_Herring/index.html)

### Inferences
- A convincing code-built horse needs, in priority order: (1) correct footfall order and beat count per gait, driven from a single phase value with per-leg offsets; (2) hip and chest vertical bounce as two offset oscillators, with the neck/head as a delayed third; (3) transitions that change gait only at a footfall and through a short blend of stride length and frequency, never an instant swap; (4) a rider whose pelvis and torso are driven from the same phase value (the "sync point" idea), with posting/absorption lagging the saddle; (5) tail, mane and plume as damped chains reacting to body acceleration and the global wind.
- RDR2's finding that foot IK alone looks wrong on slopes suggests the duel and the war theatre should either stay on near-flat ground or pitch the whole body and shift weight, rather than relying on leg IK to absorb terrain.
- Reins are a high-value, low-cost detail at duel distance: a two-anchor curve (bit to hand) with the hand held near the neck by IK avoids both failure modes the expert names (rigid bar, limp rope). Anatomical limits (neck/poll bend, jaw clearance) matter more to credibility than mesh density.
- Motion matching, ML deformers and mocap of real horses are out of reach for a no-asset pipeline; the visible *results* they deliver that are worth imitating are weight transfer in turns, exertion-dependent stride, and muscle/skin sliding — the last can be suggested at close range by a normal-map or vertex offset keyed to leg phase.
- Riderless horses and horses reacting to collisions (Crimson Desert) are cheap battlefield "life" for an army scene because they reuse the same gait code with no rider.

### Gaps
- No primary technical source found for horse systems in Ghost of Yōtei, Assassin's Creed Shadows, Kingdom Come: Deliverance II, Bannerlord (beyond reins), Dynasty Warriors: Origins, Total War cavalry or Manor Lords; no 2026 GDC horse-animation talk surfaced.
- Nothing sourced on hoof dust, hoof-impact decals or dust timing relative to footfall.
- Planned but not run (search budget): equestrian-expert reviews of 2025–2026 game horses (The Mane Quest on KCD2, Yōtei, AC Shadows); full text of the GamesRadar piece quoting an equestrian expert on the Witcher 4 horse was truncated.
- The Locomotor tutorial body (stepping thresholds, phase control) was not retrievable.

## 3. Army-scale rendering: how thousands of soldiers are drawn and animated, and what variation prevents the cloned look

### Takeaway
Across engines the pattern is the same: bake animation into textures, draw soldiers as GPU instances with a small per-instance record (transform, tint, animation and frame offset), step down through LODs to a baked proxy, and keep simulation off the render thread. The cloned look is broken by per-instance animation-time offsets, hue/tint shifts through a mask, random permutations of body pieces, and placement jitter, scale and facing randomness.

### Cited Findings

Dynasty Warriors: Origins
- [Dev] Producer Tomohiko Sho asked for "as many soldiers as possible"; the result is at least 10 times the count of Dynasty Warriors 7/8 — roughly 1,000 where there were about 100, reaching several thousand on screen — attributed to hardware and technology advances plus accumulated know-how; he was surprised himself ("こんなに出せるの!?") (26 Jul 2024) — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202407/10868)
- [Dev] Programmers raised the internal target to 10,000 on-screen soldiers (Sho, Tokyo Game Show 2024 interview as relayed; excerpt only) — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/dynasty-warriors-origins-devs-aimed-to-put-as-many-enemies-as-the-ps5-could-handle-on-screen/)
- [Dev] A bodyguard-unit feature had been cut after Dynasty Warriors 3 specifically to free rendering budget for more soldiers — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202407/10868)
- [Dev, via hands-on report] Soldiers are "governed by individually assigned algorithms" and move independently or under a leader's command (25 Sep 2024) — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=396742)
- [Publisher announcement] "Several thousand" soldiers clash per battlefield and fight "with individual intent" (26 Jul 2024) — [Inside Games](https://www.inside-games.jp/article/2024/07/26/157815.html)
- [Reviewer] Units can merge into a "large corps" (大軍団) with its own gauge, and diamond-shaped battle-zone icons turn blue or red to show who is winning — a readability layer on top of the crowd (14 Jan 2025) — [Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2025/01/14/148435.html)
- [Third-party claim by a rival developer] Kingmakers' developer contrasts its always-simulated soldiers with Dynasty Warriors "where soldiers spawn as you approach them" (excerpt only; not confirmed by Koei Tecmo) — [Dailymotion transcript](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x9ghxos)

Total War
- [Dev, Background 2018] Battle logic and animation are "decoupled from the display, allowing them to run simultaneously": "the logic generates the 'future' whilst the display renders the 'now'". LOD helps but is not enough alone because the camera can zoom and rotate; the engine must render "thousands and thousands of units" (Scott Pitkethly) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)
- [Dev, Background 2019] Total War: Three Kingdoms' GDC talk covered the engine's instancing pipeline and a move away from sorting particles to order-independent transparency (Tamas Rabel, Creative Assembly with Intel) — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026177/Instancing-and-Order-Independent-Transparency)
- [Dev, 2026] Creative Assembly's new Warcore engine (Total War: Warhammer 40,000, Medieval III) moves the series to DirectX 12; no unit-rendering figures published (excerpt only) — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/total-war/total-wars-new-warcore-engine-will-power-total-war-medieval-3-bring-the-strategy-series-to-console-and-restore-modding-we-want-to-put-these-tools-in-the-hands-of-players)

GPU-everything and vertex-animation crowds
- [Dev, Background 2021] Ultimate Epic Battle Simulator 2: "Everything, rendering/culling, animation, transforms and AI all run on the GPU"; "The key to all this was removing the CPU bottleneck"; "Characters use full bone animations now" (Brilliant Game Studios) — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1468720/discussions/0/3110267314129208740)
- [Tech-artist breakdown, 10 Jun 2026] 6,072 animated spectators using vertex-animation textures (VAT) on hierarchical instanced meshes versus skeletal meshes on an RTX 3070: 57.3 vs 28.4 fps, game thread 0.66 vs 34.15 ms, draw calls 276 vs 21,369, with a ~14,000-triangle character in both cases. Each animation is a separate 16-bit-float VAT with normals packed in the alpha channel; blending between states happens in the material (Yeonju "Kaley" Cho) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/6-000-agents-on-one-game-thread-novel-high-performance-framework-for-gpu-driven-crowd-systems/)
- [Same source] Variation: a per-instance random value offsets each agent's animation start time and drives a hue shift; body, shirt and hair are separate material groups so colours randomise per group; a stadium wave is computed in the shader from each agent's ID so the CPU sends only a few global parameters; seats are filled in shuffled order so the crowd looks organic — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/6-000-agents-on-one-game-thread-novel-high-performance-framework-for-gpu-driven-crowd-systems/)
- [Vendor, 28 Oct 2026] An Unreal crowd plugin claims VAT with a custom mesh path, compute-shader culling and indirect draws; a three-tier LOD (full mesh, low-poly mesh, baked distant character images) with soft transitions; variation through jitter, scale range, height offset, seed, facing modes and random animation choice; "about 58 FPS with 5 million characters" at 4K (unverified) — [Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/devpxsl-studios-ultimate-crowd-renderer-gpu-vertex-animation-system/2846211)
- [Third-party] Kingmakers reportedly simulates about 4,000 (up to 8,000) characters at once and uses GPU vertex animation for distant soldiers and skeletal animation near the player (fan wiki, unverified; excerpt only) — [The Games Wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/kingmakers/wiki/battlefield-ai-and-simulation)
- [Background 2007] Skinned instancing stores every bone matrix of every frame in one texture; per-instance data is a world matrix, a colour and two offsets (animation start, frame offset); characters are split into pieces (armour, weapons) and "random permutations" are generated at load; the instance colour is blended through an artist-painted alpha mask; LOD switches at 20, 60 and 100 character radii; 9,547 characters at ~34 fps in 160 instanced draws instead of 59,726 (Bryan Dudash, NVIDIA) — [GPU Gems 3, ch. 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch02.html)

Web / three.js specifically
- [Third-party showcase, 19 Feb 2026] A three.js crowd draws every character from one merged geometry in a single InstancedMesh; a per-vertex part ID lets the shader move head, body and legs; LOD is done in the vertex shader (far characters get fewer moving parts, cheaper lighting, merged limbs; characters facing away drop LOD sooner; distant ones update less often); colours and flags are bit-packed into minimal instance attributes; eyes, stripes, buttons and blinking are generated in the shader; the author reports up to 100,000 people at under 2 ms GPU time on their machine — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/one-draw-call-massive-crowd-performance-engineering-in-three-js/89928)
- [Third-party] Babylon.js ships a helper for instanced meshes whose animation is baked to vertex-animation textures; no first-party three.js equivalent surfaced (excerpt only) — [Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/instancer-treating-babylon-lite-instances-like-real-application-objects/63787)

- [Tool guidance, 2026] For top-down strategy views flipbook impostors are unsuitable (they are designed for side viewing); a remeshed proxy is preferred as the last LOD — [Xbox Game Dev Blog](https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2026/05/optimize-3d-models-for-strategy-games-with-simplygon/)

### Inferences
- For a WebGL2 scene with code-built soldiers, the two sourced routes are (a) the three.js showcase's "one merged geometry + part IDs + shader-side animation and LOD", and (b) VAT-style baking. Because the figures here are already animated procedurally, their gait and attack cycles could be evaluated once at start-up, written into a float data texture, and played back per instance with an animation index and frame offset — the 2007 layout still describes the minimal per-instance record needed.
- The cheapest high-yield anti-clone measures, ranked by how often sources use them: per-instance animation phase offset; per-instance tint/hue through a mask (so cloth varies but bronze does not); 2–4 swappable pieces (helmet, shield, weapon, banner) permuted at spawn; scale/height range; placement jitter and facing noise inside the formation; shuffled rather than row-by-row state changes; shader-computed "waves" (a charge ripple, a shield-raise, a spear-lowering) keyed by instance ID and distance from a front line.
- Grouped behaviour reads as intelligence at distance (DW Origins' leader-following and "large corps", Space Marine 2's swarm-then-separate). A few formation-level states with staggered per-soldier timing will look more alive than more individual animations.
- An angled or top-down strategy camera weakens billboard/flipbook impostors; a very low-poly proxy mesh with baked colour is the safer far LOD.
- Crowd readability needs an explicit overlay or colour discipline (DW Origins' zone icons); density alone makes the player's own figures disappear.

## 4. Combat effects craft: trails, sparks, flashes, dust, debris, blood, fire, smoke, volleys, shockwaves

### Takeaway
Effects artists consistently reduce the craft to a hierarchy (one primary, readable element; quiet secondary elements), a three-beat non-linear lifetime (brief anticipation, a peak inside the first quarter, fast erosion), mid-range values with the brightest value reserved for the instant of impact, and short lifetimes to avoid clutter. Shipping 2025–2026 action games are still criticised when spectacle overwhelms readability.

### Cited Findings

Principles (value, colour, shape, timing)
- [Dev guide, summarised 22 Oct 2026] Riot's League of Legends VFX style guide: primary elements get "high color values, clear silhouettes, high opacity, and a sharp focal point"; secondary elements (embers, mist) use lower value, lower opacity, blurred simple shapes and subtle motion — [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [Same] Avoid absolute black and pure white; paint the body of an effect in mid-range values and save the top value spike for the instantaneous flash; environments stay low-saturation as a "readable floor" while effects get "a wider, higher saturation ceiling" — [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [Same] Complementary schemes need a "strict dominant-to-submissive ratio" with the accent colour desaturated and small; "Don't stack or layer too many alpha shapes"; hand-painted shapes mixing soft and hard edges beat photographic noise — [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [Same] Timing: a rapid, brief wind-up; impact at peak value and saturation "fully active within the first quarter of the lifespan"; then a smooth erosion; "linear timing curves ruin gameplay immersion"; lingering particles cause "severe Overdraw Costs" — [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [Same] Movement: the leading tip of a projectile should carry "the highest brightness value and the sharpest contrast boundaries"; effect size should scale with the attack's importance — [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [Educator, 5 Dec 2023] "Shapes are the primary way to communicate to the gamer"; "values are the best way to get a player's attention"; particles should dissipate "immediately after their intended communication task is finished" — [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/five-artistic-principles-gaming-vfx)
- [Dev analysis, 21 Oct 2024] Halo Infinite and God of War Ragnarök: effects are identified by shape profile even with similar palettes; each element owns a colour family; "Fast motion can often imply activity or reactivity"; explosions scale on a logarithmic curve (fast growth, then linger so the final size registers); "VFX supporting core mechanics should often be unambiguous in all settings"; a weapon's own flash should seldom cover the aim point (Antonio Gil Herrera) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vfx-as-game-design-tools-the-ludology-of-vfx-in-god-of-war-ragnarok-and-halo-infinite)
- [Dev] A Creative Assembly effects artist says fine timing changes often separate a mediocre effect from a great one (excerpt only; page returned 429) — [Creative Assembly](https://creative-assembly.com/node/4446)

Construction techniques
- [Artist, Background 2018] Build effects from three phases — anticipation (fade-in, length set by design), climax ("Biggest Contrast, High Saturation", most elements visible), dissipation ("Low Contrast, Low Opacity", kept short). "Use particle systems only when you must": prefer meshes with scrolling textures. Keep flipbooks small and use motion-vector blending to need fewer frames. Drive colour from a 1–4 colour gradient lookup; pack emission and alpha in separate channels; use vertex alpha to fade edges (Stefan Jevremović, Nordeus) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/vfx-staples-shape-color-and-motion/)
- [Artist, Background 2019] Sword trail: an animation-driven trail between two points taken from 3–4 extra virtual bones on the weapon; two trails merged (a bright core plus a wider veil); "Panning only won't do the trick" — panning speed and intensity are animated by curves; the trail appears as the swing gains momentum and fades as it dies. Sparks start elongated and shrink to dots. Impact layers: ground cracks with energy, rock debris and ground dust, a white shockwave plus one in the accent colour, and two spark emitters (one spreading horizontally, one that rises, stops and floats). Clutter is avoided before the hit so the impact lands harder (Guillermo Rossell) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/trail-impact-vfx-production)
- [Third-party forum advice] Trail erosion is keyed to begin about 20% into each trail quad's life so the ribbon dissolves along its length rather than uniformly; impact sparks should "spurt out faster and disappear quick", with a ring or secondary element added to the flash (excerpts only; exact thread attribution not verified) — [RealtimeVFX thread A](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=6), [RealtimeVFX thread B](https://realtimevfx.com/t/erik-forsstrom-sketch-14-wip/5167)

What 2025–2026 engines add
- [Dev] Ghost of Yōtei: GPU particles sample terrain material, deformation and water flow; a "cut buffer" written by weapon sweeps cuts grass and flowers and spawns particles; fights knock snow off trees as particles; conservative rasterisation keeps small particles stable; god rays come from a light-space cloud shadow map in volumetric fog — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [Reviewer] Crimson Desert: debris varies with force (a sword produces shards, a great hammer larger chunks); fire spreads from wooden structures to grass; desert fights raise dust and sand plumes; siege debris persists for the rest of the battle — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/crimson-desert-30-brilliant-details-that-elevate-the-entire-experience)
- [Dev, Background 2018] Total War physically simulates "every projectile fired by each individual soldier", with accuracy varying by shooter quality — the basis for arrow volleys that look non-uniform — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)
- [Dev, Background 2019] Total War: Three Kingdoms replaced particle sorting with order-independent transparency — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026177/Instancing-and-Order-Independent-Transparency)

Reception of recent Chinese action titles
- [Reviewer] Phantom Blade Zero (Oct 2026): "flashy animations and colors that turn every fight into a vivid, almost theatrical spectacle"; weapon-specific signature effects (one blade spawns red lilies on the ground with each heavy attack); but boss effects are "sometimes overwhelming" (14 Nov 2026) — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/phantom-blade-zero-review/)
- [Reviewer] Phantom Blade Zero's lighting: Digital Foundry's Sept 2025 hands-on reported baked lighting with ray-traced shadows and reflections and no Lumen or virtual shadow maps — [Twisted Voxel](https://twistedvoxel.com/digital-foundry-analyzes-phantom-blade-performance-and-visuals-discusses-potential-ps5-version/); contradicted by a Nov 2026 analysis stating the release uses Nanite, Lumen and ray tracing — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=656112). The two refer to different builds; neither is a developer statement.
- [Reviewer] Motion blur in Phantom Blade Zero produces visible artefacts when combined with frame generation (excerpt only) — [DSOGaming](https://dsogaming.com/pc-performance-analyses/phantom-blade-zero-benchmarks-pc-performance-analysis/)

### Inferences
- A dark, drained scene is the easiest case for the value rule: keep smoke, dust and mist in the low-mid range, let bronze-gold and firelight own the upper-mid range, and reserve near-white for a one-to-three-frame hit flash and spark cores. Sparks and embers are the natural "accent colour" and should stay small and sparse.
- A sourced layering recipe for a weapon clash: restrained trail (core + veil, eroding from the tail) → single-frame flash with a ring → fast, short-lived elongated sparks that shrink → slower dust/debris and a few floating embers that outlast everything else. Each later layer is dimmer, slower and lower-contrast than the one before.
- Scale effect size and lifetime with the importance of the event (ordinary soldier hits get sparks only; a general's decisive blow gets the full stack) so the war-theatre view does not fill with overlapping transparent quads.
- For hundreds of simultaneous small effects, mesh-and-scrolling-texture or instanced quads with a gradient lookup are cheaper and more controllable than many particle systems, and short lifetimes limit overdraw — the performance argument and the readability argument point the same way.
- Arrow volleys look convincing when each arrow has slightly different launch angle and timing (per-shooter spread), not when a block of arrows moves as one object.

### Gaps
- No primary breakdown (talk, interview or artist post) was found for the combat effects of Black Myth: Wukong, Wuchang: Fallen Feathers, Phantom Blade Zero or Dynasty Warriors: Origins. A Chinese-language search surfaced a third-party phase analysis of a Wukong finisher (gather → larger faster burst → outward shockwave, with emphasis on dynamic lights) but the specific article could not be identified or opened, so it is not cited.
- Nothing sourced this round on distortion/refraction, soft (depth-faded) particles, lit particles or lit smoke, hit-flash shaders, blood decals, or shockwave construction. The query on lit-smoke techniques and the GDC 2026 VFX-track query were not run (search budget).
- No source found on dust and debris tuned for crowds (per-soldier vs. per-formation dust).

## 5. Camera and lens in duels and finishers; accessibility guidance

### Takeaway
Sourced craft rules for finisher cameras in 2024–2026 are thin; what is well documented is the accessibility side. Platform guidance and shipped settings menus converge on: every lens effect (motion blur, shake, depth of field, film grain, vignette) individually switchable or on a 0–100% slider, cinematic and gameplay depth of field separable, and flashes kept below three per second and below about a fifth of the screen.

### Cited Findings

Accessibility guidance
- [Platform guidance, page dated 13 Nov 2026] "Avoid the use of camera shake, camera bobbing effects, motion blur, mouse blur, and more or provide an option to turn off these behaviors." Halo Infinite is cited for separate 0–100% sliders for radial blur, screen shake, full-screen effects and speed lines; Cyberpunk 2077 for adjustable "additive camera motions"; adjustable field of view and the ability to disable automatic camera movement are also listed — [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)
- [Platform guidance, page dated 13 Nov 2026] A flash is a 10% luminance change with the darker value below 0.8; failure is approximately more than three flashes per second covering approximately 20% or more of the screen; saturated red flashes fail at lower contrast; about 1 in 4,000 people are affected and "eliminating game content that can potentially cause photosensitive seizures is preferred over splash-screen warnings". Fixes: reduce contrast, frequency and area — [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)

Shipped settings (2024–2026)
- [Dev] Dragon Age: The Veilguard (2024): motion blur on/off; camera shake 0–100 (does not apply to cinematics); depth of field with four choices — cinematics only, gameplay only, both, off; vignette on/off; low-health screen effect on/off — [EA accessibility portal](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/dragon-age-the-veilguard)
- [Dev-shipped settings] Phantom Blade Zero (Oct 2026): Motion Blur, Camera Shake and Film Grain Intensity under Visuals; separate Depth of Field Quality and Motion Blur Quality under Details — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/phantom-blade-zero-pc-performance-benchmarks/)
- [Dev] Assassin's Creed Shadows (2025): field of view adjustable from 85% to 115%; motion blur toggle — [Ubisoft Tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/ar-ae/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ/assassins-creed-shadows-tech-qa)

Framing and staging practice
- [Dev] Ghost of Yōtei classifies cutscenes A, B or C by cost and staging density, and reserves dedicated lighting and elaborate camera work for Class A; the gameplay camera was pulled back slightly to show wide vistas (GDC 2026, reported 10 Mar 2026) — [4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/840/G084044/20260310041/)

- [Reviewer] Phantom Blade Zero: some finishing moves are framed awkwardly, with parts of the environment obscuring the shot (Creative Bloq review as relayed; excerpt only) — [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/reviews/phantom-blade-zero-review-dark-wuxia-martial-arts-masters-with-an-apprentices-execution)
- [Reviewer] Ghost of Yōtei forces a CinemaScope aspect ratio during horse riding with no toggle, which one reviewer found limiting (excerpt only) — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/ghost-of-yotei-review/amp/)
- [Reviewer] Per-object motion blur is singled out as lifting Ghost of Yōtei's combat presentation — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=628528)
- [Dev] Dynasty Warriors: Origins: the camera pulls back as soldier numbers rise, at the cost of losing the player character; an overhead tactical view ("Eyes of the Sacred Bird") is offered for issuing orders — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202407/10868), [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=396742)
- [Dev, date not verified] An Unreal Engine tech blog on a third-person action camera applies a combat modifier of increased camera distance and stronger depth of field, and enters slow motion with a camera snap while the player selects a target; it credits John Nesky's "50 Camera Mistakes" talk (excerpt only; page returned 403) — [Unreal Engine tech blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/six-ingredients-for-a-dynamic-third-person-camera)
- [Dev, Background 2019] God of War (2018) built its presentation on a no-cut camera (Dori Arazi, Sony Santa Monica, GDC 2019) — [GameAnim](https://www.gameanim.com/2021/08/17/creating-a-deeper-emotional-connection-the-cinematography-of-god-of-war/)
- [Dev, Background 2018] Total War treats rare matched-animation moments between specific units as something meant to "feel special" (Al Bickham) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)

### Inferences
- For a cinematic cavalry duel the defensible settings design is: individual toggles or sliders for shake, motion blur, depth of field, film grain and vignette; a "reduce motion" preset that also removes slow-motion ramps and camera snaps; hit flashes limited to one brief flash per impact, well under three per second, small in screen area and never saturated red; and impact colour that leans to warm white/gold rather than red.
- The reviewer complaints give two concrete framing rules for finishers: guarantee a clear line of sight (cull or fade occluders, or pick the camera side by a visibility test) and use the finisher as a pacing breath — a held, clean, simpler image after dense action — rather than adding more effects on top.
- Depth of field is most useful, and least risky, in the close duel shot (it hides the lack of micro-detail on code-built background soldiers and isolates the two riders) and should be off or minimal in the army view, where the player needs to read everything. The Veilguard split (cinematics vs. gameplay) is the model.
- Per-object motion blur on fast-moving weapons and horse legs sells speed more safely than full-camera blur, which is the effect accessibility guidance most often asks to disable.
- Reserving elaborate camera work for a few "Class A" moments (Yōtei) suits a one-developer project: one well-staged duel finisher is worth more than many variations.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 primary source (talk or interview) was found giving explicit cutting rules, lens lengths, depth-of-field settings or slow-motion ratios for duels and finishers. Queries on Ghost of Yōtei's standoff presentation and Total War: Three Kingdoms' duel cameras were not run (search budget); the Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 developer interview that discusses its cinematic combat camera returned 403.
- No source found on lens flares or anamorphic effects in recent action titles, nor on film-grain tuning for dark scenes.
- Accessibility guidance found does not address slow motion or time dilation directly.

## 6. Code-built figures: precedents, techniques for adding detail to primitives, and where loaded skinned models become necessary

### Takeaway
No 2024–2026 shipped game was found that builds *realistic* humans or horses purely from primitives or signed-distance shapes; the precedents are stylised (code-animated creatures, shader-detailed instanced crowds) and they succeed by leaning on motion, silhouette and shader-generated surface detail. Everything in the sources that reads as close-up realism — muscle, mocap weight, scanned materials — comes from captured or authored data.

### Cited Findings
- [Third-party showcase, 19 Feb 2026] A three.js crowd whose characters are one merged primitive-style geometry with per-vertex part IDs; surface detail (eyes, stripes, buttons, blinking) is generated procedurally in the shader with no extra geometry or draw calls; distant figures merge limbs and use cheaper lighting — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/one-draw-call-massive-crowd-performance-engineering-in-three-js/89928)
- [Dev, 25 Sep 2025] Rain World: The Watcher: the player creature is two spherical chunks locked at a fixed distance, with limbs and tail as "cosmetic additions drawn on top of this base" and animated procedurally from input; most creatures are built almost entirely from scripts; the stated reason: "since code is how you make things move in a computer game, that's what I did"; believability is credited mainly to emergent behaviour rather than fidelity — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)
- [Dev tool, 2025] Unreal 5.6's experimental Locomotor node generates walking procedurally and has been used by artists for stylised creatures (a pigeon, a cricket-like enemy; article titles and excerpts only, pages not opened) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/goofy-little-pigeon-animated-with-ue5-6-s-locomotor), [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/testing-cricket-like-creature-animation-with-unreal-engine-5-6-s-locomotor)
- [Third-party student work, 2025] A fully procedural IK quadruped (one leg per side lifted at a time, spline foot paths, heightmap adaptation) — [Texas A&M course project](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/sueda/courses/CSCE450/2025S/projects/David_Herring/index.html)
- [Dev, solo developer] Manor Lords' realistic look was built by one developer in Unreal Engine 4 using photogrammetry, motion capture and pre-calculated destruction, (excerpt only) — [Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/manor-lords-solo-developer-s-highly-anticipated-medieval-strategy-game-hits-this-month); the developer also says he had to buy scan libraries early in development and that the engine's automatic LOD generation "works like a charm" (Greg Styczeń, April 2024; excerpt only, interview page returned 403) — [Unreal Engine interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/solo-dev-makes-sophisticated-sim-manor-lords-using-unreal-engine)
- [Dev] High-end realism relies on captured or learned data: ~6,300 authored horse motions in RDR2 — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse); machine-learned muscle deformation in the Witcher 4 demo — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=405351); a real horse in a motion-capture suit for KCD2 (excerpt only) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/rpg/if-you-liked-red-dead-redemption-2s-ultra-realistic-horses-kingdom-come-deliverance-2-devs-also-used-a-real-horse-in-the-motion-capture-studio)
- [Reviewer] Phantom Blade Zero's most praised quality is its motion-captured martial-arts animation ("beyond impressive"), not its model detail — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/phantom-blade-zero-review/)
- [Background 2007] Variety from few parts: characters split into pieces with random permutations at load and a per-instance colour applied through a painted mask — [GPU Gems 3, ch. 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch02.html)
- [Dev, Background 2016] At strategy distance, shape, form and distinct silhouettes carry readability — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/making-i-civilization-vi-i-both-playable-and-beautiful)
- [Tool guidance, 2026] Low-poly strategy assets can be reduced hard, but must stay welded/connected so they do not "fall apart at very low triangle counts" — [Xbox Game Dev Blog](https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2026/05/optimize-3d-models-for-strategy-games-with-simplygon/)

### Inferences
- The viable ceiling for code-built figures is "convincing at mid and far distance, stylised-but-coherent at 5 m". Sources show motion quality, silhouette and state changes dominate perception at distance (sections 1–3), and those are all achievable in code; what is not achievable without assets is facial performance, hand articulation, anatomically correct muscle and skin sliding, and mocap-grade weight in bespoke combat choreography.
- Detail-adding techniques that the sources support for primitive-built figures: shader-generated surface pattern keyed by part ID (the three.js example's stripes and buttons generalise to lamellar rows, trim bands, rivets and cloth weave as procedural normal/roughness perturbation); per-instance tint through a mask; swappable silhouette pieces (crest, plume, shield, banner, weapon); and shared wind/wetness/mud parameters.
- Additional techniques that are standard practice but were *not* sourced in this round and should be treated as unverified suggestions: baking ambient occlusion into vertex colours at build time, contact-shadow blobs under hooves and feet, decal-style projected dirt and blood, and bevelled or lofted profiles to catch rim light in a low-key scene.
- Loaded skinned models (or at least captured motion data) become necessary when the camera holds a single figure at roughly 5 m or closer for more than a beat, when faces must act, or when a duel needs many distinct, weighty exchanges; a middle path consistent with the sources is to keep code-built bodies and import only motion data or a small set of authored key poses.
- For a dark, mist-heavy art direction the low-key lighting itself hides missing micro-detail; rim light, silhouette against fog and firelit bronze highlights let lofted shapes read as armour without modelled plates.

### Gaps
- No example was found, for 2024–2026, of a shipped game or public demo rendering convincing realistic humans or horses from primitives or signed-distance fields; the SDF-specific and miniature/low-poly-style queries (e.g. Thronefall, Bad North, Diplomacy is Not an Option, Tiny Glade) were not run because the search budget was exhausted, so absence here is weak evidence.
- No source found that states the distance or shot length at which primitive-built figures stop being acceptable; the threshold above is inference.
- No sourced guidance found on normal-mapped armour plates, vertex-colour occlusion or decals specifically for procedural figures.
