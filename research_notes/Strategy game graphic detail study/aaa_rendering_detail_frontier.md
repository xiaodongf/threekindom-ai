## 1. What rendering features defined the visual-detail leaders shipped 2024 to November 2026?

### Takeaway

The leaders converged on one recipe: fully dynamic global illumination (ray-traced on consoles by 2025, path-traced only as a high-end PC option), virtualized or micropolygon geometry with no visible level-of-detail pops, simulated weather and wind, and strand hair. The titles ranked highest by analysts were the ones that delivered this at a stable frame rate with a clean image (DOOM: The Dark Ages at 60 fps, GTA VI at a locked 30), not the ones with the most features switched on.

#### DOOM: The Dark Ages (id Tech 8, May 2025) — the most completely documented case

- [DEV] id's stated requirements: "Performance > all else – Stable >= 60hz on all platforms", faster iteration, 4x to 10x bigger levels than DOOM Eternal, 22 levels versus 13. — [Tiago Sousa, "Fast as Hell: idTech 8 Global Illumination", SIGGRAPH 2025 slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] Why baked lighting was dropped: with the old path-traced lightmap pipeline the game would have needed about 44 to 110 GB of disk, about 9.5 to 24 TB of network storage, and a full re-bake turnaround of about 27 to 68 days. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf); also reported by [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/doom-the-dark-ages/ray-tracing-110gb-disk-space)
- [DEV] The real-time GI design: probes in cascaded irradiance volumes (16x16x16 x 6 cascades, 64/32/16 rays per probe per frame, only one cascade plus one local volume updated per frame); ray hits shaded into a spatially hashed world radiance cache (25 cm cells, 14 MB, about 20k entries updated per frame); a final gather of 1 ray per pixel at half or quarter resolution that reads a screen-space cache first, then the world cache, then the irradiance volumes; spatial bilateral denoise, 4-tap bilateral upscale, temporal filter. Result stored as 2-band spherical harmonics per pixel "to try maintain normal maps details". — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] Measured GI cost at a heavy vista: about 2.11 ms on Series S (900p), 1.9 ms on Series X (1440p), 2.05 ms on PS5 (1440p), 1.92 ms on PS5 Pro (1800p), 1.7 ms on a PC with an RTX 4080 (4K). — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] Reflections: screen-space reflections first, then ray tracing, falling back to reflection probes by smoothness. Ray-traced reflections were disabled on consoles for performance (about 2 ms) and run only on very smooth surfaces. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] Fine detail is added back in screen space: a "directional occlusion" pass "used for high frequency details" because the ray-tracing structures do not contain all geometry or parallax-mapped detail; it gives "contact shadows for every light" and specular occlusion. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] Billy Khan (id) said ray-traced GI made the game possible and gave artists a "what you see is what you get" workflow; path tracing is now part of id Tech 8 but is evaluated per game. — [Wccftech, citing the Digital Foundry interview](https://wccftech.com/doom-the-dark-ages-not-possible-ray-tracing/); [Nvidia](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/doom-the-dark-ages-path-tracing-dlss-ray-reconstruction-update/)
- [ANALYST, via secondary] Digital Foundry's first look noted that removing baked lighting helped dynamic objects blend into their surroundings. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/doom-the-dark-ages-id-tech-8-ray-tracing-vitualized-geometry/)

#### Assassin's Creed Shadows (Anvil, March 2025)

- [DEV] Anvil had used a baked GI pipeline since AC Unity; Shadows adds ray-traced GI so lighting follows time of day, seasons and destruction. Nicolas Lopez (Anvil technical architect): "Raytraced Global Illumination makes lighting fully reactive to gameplay." - [DEV] Geometry: "micropolygon" virtualized geometry; Lionel Berenguier: it "allows pixel-precise model quality similar to movie quality" with automatic distance reduction and no visible popping. — [Ubisoft, "Inside Anvil"](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6u0kflsdY20YlnZks7rUeG/inside-anvil-the-technology-powering-assassins-creed-shadows)
- [DEV] Weather and wind: "Atmos" is a physics-based simulation controlling rainfall, wind speed and direction, precipitation and cloud cover; vegetation is animated on the GPU by a wind system driven by a fluid simulation; puddles dry up; rain drips from eaves onto clothes and hair. — [Ubisoft, "Inside Anvil"](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6u0kflsdY20YlnZks7rUeG/inside-anvil-the-technology-powering-assassins-creed-shadows); [Ubisoft tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ/assassins-creed-shadows-tech-qa)
- [DEV] The hard cases for ray-traced GI in this setting were translucent geometry, thin walls, small windows and dense vegetation; ray-traced specular was added during the delay window. — [SIGGRAPH 2025 course page, "Ray Tracing the World of Assassin's Creed Shadows"](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/index.html)
- [DEV] The console 60 fps performance mode runs without ray tracing because 60 fps with ray tracing would have left too little GPU budget; on PC a "Selective" mode restricts ray tracing to the Hideout. — [Ubisoft tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ/assassins-creed-shadows-tech-qa)

#### Ghost of Yōtei (Sucker Punch, October 2025)

- [DEV] Lighting is a more automated baked model "augmented by short-range ray-traced global illumination"; ray tracing was spent on GI and not reflections because the setting has few mirror-like surfaces. ### Inferences

- (Inference) Across the leaders, the single shared reason the image reads as "detailed" is consistent indirect light on everything, static and moving, plus small-scale occlusion added on top. id says this outright (directional occlusion is there to restore high-frequency detail the GI cannot see), and the other leaders' first-party material leads with dynamic GI, not with texture resolution or polygon counts.
- (Inference) Reflections are the feature leaders cut first. id disabled ray-traced reflections on consoles, Sucker Punch skipped them because the setting has few mirror-like surfaces, Clair Obscur's console build fell back to screen-space reflections, and Indiana Jones kept screen-space water reflections even with path tracing. A Han-dynasty setting (matte earth, timber, cloth, lacquer, bronze) is in the same position as Yōtei.
- (Inference) The 2025 ranking order (a 60 fps game first, a visually heavier 30 fps-class game second) and the GTA VI verdict (praised image, criticized for no 60 fps option) suggest analysts now score "detail delivered cleanly and stably" above "maximum detail".

## 2. Which details do viewers actually notice, and which expensive details go unseen?

### Takeaway

The only controlled ranking I found puts indirect (bounce) light first and direct shadows a close second, with ambient occlusion and screen-space reflections last, and says occlusion mostly matters when bounce light is present. Developer and analyst practice from 2025 to 2026 agrees: spend on consistent indirect light, contact-scale shadowing and atmosphere; maximum-quality shadows, reflections on rough surfaces and "ultra" tiers are where cost goes unseen.

### Cited Findings

- [STUDY] (background, 2023) In an immersive VR study where participants spent a limited budget on rendering parameters to match a real scene, global illumination was rated the most important factor for perceived realism, closely followed by direct shadows; ambient occlusion and screen-space reflections were prioritized least. Participants insisted on highest-quality direct shadows; ambient occlusion was only noticeable when GI was also on, and GI's impact was higher with AO; perception of realism varied widely between people. — [Computers & Graphics, via ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cag.2023.10.021)
- [STUDY] (background) A V-Ray (offline) preference study found GI had no significant influence on participants' preference, so the VR result may not generalize. — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1388856/FULLTEXT02.pdf)
- [DEV] id adds a screen-space directional-occlusion pass specifically "for high frequency details", giving "contact shadows for every light" and specular occlusion, because coarse GI alone misses them. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] id's own high-versus-low GI comparison: "Relatively close, bit softer result on low quality. Loses some directional lighting details." ### Inferences

- (Inference) A working priority order for perceptual payoff, assembled from the study plus developer practice: (1) coherent indirect/ambient light on every object; (2) clean direct shadows, including contact-scale shadows where objects meet the ground; (3) atmosphere as a depth cue (fog layers, light shafts, cloud shadows); (4) material response variation (roughness breakup, wetness, skin scattering in close-ups); (5) ambient motion and reaction (wind, cloth, particles catching light); (6) sharp reflections, last, unless the scene has water or polished surfaces. Items 1 to 3 are the ones every leader funds; item 6 is the one they cut.
- (Inference) The "goes unseen" list is consistent: the top quality tier of an effect that is already present (ultra shadows, ultra AO, full-resolution GI), reflections on rough materials, and ray-traced versions of effects in dark scenes. id's own result (quarter-resolution GI looks "relatively close") supports rendering soft effects at reduced resolution.
- (Inference) Close-up scenes change the ranking. Skin, hair and micro-detail were singled out in titles with long character close-ups (Death Stranding 2, GTA VI). For this project the cavalry duel is the close-up scene, while the campaign map and war theater are distance scenes where atmosphere, density and motion matter more than micro-normal detail.
- (Inference) The study's note that ambient occlusion only reads when bounce light is present argues for adding a cheap ambient/bounce term before investing in a heavier occlusion pass.

### Gaps

- I found no published ranking or perceptual study specific to games from 2024 to 2026, and no Digital Foundry or GDC statement giving an explicit ordered list. The ranking above is my synthesis.
- No direct evidence on micro-normal detail, lit particles or foliage wind as perceptual factors; they appear only as things developers chose to build.
- Natalya Tatarchuk's "Game and Rendering Trends, a 2026 Take" (SIGGRAPH 2026) and her 2025 retrospective on "player perspectives" may address this directly, but the course pages publish no abstracts for them and I did not locate slides. — [SIGGRAPH 2026 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/index.html)

### Inferences

- (Inference) Almost nothing on the 2026 frontier transfers directly to a WebGL2 renderer: hardware ray tracing, virtualized geometry, ML upscalers and neural materials all need capabilities WebGL2 lacks. What transfers is the architecture of the cheaper tiers: probe grids for indirect light, screen-space passes for fine occlusion, soft effects computed at half or quarter resolution and upsampled with depth awareness, reflection probes instead of traced reflections, and froxel or screen-space fog. id's deck is effectively a recipe for those tiers.
- (Inference) The industry's 2026 work is mostly about cost and noise reduction (Lumen medium mode, MegaLights noise, VRRT, ORCA, the PSSR redesign). That is a signal that the leaders consider the amount of detail sufficient and stability the open problem.
- (Inference) The DLSS 5 reaction is evidence that audiences punish detail that overrides authored art direction. For a stylized "dark myth" look, any automatic enhancement (sharpening, tone, added contrast) should stay subordinate to the authored palette.
- (Inference) Sony's PSSR lesson generalizes beyond ML: use exact closed-form or analytic solutions where they exist and reserve the expensive or approximate machinery for what cannot be computed directly. Procedural geometry is well placed for this, because occlusion, curvature and height are known at build time.

## 4. The 2025 to 2026 backlash, and the lessons drawn about detail versus clarity versus performance

### Takeaway

The stated lessons are: set the performance target first and fit the detail to it; optimize for the low end early; drop a headline feature if it threatens stability; and treat a clean, stable image as part of visual quality. The games praised in this period (DOOM, Battlefield 6, Arc Raiders, KCD2, Split Fiction) followed those rules; the ones punished (Monster Hunter Wilds, Borderlands 4, Wukong on PS5) did not.

### Inferences

- (Inference) The transferable rules for a small browser game: (1) fix the frame-time target per scene before adding detail; (2) test on the weakest target device from the start; (3) prefer stable, spatially filtered effects over ones that need many frames of history, since WebGL2 has no ML reconstruction to hide noise; (4) keep the final image at native resolution and put only soft effects (ambient occlusion, fog, bloom, bounce light) at reduced resolution; (5) compile and warm shaders behind a loading moment, because a hitch on first use of a material is the browser equivalent of shader stutter; (6) offer a small number of honest quality presets instead of an "ultra" tier that costs a lot and shows little.
- (Inference) "Denoising is sharing neighbours' results" implies that any stochastic effect added to a WebGL2 scene carries a hidden blur or ghosting cost. Effects that can be computed exactly at build time for procedural geometry avoid that cost entirely.
- (Inference) The TAA complaint is strongest at low resolution and in motion. A strategy game with a slow, mostly static camera is far more tolerant of temporal accumulation than a shooter, so temporally accumulated AO or soft shadows are lower risk on the campaign map and city diorama than in the duel or battle scenes.

## 6. For each major technique, the cheap approximation and how close it gets

### Takeaway

Every frontier technique has a baked, screen-space or texture-trick counterpart that the leaders themselves still use as a fallback tier, and for a slow-camera scene with fixed geometry those counterparts get most of the visible result. The honest limits are: screen-space methods cannot see anything off screen, baked methods cannot react to change, and three.js's newest screen-space GI nodes target the WebGPU renderer, not the classic WebGLRenderer.

### Cited Findings

#### Indirect light / global illumination

- [DEV] Frontier: per-pixel ray-traced final gather over world caches (id), ray-traced GI (Anvil, RAGE). Cheap tier used by the same studios: baked lightmaps and irradiance probe volumes (id Tech 7: probes path-traced offline, 2-band spherical harmonics, per-vertex lookup blending the 8 closest probes); "automated baked lighting" with only short-range RTGI on top (Sucker Punch); Anvil's baked GI pipeline in use since AC Unity. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201); [Ubisoft tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ/assassins-creed-shadows-tech-qa)
- [DEV] What baking cannot do, per id: leaks, seams, banding and resolution limits; any lighting or geometry change forces a re-bake; dynamic objects need a separate system. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [DEV] Voxel GI as a mid tier: CryEngine's SVOGI ran (with fewer voxels) on Switch. — [GamePro](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-clevere-grafiktechnik,3427400.html)
- [three.js] `MeshStandardMaterial` supports `lightMap` (pre-baked illuminance) and `aoMap` (red channel darkens ambient light), both sampled from a second UV set. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html)
- [three.js] three.js ships a screen-space GI node (`SSGINode`; medium preset 3 slices and 8 steps, high preset 4 slices and 12 steps) and a GTAO node, but these are node-system addons used with `WebGPURenderer`, which falls back to a WebGL2 backend when WebGPU is unavailable. The community `realism-effects` library offers SSGI for the classic pipeline, with a v2 rewrite in progress. — [SSGINode docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SSGINode.html); [GTAONode docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GTAONode.html); [three.js AO example](https://threejs.org/examples/webgpu_postprocessing_ao); [realism-effects](https://www.npmjs.com/package/realism-effects)
- [Community] Limit of screen-space GI: it only sees what is in the depth buffer, so off-screen and occluded geometry contributes nothing. — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/ssgi-screen-space-global-illumination/85190)
- [ANALYST] How close the cheap tier gets: TechSpot found ray-traced versus screen-space "High" negligible in a Forza Horizon 6 night scene; Wukong's software Lumen gives "some of the feeling of path tracing". — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/3127-forza-horizon-6-optimization/); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/computing/black-myth-wukong-pc-performance-best-settings)

#### Ambient occlusion and contact shadows

- [DEV] Frontier use: id ray-marches the depth buffer along the hemisphere, stores the average unoccluded direction, denoises and temporally filters it, and uses it for contact shadows for every light and for specular occlusion. This is itself a screen-space technique. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [three.js] `GTAOPass` is an official post-processing addon for the classic pipeline; N8AO is a community screen-space AO pass with a half-resolution mode and depth-aware upsampling. — [GTAOPass docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GTAOPass.html); [N8AO](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao)
- [DEV] (background) Screen-space contact shadows: Bend Studio's technique, first used for sun shadows in Days Gone (2019), complements shadow maps by capturing small details that are otherwise hard to get without ray tracing. Unreal exposes the same idea as "Contact Shadows". — [Bend Studio](https://www.bendstudio.com/blog/inside-bend-screen-space-shadows/); [Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/contact-shadows-in-unreal-engine)
- [Community] Contact shadows can also be approximated from bent normals; a web 3D viewer documents a screen-space contact shadow effect. — [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/blogs/entry/2270222-approximated-contact-shadows-from-bent-normals); [iJewel3D docs](https://docs.ijewel3d.com/playground/screen-space-contact-shadows.html)
- [ANALYST, via secondary] How much to spend: the highest screen-space AO/GI tier in Battlefield 6 costs a lot for minimal visual return. — [OpenCritic news](https://opencritic.com/news/22194/these-two-battlefield-6-settings-are-killing-your-performance)

#### Reflections and specular response

- [DEV] Cheap tier used at the frontier: screen-space reflections first, then pre-filtered reflection probes (box-projected cube maps), chosen by surface smoothness. id's "re-fit" trick keeps static probes believable: Reflections = (Reflections / Probe Irradiance) x Frame Irradiance, which removes glowing corners and picks up local bounce colour. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [ANALYST, via secondary] Shipping examples of the fallback: Indiana Jones' water uses screen-space reflections; Clair Obscur's console build uses screen-space reflections instead of Lumen reflections; Death Stranding 2 mixes screen-space reflections with other methods. — [ixbt.games](https://ixbt.games/news/2024/12/16/digital-foundry-izucila-trassirovku-puti-na-pk-v-indiana-jones-and-the-great-circle.html); [PPE.pl](https://www.ppe.pl/news/368773/clair-obscur-expedition-33-imponuje-takze-pod-katem-technicznym-konsolowe-wersje-pod-lupa-digital-foundry.html); [ixbt.games](https://ixbt.games/news/2025/06/24/grafika-kak-v-texnodemke-digital-foundry-razobrali-death-stranding-2.html)
- [three.js] `envMap` on standard materials is pre-filtered internally with `PMREMGenerator`; `roughnessMap` and `metalnessMap` give per-pixel specular variation. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html)

#### Geometry detail

- [DEV] Frontier: Nanite, Anvil micropolygons, compute tessellation. Cheap tier still used: far terrain as baked models with detailed terrain materials stored in textures (Sucker Punch); parallax occlusion mapping with screen-space occlusion restoring its contact shading (id); tessellation with a runtime displacement buffer for snow (Sucker Punch). — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201); [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [three.js] `normalMap`, `bumpMap` (grayscale height that changes lighting only) and `displacementMap` (moves vertices) are available on standard materials. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html)
- [DEV] Density through GPU instancing and culling: over a million items culled to about 60,000 per frame. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)

#### Volumetric fog, light shafts, clouds

- [DEV] Frontier: froxel volumetric fog lit by the GI volumes (id, after Wronski 2014); god rays from a light-space cloud shadow map with artist-placed "god ray targets"; cloud maps storing average depth for parallax (Sucker Punch). — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [three.js] Options on the web: the official `GodRaysShader` addon (screen-space rays combined additively with an intensity control); `three-good-godrays` (screen-space, shadow-map based; its README warns that `PCFSoftShadowMap` is being deprecated/removed from three.js r183 onward); `three-volumetric-pass-fork` (ray-marched screen-space volumetrics); an official WebGPU volumetric lighting example with ray-march step and denoise controls. — [GodRaysShader docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/module-GodRaysShader.html); [three-good-godrays README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three-good-godrays@0.12.0/README.md); [three-volumetric-pass-fork](https://www.npmjs.com/package/three-volumetric-pass-fork); [three.js volume lighting example](https://threejs.org/examples/webgpu_volume_lighting)
- [Community] The cheapest version: a three.js forest showcase used fake light shafts drawn as a single instanced mesh placed in gaps between trees. A community project offers "game ready" volumetric clouds for three.js. — [three.js forum, forest](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/forest-environment-built-with-three-js-and-webgpu/96126); [three.js forum, clouds](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/volumetric-clouds-game-ready/86598)
- [DEV, via search summary; new in UE 5.8] Epic itself added "Fog Screen Space Scattering" (Experimental), a screen-space approximation for fog glow that works with Volumetric Fog and Local Fog Volumes. I could not open the pages; this comes from search-result summaries of them. — [Epic release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/06/see-5-key-features-for-cg-artists-in-unreal-engine-5-8/)

#### Wetness and weather response

- [DEV] Frontier: simulated weather driving puddles that dry, rain dripping from eaves (Anvil); wet, bloody, muddy and snowy states applied through a directional grid around the character (Sucker Punch). — [Ubisoft, "Inside Anvil"](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6u0kflsdY20YlnZks7rUeG/inside-anvil-the-technology-powering-assassins-creed-shadows); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [DEV] (background) The standard cheap wet look: darken (and saturate) the base colour and raise smoothness; Sébastien Lagarde notes most games darken diffuse and boost specular, and that rough wet surfaces look darker. Unity's production weather sample does exactly this in a "Wet" subgraph. — [fxguide, "Game environments Part C: making wet environments"](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=43362); [Unity Shader Graph weather sample](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.shadergraph@17.0/manual/Shader-Graph-Sample-Production-Ready-Weather.html); physical basis (background, 1999): [MIT wet materials paper](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/graphics/pubs/wet_materials_egwr99.pdf)

#### Hair, cloth, skin

- [DEV] Frontier: strand hair as the only hair solution at 60 Hz (Indiana Jones); multi-layer GPU cloth (Yōtei); ReSTIR path-traced plus diffusion subsurface scattering, developed because diffusion-only scattering shows artifacts in thin regions such as ears and nostrils (Nvidia). — [SIGGRAPH 2025 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/index.html); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [ANALYST, via secondary] What reviewers noticed was the result, not the method: skin scattering, pores and micro-detail in Death Stranding 2. — [DTF](https://dtf.ru/hard/3851866-obzor-death-stranding-2-grafika-ps5)

#### Particles, transparency, small sparkle

- [DEV] Frontier: particles and glass lit from a froxel irradiance volume so they match scene lighting (id); GPU particles sampling terrain material and water flow (Yōtei); order-independent transparency (Activision). Cheap tricks used by the same studios: per-vertex particle lighting (id); snow sparkle from stable screen-space noise (Sucker Punch); a copy of the previous frame behind particles to hide a transition (Sucker Punch). — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201); [SIGGRAPH 2025 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/index.html)

#### Wind and interaction

- [DEV] Frontier: fluid-simulated wind animating vegetation on the GPU (Anvil); a "cut buffer" texture that grass samples to be cut; a displacement buffer for snow (Sucker Punch). Both of the latter are render-to-texture tricks, not simulation. — [Ubisoft tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ/assassins-creed-shadows-tech-qa); [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)

#### Resolution of soft effects, and image formats

- [DEV] Soft lighting effects are computed at half or quarter resolution and upsampled with normal- and depth-weighted (bilateral) filters; id judged the quarter-resolution result "relatively close". Epic designs Lumen around a 1080p internal resolution. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf); [Epic Lumen performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)
- [DEV] Low-precision HDR buffers show as colour shift and banding in lighting; dithering can hide banding. — [Sousa slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)

### Inferences

All of the following are my engineering inferences for a three.js r187 WebGLRenderer (WebGL2) game with procedural geometry. None is a tested recommendation, and "how close" is my estimate unless a cited finding above says otherwise.

| Frontier technique | Cheap approximation for this renderer | How close, and where it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Ray-traced / dynamic GI | Build-time ambient and bounce baked per vertex (or into a small lightmap) for static procedural meshes; a hemisphere or sky-ground ambient plus a few low-order probes for moving units; tint the ambient by time of day or weather | Close for fixed geometry under slow lighting change (the DOOM Eternal tier). Breaks when lights move or objects are destroyed; fire as the only warm source needs its own local light, since baked bounce will not follow it |
| Ray-traced AO, contact shadows | Build-time vertex AO for static geometry, plus one screen-space AO pass (GTAOPass or N8AO at half resolution), plus blob or decal shadows under units, plus a short screen-space shadow march for the key light in close-ups | Very close for a tilted map or diorama. Screen-space parts miss off-screen occluders and can shimmer on thin geometry; the top quality tier is not worth its cost |
| Ray-traced reflections | Pre-filtered environment map (PMREM) with per-pixel roughness variation; a planar or screen-space reflection only for water | Close in a matte Han-dynasty setting, where sharp reflections are rare. Breaks on large still water seen at grazing angles |
| Virtualized geometry | Instancing with distance-based density; normal, bump or parallax detail generated procedurally; baked far-distance impostors or textures for mountains | Close at strategy-camera distances. Breaks in the duel close-up, where silhouettes need real geometry |
| Volumetric fog and light shafts | Analytic height and distance fog in the shader; a few instanced additive shaft meshes or a screen-space god-ray pass; scrolling noise for mist layers | Reads well as a depth cue, which is its main job. Not physically correct at shaft edges; screen-space rays vanish when the light source leaves the screen |
| Weather and wetness | A global wetness uniform that darkens base colour and lowers roughness; puddle masks from procedural noise in low terrain | Close; this is what shipped games did for a decade. Lacks drips and drying fronts unless animated masks are added |
| Strand hair, simulated cloth | Hair and mane cards or shells with an anisotropic highlight; banners and cloaks with vertex-shader sine-plus-noise wind | Adequate at duel distance if the highlight and motion are right; visibly fake under slow-motion close-ups |
| Subsurface skin | Wrap lighting or a warm tint in the shadow terminator | Enough at the distances this game shows faces; only matters in the duel |
| Lit particles, transparency | Particles tinted by the scene's ambient and key-light colour (per vertex or per system), soft-particle depth fade, additive fire with emissive bloom | Close; the main giveaway is particles that ignore scene lighting, which tinting fixes |
| Foliage wind, ambient motion | Vertex-shader wind with per-instance phase; a small render target for trampling or scorch marks sampled by ground and grass shaders | Close; this is the same render-to-texture idea Sucker Punch uses for cutting and snow |
| ML upscaling, temporal reconstruction | Native-resolution main pass with MSAA or a light post AA; only soft effects at reduced resolution with depth-aware upsampling | Sharper than temporal methods at the cost of some edge shimmer; acceptable with a slow camera |

- (Inference) The cheapest high-payoff package, in order, for the "dark myth" look: baked or analytic ambient with occlusion; contact shadows under every unit and building; layered cold fog with a few warm fire-lit volumes; roughness variation so bronze and lacquer catch highlights against matte cloth and earth; wind and ember motion. This order follows the section 2 ranking.
- (Inference) Because the look is low-key with drained colour, banding in dark gradients is a specific risk (id flags the same class of artifact). Dithering the final image and fog is cheap insurance.
- (Inference) Three.js r187 context: the screen-space GI and newest AO nodes are built for the WebGPU renderer's node system. Using them means migrating renderer (its WebGL2 fallback exists but is a different code path from WebGLRenderer); on the classic WebGLRenderer the practical choices are GTAOPass, N8AO or the community realism-effects SSGI. The PCFSoftShadowMap deprecation note from r183 onward should be checked against the project's current shadow settings.
