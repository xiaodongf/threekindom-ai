Reading conventions used throughout: **[V]** = vendor or reseller claim (not independently tested), **[I]** = independent test or primary documentation/licence text, **[A]** = aggregator or secondary summary. Many "comparison" pages in this field are published by resellers (3D AI Studio, Scenario, fal, Cinevva, Wavect) or competitors (Neural4D, StraySpark) and are flagged where used. ## 1. AI 3D model generation as of November 2026 (Hunyuan3D, Tripo, Meshy, Rodin, TRELLIS/TRELLIS.2, Stability, Roblox Cube, Sloyd, newer entrants)

### Takeaway
Hosted generators (Meshy 7.1, Tripo P2.0, Rodin Gen-2.5, Tencent HY 3D 3.1) now return a textured PBR GLB in roughly 1–7 minutes for about $0.40–$0.80, with explicit low-poly/quad modes (Tripo P2.0: 500–50,000 tris or 500–25,000 quads) and one-call rigging, but almost every quality claim is vendor-sourced: no independent benchmark of topology, UVs, rigging or thin-structure handling was found. Open weights lag the hosted models (Hunyuan3D 2.1 from June 2025 with a territory-restricted licence; TRELLIS.2 from December 2025 under MIT but needing 24 GB VRAM), so for a one-developer team the practical route is a paid hosted plan, used for static props and buildings first.

**Failure modes**
- Documented-sources review (updated 11 Nov 2026, explicitly not a controlled benchmark): single-view input hides invented back and underside geometry; topology follows appearance, not deformation, so shoulders, mouths and cloth fail; textures mask mesh defects and baked lighting shows after relighting; style consistency breaks across large asset sets. [A] — [Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/ai-3d-model-generators-game-development-2026/)
- Tripo's own blog says thin structures are "inherently underrepresented" in training data, names non-manifold geometry as the most frequent failure, and advises checking whether "the sword hilt merged with the hand" and whether fingers fused. [V, self-admitted] — [Tripo thin structures](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/ai-3d-model-generator-and-thin-structures-survival-strategies); [Tripo failure modes](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/common-failure-modes-of-ai-3d-generators-and-fixes)
- A 2026 survey notes autoregressive mesh generators' outputs "typically lack UV maps, PBR material decomposition, and skeletal rigging" and that current benchmarks omit topology, UV, rigging and engine-import metrics. [I, research] — [arXiv 2604.23629](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23629v2)

### Inferences
- For this project the thin, iconic Han items (spears, halberds, banners, bow strings, reins) are exactly the generators' documented weak spot; keeping those as the existing procedural TypeScript geometry attached to generated bodies is the lower-risk split.
- At diorama/strategy distance the unresolved defects (faces, eyes, fingers, text) are below visible size, while silhouette, proportion and colour blocking dominate; at close-up (a city scene or duel) the same assets would show soft faces, fused hands and baked highlights. Generated assets are therefore better suited to buildings, props and mid-distance troops than to named generals in close-up.
- Style consistency is the main set-level risk: feeding every generation from the team's own AI-painted concept images (image-to-3D, multi-view where offered) and one fixed model version is the available control; model retirements (Meshy `lowpoly` 30 Oct 2026, `meshy-7` 31 Dec 2026) mean a set should be generated in one batch and the version recorded.
- Self-hosting is not attractive for one developer: Hunyuan3D 2.1 needs 29 GB for shape plus texture and has a territory-restricted licence; TRELLIS.2 is MIT but needs 24 GB and Linux, and exports a 1M-face mesh that still needs the whole reduction pipeline.
- Licence safety ranking for a commercial web game: paid Meshy/Tripo/Sloyd plans (output owned or commercially licensed) and TRELLIS.2 (MIT) are clear; Rodin Creator is ambiguous; HY 3D Global's "for reference only" footer and the Hunyuan3D 2.1 territory clause need checking before use.

### Gaps
- No independent, quantitative 2026 benchmark of mesh topology, UV quality, rig quality or thin-structure handling across these tools was found; rankings in circulation come from resellers or single bloggers.
- Open-weight part segmentation and auto-rigging models (for example Hunyuan3D-Part, Hunyuan3D-Omni, UniRig) were not checked because the search budget ran out.
- A dev.to article title refers to "Trellis 3", but the dated release log (updated 21 Nov 2026) lists no TRELLIS release after TRELLIS.2; unverified.
- Tripo's and Hyper3D's actual terms-of-service text on output ownership was not read; the Hyper3D free/Creator commercial position is contradictory across sources ([Hyper3D pricing](https://hyper3d.ai/pricing) vs a [Dupple review](https://dupple.com/reviews/rodin-ai) claiming commercial rights on the free tier).
- Whether the Hunyuan3D 2.1 territory exclusion restricts distributing outputs to players in the EU/UK/South Korea, or only running the model there, was not established.
- No vendor documents how well any generator handles Han-period Chinese armour, lamellar, or timber-frame architecture specifically.

## 2. Shipped games using AI-generated 3D assets or textures in 2025–2026, reception, and disclosure rules

### Takeaway
Steam still requires disclosure of AI-generated content that players see (3D models included) after its 16 January 2026 rewrite, and the pattern of 2025–2026 controversies is consistent: backlash follows undisclosed or leftover AI art (Crimson Desert, Clair Obscur) and even honest disclosure draws criticism (Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis). No primary-source case of a shipped game publicly crediting an AI 3D-model generator, with reception data, was found.

### Inferences
- A browser game outside Steam faces no platform disclosure rule today, but the reputational pattern is platform-independent: the damaging cases involved non-disclosure or "placeholder" excuses, not AI use that was stated up front. This project already ships AI-painted 2D art, so adding AI-generated 3D changes the degree, not the kind, of exposure; a plain public statement of what is AI-made is the consistent position.
- If the game is ever listed on Steam, AI-generated meshes and textures (and the existing portraits) must be declared in the content survey.
- Keeping provenance records (generator, model version, plan, prompts, input images) per asset, as Wavect recommends, is cheap insurance for both licence questions and disclosure.

## 3. AI material and texture generation (tileable PBR, map estimation, retexturing, depth, skybox/HDRI, upscalers)

### Takeaway
Tileable PBR material generation is now a scriptable commodity (fal's PATINA from $0.08 per full map set via API; Ubisoft's open-weights CHORD; Meshy Retexture at 10 credits), but all of them are weakest on metals, which matters for bronze and iron. For skies, CC0 HDRIs from Poly Haven remain the safe default; AI skybox HDR export with a commercial licence sits on Blockade Labs' $140/month tier.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A scripted material loop is feasible today without an artist: prompt → PATINA or CHORD → 1K–2K tileable maps → KTX2. - A generated skybox is optional: a CC0 Poly Haven HDRI gives correct lighting data for free, and a low-resolution HDR environment plus an LDR painted backdrop (which the team already produces with AI) avoids the HDR-compression problem.

## 6. Procedural detail techniques and the scripted Blender / glTF-Transform pipeline (including AI assistants driving Blender)

### Takeaway
The web delivery pipeline is mature and fully scriptable (glTF-Transform for weld/simplify/dedup/instance/palette plus Meshopt or Draco and KTX2), and texture memory, not triangles, is usually the binding constraint: a single 4096² texture costs about 90 MB of VRAM unless stored as KTX2 (4–8× less). AI assistants can now drive Blender through an official Blender Lab MCP server (v1.0.0, 27 April 2026) and get scripts to execute about 97% of the time with error feedback, but geometric accuracy does not improve with retries, so they are reliable for mechanical steps (import, decimate, bake, export) and unreliable as modellers.

### Cited Findings

**Compression and delivery**
- glTF-Transform CLI provides `inspect`, `optimize`, `dedup`, `prune`, `instance`, `join`, `flatten`, `weld`, `simplify`, `reorder`, `quantize`, `draco`, `meshopt`, `palette` (palette textures, merged materials), `resample`, `sparse`, `resize`, `webp`, `avif`, `etc1s`, `uastc`; `optimize` runs "all available methods" with options such as `--compress draco --texture-compress webp`, and the docs warn defaults may not suit every scene. [I] — [glTF-Transform CLI](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli)
- Meshopt compression is lossy when quantising, should be the last pipeline step, and needs gzip or brotli on top for full benefit. [I] — [glTF-Transform meshopt extension source](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@gltf-transform/extensions@4.3.0/src/ext-meshopt-compression/meshopt-compression.ts)
- Uncompressed texture VRAM is width × height × 4 × 1.333 bytes: a 4096² texture with mipmaps is about 90 MB regardless of file size; PNG, JPEG, WebP and AVIF all decompress to that; GPU formats via KTX2 cut memory 4–8× and upload 4–8× faster; ETC1S files are JPEG-sized but poorly suited to normal maps, UASTC is BC7-like quality at 1–2× JPEG size; KTX2 is preferred for games and long sessions, WebP/AVIF for fastest first paint. [I] — [Don McCurdy](https://www.donmccurdy.com/2024/02/11/web-texture-formats/)
- three.js loads KTX2 through `KTX2Loader` (requires `detectSupport(renderer)` and a transcoder path) with the `KHR_texture_basisu` glTF extension. [I] — [three.js KTX2Loader docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html)
- Sample numbers: DamagedHelmet 5.03 MB → 3.4 MB with Meshopt and about 3.29 MB with Draco; one user reports 1.6 MB → 290 KB with Meshopt plus KTX. [I, small samples] — [three.js compression sample](https://github.com/klich3/threejs-gltf-with-compressions-sample); [Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/about-the-gltf-transform-tool/49116)
- three.js forum guidance: oversized GLBs are "90% un-optimized textures"; keep images under 2048 px per side; share mesh data for repeated objects. [I, community] — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/considerations-for-loading-large-glb-files/65842)

**Baking for the web**
- three.js historically expects the AO map on the second UV set; `aoMap` and `lightMap` cannot use different UVs; `aoMap` is single-channel (red), `lightMap` RGB. [I, maintainer replies] — [three.js forum AO map](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/help-with-aomap-of-gltf-glb-model/14265); [three.js forum AO vs lightmaps](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/ambient-oclusion-vs-lightmaps/31333)
- Blender's glTF exporter picks up an occlusion texture from a custom node group named "glTF Settings" with an "Occlusion" input; glTF has no lightmap slot, so the common trick is to export the lightmap in the emissive slot and move it to `.lightMap` after loading. [I, community] — [three.js forum lightmap](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/will-light-map-that-store-as-ao-map-in-blender-cause-any-lighting-data-loss/25030)

**Authoring techniques**
- Trim sheets: one atlas of generic strips reused across a modular kit; Roblox's environment-art curriculum uses a six-row sheet for a whole kit and says to "avoid contextual details that you can only apply to a single object". [I] — [Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/develop-polished-assets); [Frozenbyte wiki](https://wiki.frozenbyte.com/index.php/3D_Asset_Workflow:_Tile_Textures_and_Trimsheets)
- Vertex-colour masks drive material blending on large meshes; rocks and terrain commonly combine "a few tri-planar mapped textures" with vertex painting; a tightly tiling detail map over low-resolution textures adds detail cheaply. [I, practitioner] — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2773864); [GameDev.net](https://www.gamedev.net/articles/programming/graphics/how-to-create-a-modular-environment-r2446/)

**AI assistants driving Blender**
- Official Blender MCP server (Blender Lab): v1.0.0 on 27 Apr 2026, v1.0.3 on 11 Sep 2026; requires Blender 5.1+; GPL-3.0-or-later; 26 tools including code execution, scene summaries, screenshots, renders and API-reference search; six tools have `_for_cli` variants that open a `.blend` in a background Blender process; it runs model-generated code with no guards, and the Lab recommends a VM or a machine without sensitive data. Anthropic's Blender connector uses it. [A, article by a competitor vendor citing primary pages] — [StraySpark comparison](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/official-blender-mcp-server-comparison-2026); [Claude Blender connector](https://claude.com/connectors/blender)
- The community server (Siddharth Ahuja's blender-mcp, renamed "MCP for Blender" on 16 Sep 2026): MIT, about 29,000 GitHub stars, Blender 3.0+, 36 tools, unauthenticated socket, four CVEs published in 2026 including a path traversal. [A] — [StraySpark comparison](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/official-blender-mcp-server-comparison-2026)
- 3DCodeBench (May 2026, 12 models writing Blender Python for procedural 3D): single-turn executability about 70% on aggregate, Claude Opus 4.7 0.910 and GPT-5.5 0.906; up to two retries with traceback raise executability to 0.974; wrapping models in CLI coding agents raises it from 0.747 to 0.973 but leaves shape quality "essentially unchanged"; most failures are Blender 4.x→5.0 API drift; successful renders often have disconnected or floating geometry, and "Physical Plausibility remains the primary bottleneck". [I, research] — [3DCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057v1)
- LL3M's authors state that straightforward LLM-written Blender scripts produce "low-quality outputs or fail to execute"; CodeGen-3D reports general LLMs failing 21–92% of the time versus 3–4% for specialised iterative systems. [I, research] — [LL3M](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08228v1); [CodeGen-3D](https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/faculty_rsca/6835)
- Practitioner report (Meshy GLB → BlenderMCP 1.27.1 → Godot): 16-step loop (import, orient, decimate at ratio 0.15–0.3, armature, weights, vision checks, bake action, export GLB); first model took 2–3 hours of prompt debugging, later ones about 10 minutes; the coding agent broke scenes on multi-step instructions, hit "poll() failed, context is incorrect" mode errors, leaked stale animations between exports, and needed a rule to switch strategy after three identical failures; vision models were unreliable at counting. [I, single practitioner] — [DEV pipeline write-up](https://dev.to/aleksandr_kossarev_e23623/ai-orchestrated-3d-asset-pipeline-from-jpeg-to-game-ready-glb-without-touching-blender-1akf)

### Inferences
- The existing procedural TypeScript geometry is an advantage, not a liability: it already has controlled topology, exact silhouettes for thin objects and trivially small payloads. The largest detail gain per unit of effort is likely material-side (tileable PBR sets, a shared trim sheet for roofs/beams/walls, vertex-colour dirt and wear, baked AO) applied to that geometry, with generated meshes reserved for organic or ornate items that are painful to code (horses, statues, bronze vessels, roof ornaments).
- A deterministic headless script (`blender -b --python`) written once with AI help and then run unattended is more reliable than live agent control: the benchmarks show execution errors are fixable with feedback, but agents do not make geometry more accurate by iterating. Suitable scripted steps: import GLB, normalise scale/origin/axis, decimate to a budget, auto-UV, bake high-to-low normal and AO, pack ORM, export GLB; then glTF-Transform for weld/dedup/instance/meshopt/KTX2.
- Pin the Blender version in that script: API drift between 4.x and 5.0 was the dominant cause of LLM script failure, and the official MCP server requires 5.1+.
- Texture budget arithmetic from the McCurdy formula: 1024² ≈ 5.6 MB and 2048² ≈ 22 MB of VRAM uncompressed with mips. A generated asset that arrives with separate 4K base colour, normal and ORM maps costs about 270 MB uncompressed; resizing to 1K and UASTC/ETC1S brings three maps to a few megabytes.
- Triplanar projection, detail normals and vertex-colour blending on the project's WebGLRenderer path would be implemented as material shader patches (or by moving to node materials); this is an engineering inference from general three.js knowledge, not from a source gathered here.
- Run any Blender MCP server only on a machine or VM without credentials, given the official warning and the 2026 CVEs on the community server.

### Gaps
- No source gave an end-to-end headless bpy bake-and-export script or reliability data for scripted (non-agent) pipelines.
- No 2026 measurements of Geometry Nodes or Houdini procedural output baked to glTF for the web were found; Houdini licensing and cost were not researched.
- The Blender Lab primary page returned HTTP 403; the official server's details come through a third-party article and Anthropic's connector listing.
- three.js r187-specific behaviour (UV channel handling for `aoMap`, KTX2/Meshopt loader status) was not verified against current docs; the forum threads cited are older.
- Texel-density guidance and texture-atlasing specifics were not sourced.

## 7. Realistic cost: time and money per asset from prompt to web-ready GLB

### Takeaway
Generation itself costs roughly $0.40–$0.80 and 1–7 minutes per textured model; the real cost is the acceptance rate and the reduction work, because raw outputs are far too heavy for the web (TRELLIS.2 exports 1M faces with a 4096 texture by default; "500K+ polygons for a simple object" is typical). No published before/after size and polygon data for AI-generated assets taken to compressed web GLB was found, so per-asset figures below are assembled from price lists and one practitioner's timings.

### Inferences
- Money is not the constraint. Derived from the cited Meshy prices ($0.02 per credit on Pro): textured model 30 credits + remesh 5 + rig 5 + three clips 9 = 49 credits ≈ $1.00 per rigged, animated attempt. A set of 60 assets at three attempts each is about $110–$180 on any of the hosted services, within one to three months of a $20–$30 plan.
- Time is the constraint, and it is front-loaded: on the one practitioner's evidence, expect a few hours building and debugging the scripted reduction pipeline, then on the order of 10–30 minutes of attended time per accepted static asset (generate variants, pick, run script, inspect in the game camera) and more for anything rigged. These are estimates extrapolated from a single report plus vendor generation times, not measured for this project.
- Requesting low-poly at source (Tripo P1/P2.0 at 2,000–10,000 faces, or Meshy remesh) is cheaper than decimating a 500K–2M-face mesh and re-baking, at the price of softer silhouettes; for diorama distance that trade is favourable. For hero close-ups the high-poly-then-bake route is the one that preserves detail in a normal map.
- A plausible web target per asset, reasoned from the CC0 reference weights and the texture arithmetic: strategy-map props and soldiers 500–3,000 triangles with one shared 1K atlas (tens to a few hundred KB after Meshopt and KTX2); city close-up buildings 5,000–20,000 triangles with tiling materials and a trim sheet rather than unique textures; a single hero model 20,000–50,000 triangles with 2K maps (1–3 MB). These are design targets inferred here, not published figures.
- The cheapest first experiment that would answer the open quality question is the four-asset pilot Wavect describes, judged in the game's own camera at both distances: one Han building prop, one bronze vessel or weapon rack, one horse, one foot soldier.

### Gaps
- No published before/after (polygon count and file size) for an AI-generated asset taken through decimation, baking and Meshopt/KTX2 to a web GLB was found; the one pipeline write-up reports ratios and times only.
- No measured acceptance rates (usable outputs ÷ generations) by asset type exist in public sources.
- No independent time study of cleanup effort for AI assets; "hours of retopology" versus "twenty minutes" are anecdotes or hypotheticals.
- Tripo per-model credit costs were not confirmed from Tripo's own pricing page (the developer page lists 30–60 units per task without stating the unit).
