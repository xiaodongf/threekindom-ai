# Relight first, then replace the pegs

The 2024 to November 2026 record is consistent: a game reads as finely detailed when every object, still or moving, sits in the same indirect light, meets the ground with a contact shadow, recedes into layered air and moves with one wind, and it spends surface micro-detail only where the camera holds a close-up. The leaders (DOOM: The Dark Ages, Assassin's Creed Shadows, Ghost of Yōtei, GTA VI) buy that with real-time global illumination and cut reflections first; the admired modest-budget games (Kingdom Come: Deliverance II, Arc Raiders, Hades II) get the same reading from baked or painted light, hand-made surface variation and a clean, stable image, and a zero-asset three.js game on r186 shows that chain running on this game's own renderer. Measured against that, this game's five scenes hold at their wide framings and break at their nearest ones for four reasons the audit documents: only the duel has environment lighting, people are box-and-ball pegs or cloned toy soldiers, every surface is a flat colour or one small tile, and most of each scene never moves. The plan put forward for review has six phases ordered by visible payoff against effort: repair the three audited bugs and build a capture-and-timing harness; give the four 3D scenes one lighting and image foundation; replace the pegs with one instanced, shader-animated figure kit; add one wind, flowing water and fire that behaves; rebuild surfaces and architecture on Eastern Han evidence; and only then, if approved, pilot generated or library assets. Six decisions belong to the developer before work starts: whether to admit AI-generated or library 3D assets given their licence, disclosure and quality limits; how far to follow material culture of about 200 AD against popular imagery such as stirrups and the guandao; whether to spend money (a pilot costs tens of dollars, and time is the real cost); which hardware must run well; whether "classic" stays a first-class look; and whether the duel and tactical stages may grow. The evidence is strongest on what to prioritise and on the defects in the game, and weakest on cost: several researchers exhausted their search budgets, no technique here was measured on integrated graphics, and almost every three.js recipe below is an inference never run on this codebase, which is why Phase 0 builds the measuring stick before anything is added.

## The industry pays for light, contact, air and motion, and rations surface detail

### Leaders fund indirect light on everything and cut reflections first

The best-documented leader is DOOM: The Dark Ages. id Software's requirement list opens with "Performance > all else", its real-time global illumination was designed down to **about 2 ms on every console**, and its own comparison of high against low quality reads "Relatively close, bit softer result" with the final gather running at half or quarter resolution ([id Software, SIGGRAPH 2025](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)). Digital Foundry ranked it first for 2025 because it delivered that lighting "at an incredibly performant level", a judgement reachable here only through secondary reports ([Pure Xbox](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2025/12/xboxs-doom-the-dark-ages-wins-digital-foundrys-graphics-of-the-year-award)). The same deck shows where fine detail really comes from: a screen-space directional-occlusion pass added "for high frequency details", giving "contact shadows for every light", because coarse bounce light alone misses them ([id Software](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)).

Reflections are what leaders drop. id switched ray-traced reflections off on consoles to save about 2 ms ([id Software](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)); Sucker Punch spent Ghost of Yōtei's ray tracing on global illumination because its setting has few mirror-like surfaces, and kept a baked lighting model with only short-range ray tracing on top ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)). A Han-period world of earth, timber, cloth, lacquer and bronze is in Yōtei's position. Ubisoft's Anvil team names the depth cues it relies on at a showpiece castle: sunset light, atmospheric scattering and layered cloud, fog and water ([Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6u0kflsdY20YlnZks7rUeG/inside-anvil-the-technology-powering-assassins-creed-shadows)). GTA VI, released on 19 November 2026, reportedly ships ray-traced global illumination on every console at 30 fps; that feature list rests on a fan-site summary of Digital Foundry's review and should be treated as provisional ([gta-vi.io](https://gta-vi.io/news/gta6-digital-foundry-30fps-all-consoles)). The 2026 rendering talks (variable-rate ray tracing, a radiance cache with no frame history, a console upscaler redesigned for stability) are about making the same lighting cheaper and cleaner, not about new kinds of detail ([SIGGRAPH 2026 course](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/index.html)), and almost none of it transfers to a WebGL 2 renderer. What transfers is the structure of the cheap tiers the leaders still ship as fallbacks: baked or probe-based ambient light, screen-space occlusion, soft effects at reduced resolution, environment maps in place of traced reflections, and analytic fog.

### One controlled study and many settings tests agree on what goes unseen

Only one controlled ranking surfaced. In a 2023 virtual-reality study where participants spent a limited budget on rendering features to match a real scene, **global illumination ranked first and direct shadows a close second, with ambient occlusion and screen-space reflections last**; occlusion was noticed only when bounce light was also on ([Computers & Graphics](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cag.2023.10.021)). An offline-rendering preference study found no significant effect of global illumination, so the result does not generalise cleanly ([DiVA](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1388856/FULLTEXT02.pdf)). Analyst practice points the same way as the study. The two most expensive settings in Battlefield 6, top-tier sun shadows and screen-space occlusion, cost a lot "with minimal visual returns" ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/22194/these-two-battlefield-6-settings-are-killing-your-performance)); Kingdom Come: Deliverance II's Ultra settings "are not really worth much" ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/kingdom-come-deliverance-ii-polished-impeccable-smoothness/)); and in a Forza Horizon 6 night scene the gap between ray-traced and screen-space "High" is "negligible" ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/3127-forza-horizon-6-optimization/)). The priority order this report uses is a synthesis of that evidence, not a published ranking: coherent ambient light on every object; clean direct shadows including contact shadows; atmosphere as a depth cue; variation in how materials answer light; ambient motion; and sharp reflections last.

### Modest-budget successes bake, paint and refuse

The games praised for looking rich without frontier technology put their detail in authored light and hand-made surfaces and refuse expensive features. PC Gamer's verdict on Kingdom Come: Deliverance II is that "you don't need the latest graphics technology to have a great-looking game that also runs well" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/hardware/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-is-proof-if-any-was-ever-needed-that-you-dont-need-the-latest-graphics-technology-to-have-a-great-looking-game-that-also-runs-well)). Arc Raiders left out Unreal's dynamic lighting, virtual geometry and virtual shadow maps and was held up as a model of stability ([Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/arc-raiders-unreal-engine-5-optimization/)); Battlefield 6 decided "relatively early on" to skip ray tracing ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hardware/we-wanted-to-focus-on-performance-battlefield-6-developer-explains-why-ea-is-skipping-ray-tracing-at-launch)). Hades II paints shadows and highlights straight into its textures, typically a few values per material ([Hyperlific](https://www.hyperlific.com/articles/jen-zees-2d-defiance-and-the-hand-that-paints-hades-ii/)). One critic found Black Myth: Wukong's hand-tuned lighting good enough that its path-traced mode "can sometimes look a bit disappointing" beside it ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=182365)). DOOM Eternal's baked pipeline shows how coarse good ambient light can be: lightmaps at **2×2 texels per square metre** and probes looked up per vertex ([id Software](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)).

The closest relative of this project is a November 2026 three.js game on r186, Tides of Fortune, in which ships, towns, characters and textures "are all generated in code at load time". It runs a conventional chain (HDR buffer, sun shafts, bloom, ACES, a grade with grain), uses FXAA by default and reserves SMAA with ambient occlusion for its "Ultra" tier, and publishes no frame figures ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/tides-of-fortune-an-open-world-pirate-game-in-three-js-with-zero-asset-files/96867)). The three.js manual still calls WebGPURenderer "experimental" and WebGLRenderer "the recommended choice for pure WebGL 2 applications" ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/webgpurenderer.html)), and between r182 and r187 the classic renderer gained soft shadows by default ([PR #32303](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/32303)), a baked light-probe grid ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LightProbeGridWebGL.html)) and a sky with procedural clouds ([r183 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r183)). The foundation this plan needs already exists on the renderer the game uses.

### At overview scale the showpiece details are process and life

Strategy and city-building developers show off process, not polygons. Anno 117 quadrupled the resolution of its terrain material maps and generates them from simulated erosion and deposition ([Anno Union](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-island-creation-in-anno-117-pax-romana/)); its citizens and animals are deliberately capped in detail "since there are so many", and a wolf gets by on three animation cycles ([Anno dev blog, mirrored](https://devtrackers.gg/anno-1800/p/721951f5-devblog-animating-animals-models-rigs-and-animations)). A German technical review singled out its night lighting, where every torch and lit window is a light ([PCGH](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Anno-117-Pax-Romana-Spiel-74719/Specials/Raytracing-PBR-Technik-Check-Benchark-Kandidat-1480212/)). The Anno series shows city state on the buildings themselves: working and idle states differ in smoke, fire and worker activity, and small scripted scenes run on randomised timing. The detailed account is a 2018 blog for Anno 1800, the system Anno 117 inherits ([Anno Union](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-visual-feedback/)). The caution is Frostpunk 2, whose one vignette per district left a reviewer feeling it "never feels like you're peering into a living, breathing place", a quotation seen only in a search summary ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/frostpunk-2-review)).

Readability governs how much detail survives. Firaxis lets the camera "come right up to the front door" in Civilization VII yet uses "less rows of bricks" when zoomed out ([Hooked Gamers](https://hookedgamers.com/features/2024/10/23/the_art_of_civilization_vii_-_an_interview.html)); Stormgate changed its lighting angle specifically "to make the terrain more readable and pop" ([Stormgate](https://playstormgate.com/news/content-update-0-1-0)); Unfrozen concluded for Heroes of Might & Magic: Olden Era that readability outranks cohesion and that gritty photorealism hurt both readability and performance in earlier 3D strategy games ([Heroes 3.5 portal](https://heroes3wog.net/homm-olden-era-from-prototype-to-production/)). No studio published a sun angle, shadow length or triangle budget per zoom band.

### Figures are carried by silhouette, motion and state; crowds by instancing

Flagship character work goes into things that move or change state. Sucker Punch drives clothing, weapon tassels and hanging cloth from one wind, and marks characters wet, bloody, muddy or snowy from one interaction grid ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)). A researcher's screen-size arithmetic (an inference, not a developer rule) explains why: at 30 m on a 1080-pixel frame a figure stands about 78 pixels tall, so a 5 cm armour plate covers about two pixels while a plume or a cape edge covers 13 to 45. Dynasty Warriors: Origins reached "several thousand" soldiers on screen and its producer admits the pulled-back camera makes players ask "Where am I?" ([Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202407/10868)).

The crowd method with numbers behind it is animation baked to textures and drawn as GPU instances. In an Unreal test of 6,072 agents, skeletal meshes ran at 28 fps with 21,369 draw calls and a 34 ms game thread, while **vertex-animation textures on instanced meshes ran at 57 fps with 276 draw calls and 0.66 ms**; a per-instance random value offsets animation time and shifts hue so agents do not look cloned ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/6-000-agents-on-one-game-thread-novel-high-performance-framework-for-gpu-driven-crowd-systems/)). A three.js showcase draws its whole crowd from one merged geometry with a per-vertex part number, generating eyes, stripes and buttons in the shader ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/one-draw-call-massive-crowd-performance-engineering-in-three-js/89928)). Individually skinned characters hit a processor wall at about 200 units on an older laptop ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/optimization-of-large-amounts-100-1000-of-skinned-meshes-cpu-bottlenecks/58196)). No 2024–2026 game was found that builds convincing realistic humans or horses from primitives; the benchmark horse rests on roughly 6,300 authored motions ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rockstar-s-quest-for-the-ultimate-video-game-horse)). That absence is weak evidence, because the relevant searches were cut short, but it sets an honest ceiling for code-built heroes: convincing at middle and far distance, stylised but coherent at five metres.

### A dark look is punished for mud and glare, not for darkness

Sucker Punch's art director describes the working rule for a moody world: "we're constantly simplifying, stripping away anything that doesn't matter", with "clear silhouettes that read instantly" and one saturated accent glowing through mist ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/video-games-ghost-of-yotei/)); the studio refused blanket haze and colour filters because they dull local colour ([Atmos](https://atmos.earth/art-and-culture/this-years-most-anticipated-video-game-is-an-homage-and-elegy-to-nature/)). Critics punish the failures: the Nioh series for "dark and muddy lighting contrasted with retina-burning combat effects" ([COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/review/nioh-3-review/)), Wuchang for two visual styles with nothing tying them together ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/wuchang-fallen-feathers-review)), Where Winds Meet for a showpiece city whose density brought severe performance problems ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/where-winds-meet-is-a-graphics-masterpiece-but-kaifeng-has-severe-performance-issues)), and Phantom Blade Zero for boss effects so heavy "you can't even see what's going on" ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/phantom-blade-zero-review/)). Effects artists reduce the cure to a hierarchy: one bright, sharp primary element, quiet secondary ones, pure white reserved for the instant of impact ([VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)). The Han craftsmen's own pottery models offer a period guide to density: detail on roof edges, brackets, balconies and lattice, with wall planes left plain ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_of_the_Han_dynasty)).

## Five scenes hold at a distance and break at their nearest framing

The comparison rests on the audit in [`current_game_graphics_audit.md`](../research_notes/Strategy%20game%20graphic%20detail%20study/current_game_graphics_audit.md), which read the source, took 41 captures of the running build on 26 November 2026 and counted draw calls on one workstation GPU (an NVIDIA RTX 5000 Ada). Thirty-three of those captures were opened for this report; the table links the 21 studied at full frame. The audit's central facts are these: **everything in every 3D scene is built at run time from primitives, lofts and extrusions with standard materials**; no material uses a normal, roughness, metalness or occlusion map; there are no loaded models and no skeletal animation; the only image files that reach WebGL are four painted battle backdrops and one height map; and the four 3D scenes each create their own renderer with different anti-aliasing, tone mapping, pixel-ratio cap, shadow, fog and post-processing settings. Only the duel has an environment map. The earlier study's art direction is in place (the paper far zoom, territory as a shader texture with ink-soak conquest, tiered cities, the general-with-banner army, the full-screen war scene, the ring of soldiers). Three of its rendering recommendations were not carried out: a shared renderer with quality tiers and shader warm-up on every transition, a tone mapper other than ACES, and bloom at half resolution.

Four readings follow from the table. First, the wide shots succeed for one reason: a painted or shader-drawn layer carries the detail and the 3D supplies only silhouette and light, and close framings remove that support. Second, the triangle budget is spent where the eye is not. In the city the ground and trees outweigh the built town roughly seven to one and soldier heads (9,040 triangles) outweigh the walls (4,660); in the duel the spectators outweigh the two heroes roughly three to one. Third, classic exposes the primitive geometry more than myth does, because myth hides part of it in shadow, fog and bloom, so an upgrade judged only under the default look will understate the gap. Fourth, the industry's first two priorities are exactly what three scenes lack: the map, city and war theater have no environment map, so their metals are lit by direct lights alone, and the map has no shadows at all.

The audit separates three defects as bugs, not missing art, and the opened captures confirm each. The map's seasonal particles are untextured points that render as pale hard-edged squares (`WebGLStrategyMap.tsx:535`). The marching general's armour and trim render near-black under myth while his cloak and horse keep their colour, which the audit attributes to metal materials in a scene with no environment map. The duel's brazier flames are solid emissive cones that bloom to flat white (`duel/duelArena3D.ts:443-444`).

On this workstation every scene is trivially cheap: median frame intervals with the limiter off run from 0.3 ms on the map to 4.4 ms in the war theater. That says the scenes are not processor-bound. It says little about fill rate on weak hardware, and the audit could not confirm that those figures include GPU time. Nothing in the repository records performance on an integrated GPU.

## The plan for review: six phases and 33 work items, ranked by payoff against effort

### How to read the tables

The plan respects the project's limits: one developer with an AI coding assistant and no artists, three.js r187 on WebGLRenderer, all 3D built in code through Phase 4, myth as the default look with classic as the alternative, and the art direction already implemented taken as given. Phases are ordered by visible payoff against effort, and items inside a phase by dependency. Payoff and effort are this report's judgement, not measurements: low effort means up to about a day for one developer with an AI coding assistant, medium a few days, high a week or more. Evidence is graded twice, because the two questions have very different answers. "Payoff" asks whether viewers will notice: strong where a developer statement, official documentation, a measurement or the audit's own code and captures were read in full; moderate where one full source, secondary write-ups or agreeing reviewers support it; weak where it rests on search summaries, vendor claims or no precedent. "Recipe" asks whether the technique is known to work in three.js r187 on WebGLRenderer: documented where an official example or library was read, and untested where the recipe is an inference that has never run on this code. Most recipes are untested.

Every item is checked the same way, which Phase 0 makes possible. Before-and-after captures come from fixed camera presets named after the audit's captures, at 1600×900 and pixel ratio 1, in both myth and classic. Frame cost is recorded as draw calls and triangles from a WebGL call counter, the median and 90th-percentile frame interval with the frame limiter off, GPU time per pass where the timer-query extension exists, and scene build time. The same run is repeated on the weakest machine the developer names. Stills cannot show shimmer or motion, so items that animate are checked with a short fixed-timestep frame sequence. Until that machine is measured, the working ceilings are the researchers' synthesis, not published budgets: 100–300 draw calls, 200,000–500,000 triangles including shadow passes, at most three full-resolution passes after the scene render, and pixel ratio 1 on integrated graphics. The duel and war theater already sit at or above the draw-call ceiling, which is one reason Phase 2 matters beyond its looks.

All paths are under `src/components/` unless they begin with `src/` or `scripts/`. Line numbers are the audit's. The city scene files (`city/cityScene.ts`, `city/cityLayout.ts`, `CityView.tsx`) and `src/App.tsx` had uncommitted edits in progress from other work while the audit ran: `city/cityScene.ts` matches the last commit only up to line 1040, and later code had shifted by 85 to 299 lines. City items should therefore start after that work is committed, locate code by function name, and re-shoot their "before" captures, since new props (a steamship, waterwheels, a balloon, a pennant) appeared mid-audit.

A sensible approval path is to approve Phases 0 and 1 now, review Phase 1's captures before releasing Phases 2 to 4, and decide on Phase 5 only after item 4.6 shows how far code-built heroes can go.

### Phase 1: one lighting and image foundation for the four 3D scenes

## What to avoid, and where the evidence is thin

### Traps the evidence already marks

Do not move to WebGPURenderer or rewrite materials in its node system for this work. The manual still calls it experimental, custom shaders and the existing post chain would all need porting, and a high-priority overhead issue in its WebGL fallback was still open in September 2026 ([three.js issue #30560](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30560)). The new built-in effects path is "experimental" in its author's words ([PR #32461](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/32461)). Do not upgrade to r188 mid-plan. Do not chase the frontier's expensive tiers: screen-space global illumination has no maintained WebGL implementation, contact-hardening shadows cost 51 depth reads per shadowed pixel in the official example ([example source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/examples/webgl_shadowmap_pcss.html)), the documented light-shaft library is pinned below r188 and needs a different composer ([three-good-godrays](https://github.com/Ameobea/three-good-godrays)), parallax occlusion mapping has no WebGL example at all, and Gaussian-splat backdrops bake in photographic light that will not match this grade. Reflections beyond an environment sheen buy little in a matte world. Any "ultra" tier that costs much and shows little repeats a mistake analysts now call out by name.

Do not add detail the camera cannot resolve or the player cannot read through: lamellar plates on distant soldiers, brick rows at overview zoom, uniform density across a whole city. Do not darken to hide: the look is punished for mud against glare, so effects brightness must follow scene brightness, and a blanket tinted haze is the wrong fog. Do not let automatic sharpening or contrast override the authored palette; the hostile reception of DLSS 5's neural relighting shows how audiences treat that ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neural-rendering-nvidia-dlss-5)). Do not judge any item under myth alone. Do not add per-pixel cost before the tier system exists, and do not add sprites or decals that each carry their own material.

On assets, do not put generated models where a named hero is held in close-up, do not ship free-tier generator output, do not rely on museum scans whose reuse terms were not verified, and do not mix photographic scans with the code-built world. Black Myth's scanned temples are, on the researcher's dating, Liao to Ming, so copying its look would import post-Han brackets and roof curves. Do not use an AI assistant as a live 3D modeller. A May 2026 benchmark found models get Blender scripts to execute about 97 % of the time with error feedback while shape quality stays "essentially unchanged" ([3DCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057v1)), which makes assistants reliable for scripted reduction and unreliable for sculpting. For crowds, do not give each soldier its own skeleton and animation mixer.

### How far the evidence reaches

Four limits should shape how the plan is read. First, coverage is uneven because the researchers' shared search budget ran out. The strategy researcher found nothing from developers on several listed titles and no conference talk on any of them; the frontier researcher could not open Digital Foundry's own pages, so its rankings and the whole GTA VI account are secondary; the character researcher never ran the queries on expert horse reviews, lit smoke or finisher cameras; the three.js researcher worked from already-known pages after five searches. Second, there is no cost data where it matters. Every published millisecond figure comes from Apple M1 or M5 Max chips or RTX cards, the audit used one workstation, and the audit's own uncapped timings may omit GPU time. The only weak-hardware figure in the repository is an unexplained "36 fps" remark about the duel in the earlier report.

Third, the perceptual ranking is thin: one 2023 virtual-reality study, contradicted in part by an offline study, plus developer and analyst practice. The ordered list used here is a synthesis. Fourth, the recipes are inferences. The researchers verified no three.js implementation of per-vertex baked occlusion, height fog on WebGLRenderer, detail-normal or edge-wear injection, a flow-mapped river in a terrain shader, a ground-space track texture or a texture-animated crowd, and none has run on this code. Payoff and effort ratings, phase order and the proposed defaults are this report's judgement.

Smaller gaps matter for specific items. The Europa Universalis V diary and several Manor Lords and Frostpunk 2 statements were seen only as search summaries. The historical notes lean on encyclopaedia pages and one hobbyist blog; the roof profile of about 200 AD, soldiers' robe colours, crenellations and Three Kingdoms-specific standards are unsourced. For AI 3D tools the quality claims are vendor claims, prices conflict between sources, no shipped game was found that names a generator and reports its reception, and no acceptance rates exist. The audit's stills cannot show shimmer or animation quality, classic was captured for only some views, and several battle modes were never captured.

## Conclusion

The game's best frames already work by a division of labour: a painted or shader-drawn layer carries the texture, and the 3D supplies silhouette, light and motion. The upgrade should extend that arrangement to the close framings instead of chasing geometric fidelity there. That is why the first two phases add almost no geometry, why the figure kit is designed for silhouette and movement before surface, and why the one place the plan concedes a ceiling (the hero at five metres) is turned into an explicit test whose result gates any spending on assets. The dark-myth default is both ally and trap in this: it hides missing micro-detail, but it also hides ambient occlusion and punishes mud, so classic becomes the honest test bench.

Two things in the plan are worth more than any single effect. The harness turns "does it look better and what did it cost" from an opinion into a pair of captures and a number, and the first frame timed on an integrated GPU will reorder the later phases more than further research would. And period accuracy turns out to be open ground: no recent Three Kingdoms game appears to compete on it, and in a pipeline where every wall, roof and helmet is drawn by code, choosing rammed earth over brick or a ring-pommel sabre over a later blade costs no more than drawing the wrong one.
